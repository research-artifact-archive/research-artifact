# Candidate scope counterexample: fresh completion after observed conflicts

Exploratory hand derivation, not yet run through a checker. Original body-only whole-kernel model, deterministic universal Q<=n+r and simultaneously optimal protected-work curve. No guard/comparison charges are added here.

Take jobs of works (3,1,1,2), edges0->1->2, with job3 independent, r=3. Total work S=7 and global top sums are(0,3,5,6,7). The independent-job theorem gives its least curve(7,10,13,16,19,19,19,19) for B0..7, but this DAG appears to require(7,10,13,16,19,20,20,20).

The usual minimum-ready-work order is(3,0,1,2). Cheap until3 failures and then cached for every suffix job has the curve(7,10,13,16,19,20,21,21): three failures at the3-unit job, its cached mismatch, and both later unit-job mismatches give21. This shows why the simple fixed-order work envelope need not be the optimal one on DAGs.

Modify only one reachable suffix: if the independent2-unit job finished cheaply, the3-unit job and the first dependent unit job both mismatched under cached completion, and only the final unit job remains, complete that last job fresh. At that point there have been at least r+2=5 actual writes; protected work rises from4 to5, within globalTop2=5, and remains admissible at every larger budget. If the switch to cached occurred earlier at the independent2-unit job, do not apply this rule. Thus the modified policy retains the optimal protected-work curve at every budget and Q<=7.

Its worst duplicate work is13. When all3 cheap failures occur before the independent2-unit job completes, their cost is6 and the whole remaining mismatched body sum is at most7. When that job completes cheaply before switching, cheap-failure cost is at most9, and duplicated cached work is at most3+1=4 before the fresh suffix rule prevents another duplication. Earlier cached matches only reduce these sums. The modified curve is therefore at most(7,10,13,16,19,20,20,20).

For the candidate20 lower bound at B5, let all calls on the independent2-unit job match. Its computation/readiness can only add mandatory work. Force3 useful cheap failures at the source3-unit job. A fresh completion at fewer than r+1 writes would violate zero protected work at those budgets; a premature cached call could be invalidated with the same violation. Universal completion forces eventual progress. After the3 failures, cheap cannot be selected again under the cap. Force that job's useful cached mismatch: protected work is3 at actual budget4, saturating globalTop1. Until the first dependent unit job completes, no fresh completion of it is permitted on the no-more-write continuation. Its first matching cached completion can therefore be invalidated by a fifth write, forcing one further duplicate unit. The global charge is S+3*3+3+1=20. Free inspections or retained preparations do not remove these distinct identity charges. This lower-bound adversary must still be written with explicit unconditional counter caps before being treated as a proof.

This candidate does not prove general positive-retry DAG hardness or a general least-curve algorithm. It potentially refutes the tempting extension 'minimum-ready-work then all-cached is always least-total-work on DAGs', while retaining the original independent-job theorem. The finite formula for a fixed all-cached suffix order is S+max_j(r*w_pi[j]+Top_(B-r)(suffix_j)) for B>r; it must not be asserted as the universal optimum without an all-program lower bound.
