# Linux dentry-path investigation: bounded work and a real fallback

Exploratory author research, sole writer root. Started 2026-09-10 23:46 JST under the existing continuation authorization; global stop 2026-09-11 10:00 JST, preparation 09:50. Preserve all outcomes and source/implementation corrections. This is not a fixed final performance study.

Question: can replacing the existing post-failure fresh locked path traversal by another optimistic traversal with validation/recompute inside the exclusive reader lock preserve the source's results and its at-most-two validation/completion calls, while moving path construction out of rename_lock when there is no second invalidation? Compare the actual upstream policy, all-locked path construction, and a stronger ancestor-name-snapshot/materialization strategy. No padding or invented heavy transform; use actual bounded path construction.

Pinned source: Linux v6.12 adc218676eef25575469234709c2d87185ca223a, initially fs/d_path.c's __dentry_path/dentry_path_raw only. The getcwd/mount-path whole syscall is excluded because mount traversal and its other seqcount loop require separate bounds. The historical upstream contention rationale is evidence of the problem, not evidence of this proposed policy's performance. The existing build173 is an unpublished working draft; no native-fit/importance claim is added now.

A possible single-job body-only prediction, not yet source-verified: with work per traversal at most u(C), cached fallback has at most two validation/completion calls, at most three traversals, and W bounds u(C), 2u(C), 3u(C) for B=0, B=1, B>=2; protected traversal work is zero for B<=1 and at most u(C) otherwise. If the stronger alternative dominates, preserve that result and change the research direction.
