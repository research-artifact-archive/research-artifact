\title[Staged Updates between Verified Controllers]{Synthesizing Staged Updates between Verified Controllers}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Verifying an old controller and its replacement does not establish how to pass safely between them. Components may change at different times, while requirements overlap or begin with pending obligations. Fine-Grained Dynamic Update Controller Synthesis (FG-DUCS) constructs this transition from local transfers, requirement lifetimes and initial obligations. A winning policy completes safely from every reachable old state under every environment outcome and preserves the selected new controller's continuation. We prove correspondence between the contract and its game, and a sufficient condition for serializing merged transfers. Established lazy exploration returns a ranked policy or a losing-region certificate. On \ApplicationContracts{} adapted games, it decides \LazyDecisions{} within 1,200~s, versus 17 with eager exploration under the same pruning. Constructed examples separate granularities in both directions; 54 inherited-model comparisons show no decision change. The guarantee concerns finite event sequences in the model: commands and handover require uncontrollable quiescence, and continuous service or elapsed-time bounds are not promised.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Verified old and new controllers leave an important question open: how should control pass between them? During the transition, components may use different versions and requirements may have different lifetimes~\cite{Cheng2009SASRoadmap,Andersson2013SEAMSProcesses,Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate,Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates}. In a workcell, an occupied station may need to pass its workpiece to another station before replacement. A new inspection rule may then begin with an inspection already owed. Safe endpoints do not settle these intermediate choices.

\emph{FG-DUCS generates a policy for this handover from local inputs.} The modeler supplies the old and new controllers, permitted component transfers, requirement lifetimes and initial obligations. Synthesis chooses how to coordinate them from every reachable old state and for every environment outcome. At handover, it restores the selected new-controller state's permitted continuation. This is stronger than merely satisfying its safety requirements: deleting a permitted action can preserve safety while changing the supplied controller (Section~\ref{sec:formalization}).

The intended use is checking a finite maintenance plan before execution. Update control takes over scheduling and enforces the active requirements; an interval requirement can protect a gap between old and new rules. Work may pause. Commands and handover occur only when the active-component product has no enabled uncontrollable (UC) event. Persistent environment activity can therefore prevent completion. The guarantee counts events, not elapsed time.

Dynamic Update Controller Synthesis (DUCS)~\cite{Nahabedian2016SEAMS,Nahabedian2020} and generalized-reactivity(1), or GR(1), updating~\cite{Amram2022GR1DynamicUpdate} already generate update control. FG-DUCS fixes both endpoint controllers and constructs the intervening game from individual transfers and requirement boundaries. Section~\ref{sec:ducs-cell-construction} makes this input/output distinction concrete. Our contribution is the contract, its guarantees and granularity conditions; the reachability algorithm is established.

\Needspace{3\baselineskip}
Our contributions are:
\par\noindent\textbf{C1: Contract and game.} We prove local-history correspondence and endpoint preservation, and give an invariant-initialization criterion (Sections~\ref{sec:formalization} and~\ref{sec:monitor-meaning}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C2: Granularity conditions.} We identify obstructions to merged commands and a local sufficient condition for serializing merged transfers (Sections~\ref{sec:example} and~\ref{sec:granularity-analysis}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C3: A policy with checkable evidence.} Sound and complete lazy synthesis returns a completing policy and rank or a losing-region certificate (Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C4: Implementation evidence.} Reference-game checks and \ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts test the MTSA implementation; constructed comparisons separately test granularity (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}).

Technical Appendix A--P and Supplement S1--S5 are in our public replication package~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact}; Data Availability gives versioned links.

\subsection{Cell: Moving Work before Replacing a Station}
Our constructed \emph{Cell} example has two stations $A,B$ and exactly one workpiece~\cite{Lewerentz1995ProductionCell,Nahabedian2020}. A station can be replaced only while empty. The old controller moves the workpiece between stations and forbids inspection. The new controller requires inspection at $B$ before departure. Both endpoints are verified, yet simultaneous replacement is impossible: one station always holds the workpiece.

\input{figures/cell_story_visual.tex}

\paragraph{Coordinating moves and changing duties.}
Figure~\ref{fig:cell-story} shows the policy from both old states, called \emph{entries}. Each path replaces the empty station, moves the workpiece, and later replaces the other station. The input requires new-start before old-stop, preventing an unprotected gap; synthesis chooses the remaining order. If $B$ holds at new-start, inspection becomes pending; it must wait until old-stop removes the ban. Starting its monitor---the automaton tracking the new rule---clear would silently discard this duty.

The first path therefore inspects before handover; the second activates the new rule with $B$ empty and needs no inspection. Both reach an allowed new closed-loop state with inspection clear: $z_A$ holds the workpiece at $A$, and $z_B$ holds it at $B$. A nonnegative \emph{rank} decreases on every arrow, excluding safe but endless inspection. Handover restores the selected endpoint's full permitted behavior.

\paragraph{What synthesis chooses.}
The supplied ordering is partial, so synthesis still chooses the update sequence. All Cell events are controllable: $m_A,m_B$ move to $A,B$, $j$ inspects, and $\rho_A,\rho_B$ replace the empty station. Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:cell}~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact} gives the complete input and path proof. The next example shows why requirements also need separate boundaries.

\subsection{Audit and Role Lifetimes}
We call the second constructed example \emph{Policy(2)}: the number denotes its two old/new requirement pairs, audit and access role. At least one audit rule must remain active throughout, while the old and new roles must never overlap. These obligations require opposing boundary orders:
\[
\begin{array}{r@{\quad}c@{\;}c@{\;}c@{\;}c@{\;}c}
\text{Audit:}&\text{old only}&\xrightarrow{\text{new-start}}&\text{both active}&\xrightarrow{\text{old-stop}}&\text{new only}\\[2pt]
\text{Role:}&\text{old only}&\xrightarrow{\text{old-stop}}&\text{neither active}&\xrightarrow{\text{new-start}}&\text{new only}
\end{array}
\]
The role gap is declared by the interval requirement, rather than forced by access rules alone. The new audit monitor starts unlogged and requires a log before service. Each endpoint alternates its own log and service, permitting only its own access role. With an identity transfer, the saved policy obeys these opposing orders and reaches a compatible new endpoint from both old states in at most six events.

If the starts are merged and the stops are merged, the first start overlaps roles and the first stop removes audit coverage. Ordinary events and transfer do not change which rules are active, so neither old entry has a completing merged policy. Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:policy_interpretation}~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact} gives the complete finite input, policy and losing closure. This compares merged and separate FG-DUCS requirement boundaries.

\subsection{Execution and Handover}

To use a model policy, an implementation must preserve what the policy observes and controls. The execution connection has four premises (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:execution_proof}~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact}):
\par\noindent\textbf{A1: Observations.} Endpoint observations preserve component and monitor states and same-labeled endpoint edges.
\par\noindent\textbf{A2: Event progress.} Update control disables only unselected controllables, retains every UC event and outcome, and eventually observes a next event and its successor.
\par\noindent\textbf{A3: Commands.} Commands start quiescently and finish atomically, finitely and without failure, reporting a modeled successor.
\par\noindent\textbf{A4: Boundaries.} Entry copies the actual old state; quiescent handover installs the selected compatible state. Both boundaries are finite, failure-free and linearizable~\cite{HerlihyWing1990}.

Under A1--A4, execution follows the linked model and reaches handover within the entry rank (Theorem~\ref{thm:realization}~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact}). Event progress in A2 gives no fairness preference among choices and no time bound. For a history-based duty, the declared scope must also contain every actual activation history. Internal divergence and unobservable outcomes fall outside these premises.

\section{How Granularity Changes Feasibility}
\label{sec:granularity-analysis}
Merging removes intermediate states and can prevent or enable completion.

\paragraph{What is merged.}
Partition transfers, old stops and new starts separately into blocks. A transfer block $B\subseteq I$ simultaneously permits all targets in $\prod_{i\in B}g_i(s_i)$; a boundary block removes or initializes its monitors at the same component tuple. Other active monitors compose member-label effects, checked to commute pairwise on every monitor/residual state. Mapped precedence must remain irreflexive and acyclic. Ordinary behavior, entries, endpoints, relations, initializers and targets stay fixed; quiescence and all member guards apply. These are comparison contracts, not DUCS encodings.

\input{technical_fragments/granularity_obstruction.tex}

\paragraph{A capacity boundary.}
Readiness gates motivate the constructed Rolling contract~\cite{e6_kubernetes_deployments,e6_mongodb_enterprise_replica_set}. It starts with $n$ ready replicas, no spares, and an interval floor of $m$ ready replicas. A transfer boots its replicas; each becomes ready after one UC event, with no waiting loop. Service self-loops are controllable. The all-new, ready target restores capacity; service may pause during the update.

\begin{proposition}[Rolling threshold]\label{prop:rolling-threshold}
For $n\ge2$, $1\le m<n$ and $1\le k\le n$, fixed consecutive batches of size $k$ (with a smaller final remainder) admit safe finite completion iff $k\le n-m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Some mandatory batch has size $k$. Its transfer leaves at most $n-k$ ready replicas, so $k>n-m$ violates the interval. Otherwise, transfer one batch only when all replicas are ready, disable service, and retain every UC readiness outcome before the next transfer. Readiness never falls below $n-k\ge m$. With $u$ old and $b$ booting replicas, $2u+b$ strictly decreases on every selected transfer and every UC event, reaching zero exactly at the target. Thus all maximal runs finish without fairness.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{When separate transfers preserve feasibility.}
Finer commands need not preserve a merged winner. Two one-state components can synchronize UC labels so that both endpoints are quiescent, but either mixed version has a UC loop. Separate identity transfers then lose; a joint transfer skips the mixed state and wins at rank one (complete contract in Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:granularity-reverse}~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact}).

\begin{proposition}[Serial transfer preservation]\label{prop:serial-transfer}
Consider transfer-only merging with unchanged entries, endpoints, targets, initializers and ordinary behavior, Cartesian transfer outcomes, commuting active-monitor effects and valid mapped precedence. If every transfer preserves its component's UC synchronization alphabet and enables no previously disabled local UC label, a merged winner implies a fine winner preserving the same selected endpoint continuations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The local condition keeps partial batches UC-quiescent. Any partial monitor error would persist to a completed Cartesian outcome, contradicting the merged winner. Finite serial expansion retains completion and endpoint choices; Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:serial-transfer}~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact} gives the full argument.
\end{proof}
The constructed Threads family, with workers sharing a bounded queue, satisfies this condition: old/new worker graphs coincide and transfers preserve idle states and the queue (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:constructed_contracts}~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact}). Rolling enables readiness and violates it, but its capacity threshold separately gives safe batch sizes.
\section{Synthesizing and Checking an Update Policy}
The procedure specializes strong-planning and on-the-fly reachability principles~\cite{Cimatti1998StrongPlanning,TripakisAltisen1999,CiolekDCS}, retaining every queried event outcome and producing evidence for every old entry.
\label{sec:algorithm}

A \emph{bucket} is $\Post(q,a)$; empty means disabled. Disabling controls at a UC-enabled state removes runs without creating deadlock, preserving winning (\emph{UC priority}, Lemma~\ref{lem:uc-priority}~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact}). With quiescent goals and no fairness or controller priority, permitting commands while UC is enabled therefore adds no winning entries. Changing fairness or priority changes the game. The proof establishes memoryless sufficiency.

\subsection{Lazy Successor Generation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:otf} constructs a reachability attractor~\cite{CassezEtAl2005TimedGames}. $V$ contains discovered states, $X$ those with all UC candidates queried, and $W$ proved winners. \emph{Promote} adds an unproved safe state in $X$ to $W$ when its nonempty UC successor set lies in $W$ or, at quiescence, one complete nonempty control bucket lies in $W$. It retains that proving set and assigns rank one above its maximum successor rank. Predecessors are then reconsidered. Lazy queries controls incrementally, keeping unfinished cursors in $\mathsf{Retry}$. Failure requires exhausted candidates and no further promotion. Extraction retains lower-rank controls and matching endpoint targets.

\SetAlgoNlRelativeSize{-1}
\SetAlgoRefRelativeSize{-1}
\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Lazy}
\label{alg:otf}
\footnotesize
\KwIn{Typed update contract (Section~\ref{sec:formalization})}
\KwOut{$(\sigma,\rank,\kappa)$ or $(\textnormal{no solution},L)$}
$V\leftarrow Q_0$; $X,\mathsf{Retry}\leftarrow\emptyset$; $W\leftarrow V\cap\Goal$\;
$\mathsf{Open}\leftarrow\{q\in Q_0\mid\Safe(q)\land q\notin\Goal\}$; set $\rank(q)\leftarrow0$ for $q\in W$\;
\While{true}{
  Add queued goals to $W$ once with rank zero; exhaust \emph{Promote}, retaining witnesses/ranks, removing winners from Retry and propagating predecessors\;
  \lIf{$Q_0\subseteq W$}{\Return $(\sigma,\rank|_{\Reach_\sigma(Q_0)},\kappa)$ constructed from the goal witnesses and retained lower-rank choices}
  \eIf{$\mathsf{Open}\neq\emptyset$}{
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Open}$, add it to $X$, and query all uncontrollable candidates\;
    Close the control cursor if UC-enabled; otherwise initialize it and query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion\;
    Insert $q$ into $\mathsf{Retry}$ iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }{
    \lIf{$\mathsf{Retry}=\emptyset$}{\Return $(\textnormal{no solution},L\leftarrow V\setminus W)$}
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Retry}$; query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion; reinsert iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }
  Add every queried successor to $V$; queue newly discovered goals for rank zero and safe non-goals for $\mathsf{Open}$ (unsafe states stay outside $W$ and $\mathsf{Open}$)\;
}
\end{algorithm}

We examine feasibility (RQ1), the scope of implementation checks (RQ2), and exploration cost (RQ3). Constructed examples isolate mechanisms; \ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts test performance.

Our MTSA extension~\cite{paper:MTSA2008} checks certificates~\cite{McConnellEtAl2011Certifying}; its composition checker, Link, checks the connected old, update and new controllers, including entries and targets. An independent oracle parses a restricted subset of Finite State Processes (FSP)~\cite{MageeKramer2006}. Finite-JSON runs save losing membership; FSP benchmarks report only its statistics.

\paragraph{Use of generative AI}

Nine inputs adapt eight DUCS sources. PC1 and PC2 are production-cell variants sharing one parent; they retain state-gated arrivals and make tool results UC. Pending results and reset/arrival chains finish when work/output is withheld. MTSA synthesizes fixed endpoints from FSP and prepares monitors and initialization tables before search. Interval requirements use a constant post-entry state; physical observers survive commands and transfers. The Cell$_n$ family extends Cell to $n$ stations with holder-dependent initialization. S3--S4~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact} detail these paths and their preparation costs.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\label{sec:expressiveness}
\paragraph{Generating update environments.}
Update-environment generation from supplied global state-preservation transitions precedes FG-DUCS~\cite[Sec.~V]{Yamauchi2020Partitioning}. DUCS adds \texttt{reconfigure} edges through possibly partial $R$, without a UC-quiescence guard~\cite[Sec.~4.2, Defs.~3.7, 5.1]{Nahabedian2020}. FG-DUCS requires global UC quiescence at commands and handover.

\paragraph{On-demand synthesis.}
Directed controller synthesis (DCS) already combines lazy products and controls, UC priority and backward propagation~\cite[Sec.~IV]{CiolekDCS}. Its 2016 formulation requires safety and eventual goal occurrence on every trace; the later nonblocking formulation (OTF-DCS, 2023) requires a marked extension of each finite controlled trace~\cite[Def.~4]{CiolekEtAl2023OTFDCS}. FG-DUCS specializes these patterns to its contract, with full outcome buckets and all-entry certificates. Other approaches structure specifications compositionally or modularly~\cite{MohajeraniMalikFabian2014Compositional,WonhamRamadge1988Modular}.

\paragraph{Supervisor switching.}
Supervisor switching addresses safety and deadlock freedom~\cite[Sec.~3.3]{MeiraGoesLafortune2020Switching}, switching speed~\cite[Sec.~III]{JiaoGanXiao2013Reconfiguration}, startup bounds~\cite{NooruldeenSchmidt2015} and mode invariants~\cite{ReveliotisFei2017SwitchedDES}. FG-DUCS addresses staged handover to a supplied controller.

\paragraph{Requirements across adaptation.}
Obligations across change occur in temporal adaptation, scenario updatability and Live Synthesis~\cite{ZhangCheng2006ALTL,Ghezzi2012DynamicScenarioUpdate,PanzicaLaManna2013CorrectnessDynamicUpdates,FinkbeinerEtAl2022LiveSynthesis}; FG-DUCS uses scoped requirements and residual reasoning~\cite{Dwyer1999Patterns,RosuHavelund2005Rewriting}. Thuijsman and Reniers~\cite[Secs.~4--7]{ThuijsmanReniers2024DynamicFeature} support individual/simultaneous reconfiguration, feature-dependent requirements and state retention/reset. Their guarantee requires a marked continuation from every reachable state; FG-DUCS requires all maximal update runs to finish at a supplied controller.

\paragraph{Safe adaptation paths.}
Safe adaptation graphs support minimum-cost paths~\cite[Secs.~3--4]{Zhang2005SafeAdaptation}. Reconfiguration sequences preserve lifecycle and architectural constraints, reaching the target without failures or a consistent architecture after recovery~\cite[Secs.~II--VI]{BoyerGruberPous2013}. FG-DUCS instead checks finite handover to a fixed controller under declared requirement lifetimes; it does not optimize cost or repair failures.

\paragraph{Operational update mechanisms.}
Local, live, workflow and network updating address migration and transition safety~\cite{PanzicaLaManna2012LocalUpdate,Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate,Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates,e6_hayden2014_kitsune,McClurgEtAl2015NetworkUpdates}. Transaction version consistency~\cite{MaEtAl2011VersionConsistency} is a separate guarantee that finite handover does not imply.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
Execution and monitor premises are unvalidated on a running platform (Section~\ref{sec:interpretation-execution}). Persistent uncontrollable activity can prevent completion, and the contract excludes failure repair. Constructed cases assume finite startup, reporting, recovery and election; selection may favor separations. Their deployed prevalence and the feasibility effects of model adaptation are unestablished.

Shared authorship, AI and code risk correlated faults; independent parsing covers restricted FSP. Direct-Full is in-house, and external solvers and randomized orders are untested. Extended trials lack medians. Sampling process memory can miss peaks and does not identify heap use or bottlenecks; S4~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact} retains interruptions.

The null comparisons, losses, timeouts and faster or smaller alternatives reported in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} bound the gains. Timings use one machine, and fixtures/repeats are not independent applications. Inherited inputs lack precedence and only Railcab transfers branch; exponential separation assumes the default order.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Verified endpoints leave the handover between them unresolved. FG-DUCS constructs that handover from local component transfers, requirement lifetimes and initial obligations. A winning policy safely completes from every old entry under every modeled outcome and preserves the selected new controller's continuation. We identify obstructions to merged commands and a sufficient condition for serializing merged transfers.

The implementation decides 26 of 27 adapted games within 1,200~s. Constructed examples separate granularities in both directions; 54 inherited-model comparisons show no change. These results support finite maintenance planning that permits uncontrollable quiescence. Actual activation conformance remains unverified, and the contract gives neither continuous-service nor elapsed-time bounds.
