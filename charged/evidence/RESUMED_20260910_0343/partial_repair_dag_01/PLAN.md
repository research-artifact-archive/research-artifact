# Partial repair with a global retry allowance: exploratory extension

SCIENTIFIC, 2026-09-10. Root sole writer. Read-only author helper Fermat proposed the no-toll n-job characterization and a two-job example; root checked the argument before this numerical protocol. The helper's small example is already observed and is labelled as such. Prior single-job proofs/results and every adverse outcome remain unchanged. This is a new interface extension, not a Roslyn reduction, independent-blind review, timing result, or proof of literature-wide novelty.

Model: n persistent jobs in a finite DAG. Each job has its own finite positive-weight components and fixed nonempty repeatable write-footprint family. A call observes the union of writes since its fresh preparation and either repairs/accepts/completes that job or rejects. Rejection consumes one of global r extra calls and resets that job's preparation. No cross-job footprint, retained guard, partial publication, free dirty queries, or outside completion. Only the currently attempted job is dirtied. Whole fresh completion is dominated by observing and accepting. Protected repair work is additive; per-call overhead is excluded in this study. Controller receives no actual writer count; a known-B comparator uses the supplied upper bound and minimum consistent consumption from observations. The universal call cap is n+r, and all operations terminate.

Candidate theorem: for r>=1 a simultaneous optimum for every B exists iff each job's maximum damage can be caused by one write. Under that condition its exact curve is the sum of the largest min((B-r)+,n) reachable job works. At r=0 accepting every observation is simultaneous for arbitrary footprints. For a nonsaturating job, B0=sum sigma_i+r separates low-budget-optimal unaware policies (forced full work) from the known-B optimum (strictly below full work). Also test the candidate full-work saturation threshold sum sigma_i+r*max sigma_i.

Fixed numerical universe before root execution: job types consist of all nonempty footprint families on two components with weights (1,1),(1,2),(2,3), plus one-component weights1,2,3 (24 types). All ordered pairs, and DAGs with no edge or either single edge, each r=0,1,2,3. In addition all ordered triples from four fixed representative types, every forward-edge DAG on3 jobs, and r=0,1,2,3. For each input evaluate every B from0 through the full-work saturation threshold+1; omit none. An exact dirty-observation minimax recurrence computes known-budget values without invoking the candidate formulas. For the pair inputs with r<=2, and triple inputs with r<=1, a separate quantified policy-existence search checks one unaware policy against the entire known-budget curve, using states (unfinished set, slack, minimum consistent writes, accumulated work). It does not use the saturation predicate as its answer. Both fixed orders are represented by the two chain DAGs; unrestricted selection is represented by the empty DAG.
