\paragraph{Fixed post-update control.}
\label{sec:ducs-cell-construction}
DUCS synthesizes update and subsequent control from old control, old/new goals and transition requirements~\cite[Defs.~4.1--4.3]{Nahabedian2020}. GR(1) updating synthesizes a new controller $C_2$ and a bridge, then runs that $C_2$ unchanged~\cite[Def.~3.1, Sec.~4]{Amram2022GR1DynamicUpdate}. FG-DUCS instead takes the new controller and loadable targets as inputs. Its update policy selects a target while preserving the supplied continuation (Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-continuation}).

\paragraph{Independent requirement boundaries.}
DUCS's transition requirement $T$ already expresses overlap, gaps and gap duties for one global stop/start pair~\cite[Sec.~2, Def.~4.1]{Nahabedian2020}. FG-DUCS gives each old requirement its stop and each new requirement its start and initializer. Policy(2) requires audit overlap and role exclusion.

\paragraph{Local inputs and generated stages.}
Yamauchi et al. already generate an update environment from pre/post environments and supplied global state-preservation transitions~\cite[Sec.~V]{Yamauchi2020Partitioning}. FG-DUCS generates mixed-version and independent boundary transitions from component transfers, monitors and initializers. DUCS and GR(1) express Cell's paths with added structure; Table~\ref{tab:main-generation} and Technical Appendices~\ref{ta:ducs_cell_complete}--\ref{ta:gr1-cell} give the inputs, full constructions and proofs. The DUCS witness retains all endpoint edges after its added handover event $h$ and requires eventual $h$, beyond the published safety-$T$ implementation~\cite[Sec.~5.3]{Nahabedian2020}; GR(1) may select fewer endpoint edges. These fully controllable, deterministic examples establish neither general translation nor cost advantage. S5 compares native contracts.
