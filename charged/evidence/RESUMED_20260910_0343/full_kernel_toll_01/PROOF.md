# Full-kernel toll theorem and its limits

Let w_i>0, kappa>=0, r>=0, and let P_r be the serial freshly preparing three-mode policy that uses a cheap validation/publication until r failures have occurred, then cached-completes every remaining job. Ready-job choice may depend on history. Write F(B)=Omega_min((B-r)+,n). The call cap is Q<=n+r on every finite-write execution. The B-informed scalar comparator C*(B,r) minimizes worst C=L+kappa Q subject to the call cap on environments with at most B writes, over the full program class in the paper, not merely threshold policies.

P_r has f<=min(B,r) failures, exactly n completing calls, and L<=F(B), so C(P_r,B)<=n*kappa+kappa*min(B,r)+F(B). Equality: if B<r, reject the first B attempted cheap calls, then cease writing. If B>=r, reject the first r cheap calls; none completes. Thereafter spend one write before each cached comparison of the min(B-r,n) heaviest jobs. All other calls match. Persistence and own-job targeting make this adversary valid under any DAG or ready-job selection. It gives f=r and L=F(B), using at most B writes. The cases r=0, B=0 and excess unused writes are covered.

Every completing program performs at least n counted calls. The whole-kernel lower bound supplies, for each admissible program, an environment with L>=F(B), on which Q>=n as well. Hence C*(B,r)>=n*kappa+F(B), and

0 <= C(P_r,B)-C*(B,r) <= kappa*min(B,r) <= r*kappa.

The left inequality holds since P_r is a candidate for the informed comparison. This is a loss in charged operation units, not elapsed time and not a sum of independently attained maxima: the threshold's simultaneous maximum was explicitly constructed, and the comparator bound uses Q>=n on the same L-witness path. It requires only the fixed toll per counted call. If each actual per-call overhead merely lies in [0,kappa_max], the analogous additive statement relative to L alone is different and is not asserted here.

The threshold bound is sharp for n=1 at B=r+1: the adversary invalidates every cheap/cached attempt until completion; any fresh completion already costs w. Thus every candidate has worst cost at least kappa+w. Immediate cached (or fresh) completion attains kappa+w. P_r pays (r+1)*kappa+w, a gap r*kappa for any positive kappa,w. No claim is made that this is the smallest possible worst-budget regret among all budget-unaware programs.

Exact simultaneous optimality can fail. Take n=1, r>=1 and 0<kappa<w. At B=1, a cheap-first informed policy achieves2*kappa by using another cheap call after its single failure. A first completing call has worst kappa+w>2*kappa. A first cheap call can be forced to fail, after which any completion costs at least another kappa. Thus C*(1,r)=2*kappa and any simultaneous optimum must begin with cheap, up to free preparation/inspection/discard operations. Now consider B=r+1. Invalidating each subsequently attempted cheap/cached call is affordable through the at-most-r failures and the one completion. After the first cheap failure, eventual completion costs at least another kappa+w; noncompletion violates admissibility. This gives at least2*kappa+w>C*(r+1,r)=kappa+w. No common optimum exists. Fresh identities and the lack of a retained guard make intervening free inspections unable to defeat the next write.

Consequently the exact kernel-work frontier does not become an exact protected-time frontier by adding a small toll. It remains useful as a counted-resource theorem and gives an explicit robustness bound for the same implementable policy. The partial-repair call-toll theorems use a different, stronger observation-then-accept interface and are not silently substituted for this before-comparison mode choice.
