The theorem bounds every permitted immediate-preparation caller by `(3r+2)/(2r+2)` times the full simultaneous-contract general-program optimum for an arbitrary finite DAG. Its sharpness ranges over the full permitted-caller class. It does not establish a sharp factor for minimum-ready scheduling or solve the general-DAG exact optimum. The whole-kernel accounting assumptions, geometric witness interpretation and inherited lower-bound obligations remain explicit in the proofs.

The [finite protocol](evidence/RESUMED_20260911_1123/dag_approximation_01/FINITE_PROTOCOL01.md) fixes n=1..4, every index-ordered edge subset, weights in {1,2,4}, r=0,1,2 and B=0..n+r. The result is **16,263 roots,96,795 budget rows,4,632,516 expanded states and zero violations**. The96,795 count belongs to this new approximation study; it is unrelated to the older retained-record study with the same numeric count. Per-budget maximization in the checker does not furnish B to an implemented caller and does not enumerate the full general-program comparator class; its lower bound remains an analytical obligation.

The same evaluator produces72 integer geometric illustrations for r=1..8 and n in {1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256}. It retains every integer weight, exact fraction, decimal ratio, alpha and gap. The finite maximum observed ratio is26/21; the uniform limiting sharpness is an analytic statement, not the finite maximum.
