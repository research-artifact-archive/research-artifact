\title[Staged Updates between Verified Controllers]{Synthesizing Staged Updates between Verified Controllers}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Verified controllers for old and new versions do not by themselves provide a safe transition. Component replacement and requirement activation can require different schedules. Fine-Grained Dynamic Update Controller Synthesis (FG-DUCS) compiles local transfers, requirement lifetimes and initial obligations into an update policy between supplied controllers. A winning policy covers every reachable old entry and permitted outcome, enforces the declared lifetimes, completes the update and preserves the selected new controller's continuation. We prove local-contract/game correspondence and specialize established lazy reachability methods to return a policy with a progress rank or a losing-region certificate. Complete examples show that merging update steps can prevent or enable completion. Within 1,200~s, an MTSA implementation decides \LazyDecisions{}/\ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts, compared with \FixedEagerDecisions{} for eager successors, \FixedUpdateFirstDecisions{} for update-first exploration and \DirectFullDecisions{} for full construction. Update-first is sometimes faster. Constructed families exhibit granularity effects; inherited inputs exhibit none. Commands and handover require uncontrollable quiescence; continuous service and elapsed-time bounds are not guaranteed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
A verified controller governs one version. Updating requires reaching another while component states and active requirements change~\cite{Cheng2009SASRoadmap,Andersson2013SEAMSProcesses,Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate,Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates}. The endpoints do not supply this intervening policy. We generate it while preserving the supplied new controller's behavior after handover.

Components and requirements pose different scheduling choices. With one workpiece shared by two stations, an empty-station guard prevents joint replacement, but moving the workpiece enables separate replacements. Requirements can need opposite boundary orders: new-start before old-stop maintains audit coverage; old-stop before new-start avoids overlapping exclusive roles.

The policy must handle different old states and every environment outcome, retaining obligations already due, such as inspection before departure. A fixed sequence may not suffice, and safety alone permits an update to remain unfinished forever.

\emph{FG-DUCS compiles local choices into a policy for safe finite handover.} The modeler supplies transfers, requirement lifetimes, monitor initializers and compatible endpoint states. The compiler generates mixed-state behavior and chooses a policy; neither a mixed-state graph nor a sequence is supplied. Modeling and transfer code remain the modeler's responsibility; effort reduction is unmeasured.

Existing DUCS generates update and subsequent control from global transfers and transition requirements~\cite{Nahabedian2016SEAMS,Nahabedian2020}; GR(1) updating generates a new controller and bridge~\cite{Amram2022GR1DynamicUpdate}. Both express Cell with additional supplied structure (Section~\ref{sec:ducs-cell-construction}). FG-DUCS fixes the endpoints and generates the intervening game from local transfers, independent boundaries and initializers. Its contribution is this construction and its guarantees, not greater general expressiveness or a new reachability algorithm.

Our contributions are:
\par\noindent\textbf{C1: A local contract and its meaning.} We define the generated game, prove local-history correspondence and selected-endpoint preservation, and give an invariant-initialization criterion (Sections~\ref{sec:formalization} and~\ref{sec:monitor-meaning}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C2: Boundaries of update granularity.} Complete examples and merging obstructions explain when separate choices are necessary and when they prevent completion (Sections~\ref{sec:example} and~\ref{sec:granularity-analysis}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C3: A policy with checkable evidence.} Sound and complete lazy synthesis returns a completing policy and rank or a losing-region certificate (Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C4: Implementation evidence.} An MTSA implementation is checked against finite reference games and evaluated on \ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts; constructed comparisons test the granularity mechanisms separately (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}). All unfavorable and unresolved outcomes are retained.

The target is a finite maintenance interval: controllable work may pause while pending environment events finish. Commands and handover require global uncontrollable quiescence. Duties needed throughout must be declared as interval requirements; otherwise old-stop and new-start may leave a gap. Continuous service and elapsed-time bounds are not guaranteed; an environment that can avoid quiescence forever prevents completion.

Our public replication package~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact}, prepared for this paper, includes the cited Technical Appendix A--P and Supplement S1--S5 as Parts I and II of \texttt{supplementary\_material.pdf}. Data Availability gives versioned links.

Policy(2) makes the second choice explicit. Four bits record active old/new audit rules $a_o,a_n$ and roles $r_o,r_n$. The interval invariant $(a_o\lor a_n)\land\neg(r_o\land r_n)$ requires opposing boundary orders:
\[
\begin{array}{r@{\quad}c@{\;}c@{\;}c@{\;}c@{\;}c}

\subsection{Execution and Handover}

The execution connection has four distinct premises (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:execution_proof}):
\par\noindent\textbf{A1:} endpoint observations preserve component/monitor states and same-labeled endpoint edges.
\par\noindent\textbf{A2:} update control disables only unselected controllables, preserves every UC event/outcome, reports the successor and eventually observes a next event.

\section{How Granularity Changes Feasibility}
\label{sec:granularity-analysis}
Merging commands removes intermediate states. We show why this can either prevent or enable completion; Section~\ref{sec:rq2} evaluates the specified comparisons.

\paragraph{What is merged.}
Partition transfers, old stops and new starts separately into blocks. A transfer block $B\subseteq I$ simultaneously permits all targets in $\prod_{i\in B}g_i(s_i)$; a boundary block removes or initializes its monitors at the same component tuple. Other active monitors compose member-label effects, checked to commute pairwise on every monitor/residual state. Mapped precedence must remain irreflexive and acyclic. Ordinary behavior, entries, endpoints, relations, initializers and targets stay fixed; quiescence and all member guards apply. These are comparison contracts, not DUCS encodings.

The same obstruction explains three cases.
\input{technical_fragments/granularity_obstruction.tex}

\paragraph{A capacity boundary.}
Readiness gates motivate the constructed Rolling contract~\cite{e6_kubernetes_deployments,e6_mongodb_enterprise_replica_set}. It starts with $n$ ready replicas, no spares, and an interval floor of $m$ ready replicas. A transfer boots its replicas; each becomes ready after one UC event, with no waiting loop. Service self-loops are controllable. The all-new, ready target restores capacity; service may pause during the update.

\begin{proposition}[Rolling threshold]\label{prop:rolling-threshold}
For $n\ge2$, $1\le m<n$ and $1\le k\le n$, fixed consecutive batches of size $k$ (with a smaller final remainder) admit safe finite completion iff $k\le n-m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Some mandatory batch has size $k$. Its transfer leaves at most $n-k$ ready replicas, so $k>n-m$ violates the interval. Otherwise, transfer one batch only when all replicas are ready, disable service, and retain every UC readiness outcome before the next transfer. Readiness never falls below $n-k\ge m$. With $u$ old and $b$ booting replicas, $2u+b$ strictly decreases on every selected transfer and every UC event, reaching zero exactly at the target. Thus all maximal runs finish without fairness.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why finer is not always better.}
Two components with one state per version share UC labels $x,y$. The only UC loops are $y$ in old~1, $x$ in new~1, $x$ in old~2 and $y$ in new~2; shared labels synchronize. Thus all-old and all-new are quiescent, but either mixed product has a UC loop. With identity transfers and no requirements, either first local transfer prevents quiescence forever, so fine loses. A joint transfer skips the mixed state and wins at rank one. A shared controllable loop makes both endpoints deadlock-free. Atomicity can therefore remove either a necessary or a harmful intermediate state; there is no general refinement order (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:granularity-reverse}).
\section{Synthesizing and Checking an Update Policy}
The procedure specializes strong-planning and on-the-fly reachability principles~\cite{Cimatti1998StrongPlanning,TripakisAltisen1999,CiolekDCS}, retaining every queried event outcome and producing evidence for every old entry.
\label{sec:algorithm}

A \emph{bucket} is $\Post(q,a)$; empty means disabled. Disabling controls at a UC-enabled state removes runs without creating deadlock, preserving winning (\emph{UC priority}, Lemma~\ref{lem:uc-priority}). With quiescent goals and no fairness or controller priority, permitting commands while UC is enabled therefore adds no winning entries. Changing fairness or priority changes the game. The proof establishes memoryless sufficiency.

\subsection{Lazy Successor Generation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:otf} constructs a reachability attractor~\cite{CassezEtAl2005TimedGames}. $V$ contains discovered states, $X$ those with all UC candidates queried, and $W$ proved winners. A UC state needs all successors to win; a quiescent state needs one complete control bucket. Lazy queries controls incrementally, keeping unfinished cursors in $\mathsf{Retry}$. Each promotion records a complete nonempty proving set and a rank above its members. Reverse incidences propagate promotions. Failure requires exhausted candidates and a promotion fixed point. Extraction chooses lower-rank controls and compatible targets; it need not minimize path length.

\SetAlgoNlRelativeSize{-1}
\SetAlgoRefRelativeSize{-1}
\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Lazy}
\label{alg:otf}
\footnotesize
\KwIn{Typed update contract (Section~\ref{sec:formalization})}
\KwOut{$(\sigma,\rank,\kappa)$ or $(\textnormal{no solution},L)$}
$V\leftarrow Q_0$; $X,\mathsf{Retry}\leftarrow\emptyset$; $W\leftarrow V\cap\Goal$\;
$\mathsf{Open}\leftarrow\{q\in Q_0\mid\Safe(q)\land q\notin\Goal\}$; set $\rank(q)\leftarrow0$ for $q\in W$\;
\While{true}{
  Add queued goals to $W$ once with rank zero; exhaust \emph{Promote}, retaining witnesses/ranks, removing winners from Retry and propagating predecessors\;
  \lIf{$Q_0\subseteq W$}{\Return $(\sigma,\rank|_{\Reach_\sigma(Q_0)},\kappa)$ constructed from the goal witnesses and retained lower-rank choices}
  \eIf{$\mathsf{Open}\neq\emptyset$}{
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Open}$, add it to $X$, and query all uncontrollable candidates\;
    Close the control cursor if UC-enabled; otherwise initialize it and query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion\;
    Insert $q$ into $\mathsf{Retry}$ iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }{
    \lIf{$\mathsf{Retry}=\emptyset$}{\Return $(\textnormal{no solution},L\leftarrow V\setminus W)$}
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Retry}$; query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion; reinsert iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }
  Add every queried successor to $V$; queue newly discovered goals for rank zero and safe non-goals for $\mathsf{Open}$ (unsafe states stay outside $W$ and $\mathsf{Open}$)\;
}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\label{sec:expressiveness}
\input{technical_fragments/ducs_cell_correspondence.tex}

\paragraph{On-the-fly updating.}
O-DUCS separates update and post-update analysis~\cite[Sec.~3]{HiranoEtAl2024ODUCS}. The fork's plain OTF precomputes a new controller and reconnects matching environment/safety states~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact}; identity with the 2024 release is unestablished. FG adds supplied endpoints/targets, local transfers, individual lifetimes and initializers to this existing reconnection.

\paragraph{On-demand synthesis.}
DCS already explores component products on demand, defers controllable alternatives, treats states enabling both event classes as uncontrollable, and propagates success or failure backward~\cite[Sec.~IV]{CiolekDCS}. The 2016 formulation requires safety and eventual goal occurrence on every closed-loop trace; OTF-DCS (2023) requires a marked extension of each finite controlled trace~\cite[Def.~4]{CiolekEtAl2023OTFDCS}. FG specializes these search patterns to its generated contract, with complete event-outcome buckets and all-entry certificates for requirement lifetimes and matched handover. Compositional and modular control instead structure specifications for synthesis~\cite{MohajeraniMalikFabian2014Compositional,WonhamRamadge1988Modular}.

\paragraph{Requirements across adaptation.}
Temporal adaptation~\cite{ZhangCheng2006ALTL}, scenario-based updatability~\cite{Ghezzi2012DynamicScenarioUpdate,PanzicaLaManna2013CorrectnessDynamicUpdates} and Live Synthesis~\cite{FinkbeinerEtAl2022LiveSynthesis} address obligations across change. Our initialization uses scoped requirements~\cite{Dwyer1999Patterns} and residual reasoning~\cite{RosuHavelund2005Rewriting}. State attraction bounds startup under weak invariance~\cite{NooruldeenSchmidt2015}. Thuijsman and Reniers~\cite[Secs.~4--7]{ThuijsmanReniers2024DynamicFeature} allow individual or simultaneous reconfiguration, requirements that depend on features, and state retention or reset. Their supervisor permits a marked continuation from every reachable state; FG requires every maximal update run to finish at a supplied controller.

\paragraph{Safe adaptation paths.}
Zhang et al. generate safe adaptation graphs from dependency invariants and adaptive actions, then select minimum-cost configuration paths~\cite[Secs.~3--4]{Zhang2005SafeAdaptation}. FG-DUCS seeks finite handover to a supplied controller from every old entry under every permitted outcome, with independent requirement boundaries; it does not optimize adaptation cost.

\paragraph{Operational update mechanisms.}
Local updating~\cite{PanzicaLaManna2012LocalUpdate}, PROTEOS~\cite{Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate}, workflow updating~\cite{Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates}, Kitsune~\cite{e6_hayden2014_kitsune} and network synthesis~\cite{McClurgEtAl2015NetworkUpdates} address migration and transition safety. Ma et al. enforce transaction version consistency using dynamic dependencies and sufficient freeness, permitting concurrent versions and selective blocking~\cite{MaEtAl2011VersionConsistency}. These mechanisms differ from global UC quiescence; finite handover alone gives no transaction consistency.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
Permanently enabled UC arrivals or polling block even local commands. Threads contrasts finite UC phases with perpetual activity. Fairness-dependent waiting is outside the contract; inherited-adaptation effects on feasibility are not isolated. Decoding, history completeness and A1--A4 remain unvalidated; repair is excluded. Constructed cases assume finite startup, reporting, recovery and election; selection may favor separations, and deployed prevalence is unestablished.

Shared authorship, AI and code risk correlated faults; independent parsing covers restricted FSP. Direct-Full is in-house; Legacy's objective differs. Pruning isolation, external solvers and randomized orders remain untested. Ratios omit unresolved costs; extended trials lack medians. Sampling can miss peaks; RSS identifies neither heap occupancy nor bottlenecks. S4 retains interruptions.

Null/LOSS results, faster Update-first, lower Eager/Update-first memory and Travel/PC limits bound gains. PC2's merged timeout remains unresolved. Fixtures/repeats are not independent applications; timing uses one machine. Inherited inputs lack precedence; only Railcab transfers branch. Exponential separation assumes the default order.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
FG-DUCS compiles local contracts into certified finite updates between verified controllers, preserving the selected endpoint's continuation. Granularity changes feasibility in both directions. Evaluation shows reduced exploration and more fixed-budget decisions. These guarantees require declared requirement lifetimes, uncontrollable quiescence and faithful execution.
