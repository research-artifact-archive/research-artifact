\paragraph{A duty installed at activation.}\label{sec:cell-activation-duty}
Cell's initializer sets the owed-inspection bit exactly when $B$ holds the workpiece. Event $m_B$ sets it, $j$ clears it, and $m_A$ is forbidden while it is 1; other labels preserve it. Monitor states $c,d$ encode bits 0,1 with identical transitions, including rejection. Matching initialization and induction over events therefore prove exact enforcement of this duty rule. A start-only history reference would also admit clear initialization while $B$ holds, omitting this duty (TA~\ref{ta:cell-residual}). Prior-history exactness alone therefore does not select the intended meaning.

\paragraph{Invariants that begin at activation.}\label{sec:activation-scoped-main}
For an invariant $\Box\varphi$ intended to begin at activation, let $v$ be the post-start observer values and $d^*(v,u)$ their values after event word $u$. Its permitted continuations are
\[
 \operatorname{ACT}_\varphi(v)=\{w\mid\forall u\preceq w:\varphi(d^*(v,u))\},
\]
including the empty prefix: the invariant must hold at activation itself. A matching initializer selects monitor state $m$ with continuation language $L_M(m)=\operatorname{ACT}_\varphi(v)$. For a faithful invariant monitor, safe reachability of $(m,v)$ in the unrestricted monitor--observer product suffices (TA~\ref{ta:lookup-act}).

The witnessing reference word establishes the continuation language and need not be an executable update history; actual initialization and subsequent observations must realize that language, and commands that preserve physical observer values require the compatibility argument in TA~\ref{ta:command-observation}. Truth of $\varphi$ alone does not ensure a lookup entry, and undefined entries or fallback initializers are outside this criterion.
