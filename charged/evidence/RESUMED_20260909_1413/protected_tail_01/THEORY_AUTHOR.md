# Protected-tail certificate: same universal frontier, weakly lower worst work

This is an author-derived result after the recorded four-job counterexample, checked by a read-only author-side mathematical helper and fixed equations/native traces. It is not an independent blind review or mechanical proof. The earlier threshold-family proposition remains valid; its extension to all causal universally frontier-optimal programs is false.

Use the paper's fresh-preparation, whole-kernel atomic-call interface. Let Ω(S) be remaining mandatory work and Top_h(J) the sum of the largest min(h,n) input weights. Start with a fixed legal order (the minimum-ready-work order is one choice) and a global allowance r of cheap failures. After allowance exhaustion, keep h = number of observed cached mismatches and ell = work already executed under protection. At a boundary BEFORE preparing another job, with no outstanding preparations for unfinished jobs, test

    ell + Ω(S) <= Top_h(J).

If true, finish all remaining jobs freshly under protection. Otherwise freshly prepare the next job outside and cached-complete it. h/ell change only from observed callback outcomes; the selector receives neither B nor writer/path state. The weights must be fixed source work, so this refinement does not transfer as a work theorem to variable/lazy Roslyn transformations.

Every cheap failure consumes one allowance and every later call completes, hence Q=n+f<=n+r. Fresh preparations and disjoint read/comparison intervals back r+h mismatches by distinct writes, implying B>=r+h. Before firing the guard, ell is a sum of h distinct cached-recomputation job works, bounded by Top_h(J). A firing guard gives final L=ell+Ω(S)<=Top_h(J)<=Top_min((B-r)+,n)(J), regardless of later writes. If no guard fires, the original cached bound applies. The universal lower bound over all Q-bounded budget-unaware programs gives equality of worst L at every B. It is unnecessary to preserve the original threshold policy's exact attaining executions.

For work dominance, fix any guarded execution at budget at most B. Until a firing guard, its prefix π is identical to a threshold execution. The guarded suffix costs exactly Ω(S). Continue the threshold prefix with NO further writes: it freshly prepares each remaining job and all cached completions match, also costing exactly Ω(S). This continuation uses at most B writes. Thus every guarded W value is attained by some legal threshold execution. No-guard executions are already threshold executions. Taking suprema proves worst W_guard(B)<=worst W_threshold(B) for every B. This is a comparison of worst values, not pathwise equivalence under one fixed schedule.

For the chain (2,4,1,1), r=1, B=4, threshold worst W=18 and guarded worst W=17, with both worst L=7 and Q=5. If the first cheap failure is job2 and cached jobs2/3 mismatch, ell+Ω(S)=5+1=6=Top_2(J), so the final job is fresh. First cheap failure on job1 permits at most 2+Top_3(J)=9 excess work; failure first on job2 permits at most4+5=9; later first failures give less. A failure on job1 followed by cached mismatches on jobs1/2/3 attains W=8+9=17. The successive nonempty guard comparisons remain 8>0,4,6,7.

The full four-job curves for B=0,1,2,3,4,5+ are L=0,0,4,6,7,8; threshold W=8,12,16,17,18,18; guarded W=8,12,16,17,17,18. The final return to18 is adverse evidence retained: the gain is budget-specific, not an all-B strict improvement. General W-optimality remains unproved. Observing cached recomputation, finite at-most-B writer histories that may stop after a prefix, fixed whole work and the full lower-bound environment are essential.
