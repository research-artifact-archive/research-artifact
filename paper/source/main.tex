\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Verified endpoints leave their intervening update unspecified. Fine-Grained Dynamic Update Controller Synthesis (FG-DUCS) compiles local transfers and requirement lifetimes into policies that safely finish from every old entry under every outcome and preserve all continuations of the selected new-controller state. We prove correspondence between local histories and the generated game, and certificate reuse across endpoints with equal entry/target interfaces. A sound and complete lazy procedure returns a policy, rank and handover map, or certifies impossibility. Structural results give merging obstructions and the exact Rolling threshold $k\le n-m$. An MTSA implementation decides \LazyDecisions{}/\ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts within budget versus \DirectFullDecisions{} with full construction. Among \VThreeESixMainPairs{} controlled comparisons, \VThreeESixWitness{} across five families are local-WIN/merged-LOSS. Guarantees cover declared safety lifetimes and quiescent commands/handover; deployment requires a conforming adapter.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Verified endpoints leave their intervening update unspecified~\cite{Cheng2009SASRoadmap,Andersson2013SEAMSProcesses,Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate,Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates}. Updating a cell that holds a workpiece may require ordinary movement before replacing an empty station, while old and new obligations overlap. Every old entry and transformation outcome must safely reach a compatible state of the intended new controller; safety alone permits infinite execution.

\emph{Our result is a controller-preserving construction from local update contracts.} FG-DUCS aligns mixed component versions, active requirement memories and the controller state installed at handover. Inputs give component transfers, requirement lifetimes and explicit initializers; compilation generates mixed behavior and scheduling choices. Synthesis returns a completing policy, rank and handover map, or proves impossibility.

DUCS synthesizes updates and subsequent control from global transfers and transition requirements~\cite{Nahabedian2016SEAMS,Nahabedian2020}. Both DUCS and GR(1) updating already generate update models or bridges. FG-DUCS's contribution is its local input contract and the generation of mixed behavior with fixed-endpoint continuation preservation. With local data fixed, equal entry/target interfaces preserve WIN/LOSS certificates even when subsequent endpoint behavior differs. Section~\ref{sec:ducs-cell-construction}'s Cell constructions make these responsibilities concrete.

\par\noindent\textbf{C1: Contract compilation.} Local inputs generate mixed behavior with continuation preservation and endpoint-interface certificate reuse (Section~\ref{sec:formalization}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C2: Certifying synthesis.} Sound and complete lazy construction, with checkable WIN/LOSS certificates and explicit interpretation/execution premises (Sections~\ref{sec:algorithm}--\ref{sec:interpretation-execution}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C3: Structural boundaries.} Mandatory-command obstructions, an exact Rolling batch threshold, and default-order $n+1$ versus $2^n$ exploration (Sections~\ref{thm:lazy-separation},~\ref{sec:rq2}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C4: Traceable implementation evidence.} \ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts and \VThreeESixMainPairs{} controlled comparisons, with source-to-monitor/handover checks and all adverse outcomes (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}).

The evaluation examines feasibility (RQ1), correctness (RQ2) and exploration (RQ3). Commands and handover require uncontrollable (UC) quiescence. Guarantees cover declared safety lifetimes and finite completion; old liveness and undeclared service rates are outside the contract.

\paragraph{Why matching and reconnection both matter.}
The Cell prefix $\rho_B,m_B,\mathsf{start},\mathsf{stop},\rho_A$ safely executes every command but leaves inspection pending. \section{Synthesizing and Checking an Update Policy}
Lazy explores products on demand using finite reachability reasoning~\cite{Cimatti1998StrongPlanning,TripakisAltisen1999,CiolekEtAl2023OTFDCS}.
\label{sec:algorithm}

A \emph{bucket} is $\Post(q,a)$; an empty bucket disables $a$. At a UC-enabled state, disabling controls preserves winning because every UC outcome already requires handling (Lemma~\ref{lem:uc-priority}).

\subsection{Lazy Successor Generation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:otf} constructs a reachability attractor~\cite{CassezEtAl2005TimedGames}. It tracks discovered states $V$, states $X\subseteq V$ with all UC candidates queried, and proved winners $W$. States in $X$ may retain unqueried controls; $W$ may omit winners.

Delaying another controllable event never removes an outcome of the selected event.

Expansion queries every UC candidate. If none is enabled, it queries controls through the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion; $\mathsf{Retry}$ resumes unfinished cursors. Each query retains all outcomes. Candidates include local events and precedence-enabled commands in any fixed finite order.

\emph{Promote.} A safe expanded state wins when its nonempty proving set---all UC successors, or one control bucket at quiescence---lies in $W$; its rank is one above their maximum. Reverse incidences trigger predecessor reconsideration. Failure requires exhausted candidates and a promotion fixed point. Once all entries win, extraction selects one lower-rank control bucket at each reached quiescent non-goal, none elsewhere; it need not follow promotion or minimize length.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Lazy}
\label{alg:otf}
\footnotesize
\KwIn{Typed update contract (Section~\ref{sec:formalization})}
\KwOut{$(\sigma,\rank,\kappa)$ or $(\textnormal{no solution},L)$}
$V\leftarrow Q_0$; $X,\mathsf{Retry}\leftarrow\emptyset$; $W\leftarrow V\cap\Goal$\;
$\mathsf{Open}\leftarrow\{q\in Q_0\mid\Safe(q)\land q\notin\Goal\}$; set $\rank(q)\leftarrow0$ for $q\in W$\;
\While{true}{
  Add queued goals to $W$ once with rank zero; exhaust \emph{Promote}, retaining witnesses/ranks, removing winners from Retry and propagating predecessors\;
  \lIf{$Q_0\subseteq W$}{\Return $(\sigma,\rank|_{\Reach_\sigma(Q_0)},\kappa)$ constructed from the goal witnesses and retained lower-rank choices}
  \eIf{$\mathsf{Open}\neq\emptyset$}{
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Open}$, add it to $X$, and query all uncontrollable candidates\;
    Close the control cursor if UC-enabled; otherwise initialize it and query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion\;
    Insert $q$ into $\mathsf{Retry}$ iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }{
    \lIf{$\mathsf{Retry}=\emptyset$}{\Return $(\textnormal{no solution},L\leftarrow V\setminus W)$}
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Retry}$; query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion; reinsert iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }
  Queue newly discovered goals for rank-zero insertion; put newly discovered safe non-goals in $\mathsf{Open}$\;
}
\end{algorithm}

The execution connection has four distinct premises (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:execution_proof}):
\par\noindent\textbf{A1:} endpoint observations preserve component/monitor states and same-labeled endpoint edges.
\par\noindent\textbf{A2:} update control disables only unselected controllables, preserves every UC event/outcome, reports the successor and eventually observes a next event.

\subsection{Design and Implementation}

Four same-game methods use \ApplicationContracts{} contracts from \ApplicationModels{} inputs. Legacy, our traditional DUCS/GR fork, instead synthesizes subsequent control. Base has no interval requirement: its policy may stop all old requirements before starting any new one. R1/R2 constrain that gap: R1 restricts ordinary actions (Railcab bans braking); R2 constrains boundaries versus transfers (GSM's decode boundaries precede ENV transfer). \paragraph{Implementation.}
Our Modal Transition System Analyser (MTSA) extension~\cite{paper:MTSA2008} checks certificates~\cite{McConnellEtAl2011Certifying}; Link checks old--update--new composition, entries and targets. An independent oracle parses restricted Finite State Processes (FSP). Finite-JSON runs save losing membership; FSP benchmark runs report only its statistics. Runtime conformance is unchecked.

\paragraph{Use of generative AI}
Generative AI assisted design, implementation, tests, experiments, analysis, proofs and writing. Editorial reviews used Claude Opus 5.5 and ChatGPT Pro/Latest, not human evaluation. Authors checked outputs through tests, certificates, oracles and primary sources and retain responsibility; correlated faults remain possible (models, dates and roles in Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:implementation_details}).

\paragraph{Constructing the benchmark inputs.}
Nine inputs---GSM, Industry, MetaSocket, PC1, PC2, PowerPlant, Railcab, Surveillance and Workflow---adapt eight DUCS sources; PC1/PC2 share a parent. Base/R1/R2 vary only interval requirements. Adaptations make PC tool results UC, shorten Railcab transfer paths while preserving both outcomes, and omit PowerPlant's initial ENV transfer pair. They also change safety and ordering; whole-problem equivalence is unestablished (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:additional_populations}).

MTSA's GR (generalized-reactivity) backend fixes endpoints in each JVM; targets include every reachable new-endpoint state. Users supply FSP goals and component/transfer declarations. Before search, the frontend compiles monitors/boundaries, enumerates physical closure under ordinary events and transfers, and fills new-initializer tables from current fluent observers; interval tables use post-entry values. These global-tuple maps are generated, not independently local initializers. The first component stores observers: nonlocal labels stutter its plant while updating them; commands/transfers preserve them. Lazy generates the additional monitor/command product on demand. Total JVM cost includes preprocessing. PC1/PC2 use one/two arms.

\paragraph{Exploration variants.}
All methods share entries, successors, goals, checks and terminals. The on-the-fly (OTF) variants omit controls at UC-enabled states and stop when all entries win. \subsection{RQ1: Can Local Choices Change Feasibility?}
\label{sec:claim-granularity}
\label{sec:rq2}
Cell and Policy isolate component and requirement-lifetime obstructions (Section~\ref{sec:example}). We next examine availability mechanisms and controlled comparisons.

\paragraph{What is merged.}
Partition transfers, old stops and new starts separately into blocks. A transfer block $B\subseteq I$ simultaneously permits all targets in $\prod_{i\in B}g_i(s_i)$; a boundary block removes or initializes its monitors at the same component tuple. Other active monitors compose member-label effects, checked to commute pairwise on every monitor/residual state. Mapped precedence must remain irreflexive and acyclic. Ordinary behavior, entries, endpoints, relations, initializers and targets stay fixed; quiescence and all member guards apply. These are comparison contracts, not DUCS encodings.

The following obstruction explains the Cell, Policy and PC2 merges directly; the rolling threshold then gives an exact quantitative boundary.
\input{technical_fragments/granularity_obstruction.tex}

\paragraph{Mechanisms and scope.}
Readiness/rejoining gates motivate staged replacement~\cite{e6_kubernetes_deployments,e6_mongodb_enterprise_replica_set}. Rolling begins with $n$ ready replicas and no spares; its interval requirement keeps at least $m$ ready. Each transfer boots its replicas; each has one UC readiness event and no waiting loop. Service self-loops are controllable. The target is all-new and ready.

\begin{proposition}[Rolling threshold]\label{prop:rolling-threshold}
For $n\ge2$, $1\le m<n$ and $1\le k\le n$, fixed consecutive batches of size $k$ (with a smaller final remainder) admit safe finite completion iff $k\le n-m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Some mandatory batch has size $k$. Its transfer leaves at most $n-k$ ready replicas, so $k>n-m$ violates the interval. Otherwise, transfer one batch only when all replicas are ready, disable service, and retain every UC readiness outcome before the next transfer. Readiness never falls below $n-k\ge m$. With $u$ old and $b$ booting replicas, $2u+b$ strictly decreases on every selected transfer and every UC event, reaching zero exactly at the target. Thus all maximal runs finish without fairness.
\end{proof}

The 15 Rolling separations compare $k=1$ with $k=n$; the \VThreeESixRollingThresholdCells{} threshold cells are separate. Canary adds health reports/recovery, DB-Rolling role-sensitive replacement, and Rolling+Audit overlapping duties. Policy isolates opposing boundaries; Threads supplies both-WIN/both-LOSS quiescence controls. Table~\ref{tab:rq2-family-counts} keeps all outcomes. Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:constructed_contracts} specifies mechanisms and assumption controls; their deployed prevalence is unestablished.

\textbf{Answer.} The suite yields \VThreeESixWitness{}/\VThreeESixMainPairs{} fine-WIN/merged-LOSS comparisons across five families. Each obstruction identifies choices lost by the specified merge.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\label{sec:expressiveness}
Section~\ref{sec:ducs-cell-construction} compares DUCS and GR(1) through concrete Cell constructions.

PROTEOS filters replacement interfaces~\cite[Secs.~4.3--4.6]{Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate}; workflow updating blocks tasks~\cite[Secs.~4.3, 5.2]{Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates}; Kitsune migrates C programs~\cite[Sec.~2]{e6_hayden2014_kitsune}; network synthesis preserves packet-processing properties~\cite{McClurgEtAl2015NetworkUpdates}. FG-DUCS jointly schedules ordinary events, transfers and monitor lifetimes with finite completion.

O-DUCS prioritizes updates~\cite{HiranoEtAl2024ODUCS}; Live Synthesis preserves unfinished obligations~\cite{FinkbeinerEtAl2022LiveSynthesis}. RS builds on scopes~\cite{Dwyer1999Patterns} and residual rewriting~\cite{RosuHavelund2005Rewriting}.

State attraction bounds startup steps under weak invariance~\cite{NooruldeenSchmidt2015}. Dynamic-feature~\cite{ThuijsmanReniers2024DynamicFeature}, compositional~\cite{MohajeraniMalikFabian2014Compositional} and modular~\cite{WonhamRamadge1988Modular} control address specification structure. OTF-DCS requires marked extensions~\cite[Sec.~3]{CiolekEtAl2023OTFDCS}; FG requires finite quiescent handover on every maximal run. Their different objectives preclude direct performance attribution.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
Perpetual UC activity can prevent completion. Decoding, history completeness and A1--A4 remain unvalidated; resynthesis/repair are excluded. Witnesses assume finite startup, reporting, recovery and election; prevalence is unestablished. Only 39/126 PC2 entries have a safe full prefix for the selected new requirement.

Shared authorship, AI and code risk correlated faults; independent parsing covers restricted FSP. Direct-Full is in-house; Legacy's objective differs. Pruning isolation is incomplete; external/compositional/symbolic solvers and randomized orders are untested. Ratios omit unresolved costs; extended trials lack medians. Sampling may miss peaks; RSS reveals neither heap occupancy nor bottlenecks. S4 retains interruptions.

Null/LOSS, faster Update-first, lower Eager/Update-first memory and Travel/PC limits bound gains; PC2's merged timeout is unresolved. Fixtures/repeats are not independent applications; timing uses one machine. Inherited inputs lack precedence; only Railcab transfers branch. Exponential separation assumes the default order.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
\label{sec:conclusion}
FG-DUCS compiles local contracts into safe, completing updates that preserve continuations of selected endpoints. Guarantees depend on declared requirement meanings and execution premises.
