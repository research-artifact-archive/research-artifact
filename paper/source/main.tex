\title[Compiling Local Update Contracts]{Compiling Local Update Contracts into Controller-Preserving Policies}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Updating a controlled system can require components to change at different times while old and new requirements overlap or exclude one another. Choosing an update sequence is insufficient when its steps have multiple possible outcomes. We present Fine-Grained Dynamic Update Controller Synthesis (FG-DUCS), which compiles local state transfers, individual requirement lifetimes and monitor initialization into a policy between supplied verified controllers. The policy must safely finish from every admitted starting state and for every possible outcome. We prove that the generated game implements the local contract and preserves the chosen new controller's behavior after handover. A sound and complete lazy procedure returns a policy with a progress certificate or proves that none exists. Complete examples show how merging components or requirement boundaries can prevent completion; a reverse example shows that merging can also help. An MTSA implementation decides \LazyDecisions{}/\ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts within 1,200~s, compared with \FixedEagerDecisions{} for eager successors and \DirectFullDecisions{} for full construction. Five constructed families exhibit granularity-dependent feasibility; inherited inputs show no feasibility change. Commands and handover require uncontrollable quiescence, and the contract guarantees finite completion without fairness, rather than service continuity or elapsed-time bounds.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Verified endpoints leave their intervening update unspecified~\cite{Cheng2009SASRoadmap,Andersson2013SEAMSProcesses,Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate,Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates}. Consider replacing two stations while retaining exactly one workpiece. Each replacement needs its station empty. Replacing both together is impossible, but moving the workpiece between separate replacements can make the update possible. A second choice concerns requirements: audit coverage may require the new audit rule before the old rule ends, whereas mutually exclusive access roles require the old role to end first. One global start/stop order cannot satisfy both. These are different reasons to expose update granularity.

An update may begin in any reachable old state, with environment-chosen events and transfer outcomes. Initial memory also matters: starting a monitor must not clear an existing inspection duty. A safe policy may inspect forever. The required result must cover every admitted entry and outcome, preserve declared obligations and force finite completion.

\emph{Our result compiles local update contracts into controller-preserving policies.} Components, transfers, requirement lifetimes and initializers generate the mixed-state game between verified endpoints. Neither that graph nor an update sequence is supplied. Requirement intent and transfer code remain inputs; modeling effort is unmeasured.

DUCS~\cite{Nahabedian2016SEAMS} generates update and subsequent control from global transfers and transition requirements~\cite[Defs.~4.1--4.3]{Nahabedian2020}; GR(1) updating constructs its new controller and bridge independently and runs the new controller afterward~\cite[Def.~3.1, Sec.~4]{Amram2022GR1DynamicUpdate}. Reconnection to subsequent control is therefore already established. FG-DUCS adds local transfers, independent boundaries and initializers to supplied endpoints and targets. Sections~\ref{sec:ducs-cell-construction} and~\ref{sec:related} compare generation responsibilities and OTF implementations.

\par\noindent\textbf{C1: Contract compilation.} A local input semantics with individual requirement boundaries, its generated game, and proofs of correspondence and endpoint-continuation preservation, with an initialization criterion for pure invariants (Sections~\ref{sec:formalization} and~\ref{sec:monitor-meaning}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C2: Certifying synthesis.} A sound and complete specialized lazy procedure with checkable WIN/LOSS certificates (Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C3: Granularity boundaries.} Complete component and requirement examples, sufficient merging obstructions and a reverse separation; Rolling supplies an exact batch bound (Sections~\ref{sec:example} and~\ref{sec:rq2}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C4: Implementation evidence.} An MTSA tool evaluated on \ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts and \VThreeESixMainPairs{} constructed comparisons, retaining all adverse outcomes (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}).

\subsection{Cell: Complete Local Input and Whole Policy}
Consider a constructed two-station cell with exactly one workpiece~\cite{Lewerentz1995ProductionCell,Nahabedian2020}. Both transfers require an empty station. $A$ keeps its behavior; new $B$ requires inspection before departure, while the old rule forbids inspection. Ordinary events move or inspect the workpiece. New-start before old-stop prevents an unprotected gap; a pending inspection waits for old-stop. Inspection can repeat safely forever.

Table~\ref{tab:cell-finite-main} specifies every component, endpoint and monitor. The interval requires exactly one workpiece, which plant transitions preserve. All labels are controllable; new-start before old-stop is the only dependency. Transfers are $g_A=g_B=\{(e,e)\}$, where $e$ means empty.

Endpoint tuples list $A,B$, controller and its requirement monitor. The old initial tuple is $x_A=(h,e,c_o,k)$ and its other reachable tuple is $x_B=(e,h,c_o,k)$; both are entries. The new initial tuple is $z_A=(h,e,c_0,c)$; its other reachable tuples are $y_B=(e,h,c_1,d)$ and $z_B=(e,h,c_0,c)$. Only $\{z_A,z_B\}$ are loadable targets: inspection must be clear at handover. \subsection{Policy: Scheduling Requirement Lifetimes}
Policy(2) separates requirement boundaries. Four bits record active old/new audit rules $a_o,a_n$ and roles $r_o,r_n$. The interval invariant $(a_o\lor a_n)\land\neg(r_o\land r_n)$ forces new-audit start before old-audit stop, but old-role stop before new-role start. No order is supplied. The plant has one state in each version and all ordinary self-loops; transfer is identity. Each endpoint alternates its version-specific log and service, with its own access event enabled in either state. Its audit monitor rejects service without a log, and its role monitor rejects the other version's access. New audit starts unlogged and new role starts safe. The old endpoint supplies logged and unlogged entries; one service event first clears a logged entry. A whole policy then executes
\[
\mathsf{start}_A,\ \mathsf{stop}_A,\ \mathsf{stop}_R,\ \rho,\ \mathsf{start}_R,
\]
where $A$ denotes audit, $R$ role and $\rho$ an identity component transfer. Ranks six and five decrease to the compatible new endpoint; other controllables are disabled.

If both starts are merged and both stops are merged, the first start overlaps roles and the first stop removes audit coverage. Ordinary events and transfer cannot change these active bits, so neither entry has a completing merged policy. Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:policy_interpretation} supplies every plant, controller, monitor, initializer and target, plus the whole policy and losing closure. This isolates boundary granularity; it does not assert greater expressiveness than DUCS or service continuity.

\section{Synthesizing and Checking an Update Policy}
Lazy explores products on demand using finite reachability reasoning~\cite{Cimatti1998StrongPlanning,TripakisAltisen1999,CiolekEtAl2023OTFDCS}.
\label{sec:algorithm}

A \emph{bucket} is $\Post(q,a)$; an empty bucket disables $a$. \emph{UC priority:} disabling controls at a UC-enabled state preserves winning: it removes runs and creates no deadlock because every UC outcome remains (Lemma~\ref{lem:uc-priority}). \emph{Memoryless sufficiency:} whenever a history-dependent policy wins, a state-based one wins; the proof below establishes this by attractor ranks and a history-independent losing-region response.

\subsection{Lazy Successor Generation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:otf} constructs a reachability attractor~\cite{CassezEtAl2005TimedGames}. It tracks discovered states $V$, states $X\subseteq V$ with all UC candidates queried, and proved winners $W$. States in $X$ may retain unqueried controls; $W$ may omit winners.

Expansion queries every UC candidate. If none is enabled, it queries controls through the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion; $\mathsf{Retry}$ resumes unfinished cursors. Each query retains all outcomes. Candidates include local events and precedence-enabled commands in any fixed finite order.

\emph{Promote.} A safe expanded state wins when its nonempty proving set---all UC successors, or one control bucket at quiescence---lies in $W$; its rank is one above their maximum. Reverse incidences trigger predecessor reconsideration. Failure requires exhausted candidates and a promotion fixed point. Once all entries win, extraction selects one lower-rank control bucket at each reached quiescent non-goal, none elsewhere; it need not follow promotion or minimize length.

\caption{Lazy}

\footnotesize
\KwIn{Typed update contract (Section~\ref{sec:formalization})}
\KwOut{$(\sigma,\rank,\kappa)$ or $(\textnormal{no solution},L)$}

$\mathsf{Open}\leftarrow\{q\in Q_0\mid\Safe(q)\land q\notin\Goal\}$; set $\rank(q)\leftarrow0$ for $q\in W$\;
\While{true}{
  Add queued goals to $W$ once with rank zero; exhaust \emph{Promote}, retaining witnesses/ranks, removing winners from Retry and propagating predecessors\;
  \lIf{$Q_0\subseteq W$}{\Return $(\sigma,\rank|_{\Reach_\sigma(Q_0)},\kappa)$ constructed from the goal witnesses and retained lower-rank choices}
  \eIf{$\mathsf{Open}\neq\emptyset$}{
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Open}$, add it to $X$, and query all uncontrollable candidates\;
    Close the control cursor if UC-enabled; otherwise initialize it and query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion\;
    Insert $q$ into $\mathsf{Retry}$ iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }{
    \lIf{$\mathsf{Retry}=\emptyset$}{\Return $(\textnormal{no solution},L\leftarrow V\setminus W)$}
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Retry}$; query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion; reinsert iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }
  Add every queried successor to $V$; queue newly discovered goals for rank zero and safe non-goals for $\mathsf{Open}$ (unsafe states stay outside $W$ and $\mathsf{Open}$)\;
}
\end{algorithm}

The execution connection has four distinct premises (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:execution_proof}):
\par\noindent\textbf{A1:} endpoint observations preserve component/monitor states and same-labeled endpoint edges.

\subsection{RQ1: Can Local Choices Change Feasibility?}
\label{sec:claim-granularity}
\label{sec:rq2}
Cell and Policy isolate component and requirement-lifetime obstructions (Section~\ref{sec:example}). These new cases exercise choices absent from the inherited DUCS-oriented benchmarks. We test whether merging those choices, with the remaining contract fixed, removes all completing policies; this is a mechanism question, not a frequency estimate over deployments.

\paragraph{What is merged.}
Partition transfers, old stops and new starts separately into blocks. A transfer block $B\subseteq I$ simultaneously permits all targets in $\prod_{i\in B}g_i(s_i)$; a boundary block removes or initializes its monitors at the same component tuple. Other active monitors compose member-label effects, checked to commute pairwise on every monitor/residual state. Mapped precedence must remain irreflexive and acyclic. Ordinary behavior, entries, endpoints, relations, initializers and targets stay fixed; quiescence and all member guards apply. These are comparison contracts, not DUCS encodings.

The following obstruction explains the Cell, Policy and PC2 merges directly; the rolling threshold then gives an exact quantitative boundary.
\input{technical_fragments/granularity_obstruction.tex}

\paragraph{Mechanisms and scope.}
Readiness/rejoining gates motivate staged replacement~\cite{e6_kubernetes_deployments,e6_mongodb_enterprise_replica_set}. Rolling begins with $n$ ready replicas and no spares; its interval requirement keeps at least $m$ ready. Each transfer boots its replicas; each has one UC readiness event and no waiting loop. Service self-loops are controllable. The target is all-new and ready.

\begin{proposition}[Rolling threshold]\label{prop:rolling-threshold}
For $n\ge2$, $1\le m<n$ and $1\le k\le n$, fixed consecutive batches of size $k$ (with a smaller final remainder) admit safe finite completion iff $k\le n-m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Some mandatory batch has size $k$. Its transfer leaves at most $n-k$ ready replicas, so $k>n-m$ violates the interval. Otherwise, transfer one batch only when all replicas are ready, disable service, and retain every UC readiness outcome before the next transfer. Readiness never falls below $n-k\ge m$. With $u$ old and $b$ booting replicas, $2u+b$ strictly decreases on every selected transfer and every UC event, reaching zero exactly at the target. Thus all maximal runs finish without fairness.
\end{proof}

The 15 Rolling separations compare $k=1$ with $k=n$; the \VThreeESixRollingThresholdCells{} threshold cells are separate. Canary adds health reports/recovery, DB-Rolling role-sensitive replacement, and Rolling+Audit overlapping duties. Policy isolates opposing boundaries; Threads supplies both-WIN/both-LOSS quiescence controls. Of the 33 separations, 30 are parameter points in Rolling and Canary; they are not 33 independent mechanisms. Table~\ref{tab:rq2-family-counts} keeps all outcomes. Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:constructed_contracts} specifies mechanisms and assumption controls; their deployed prevalence is unestablished.

\textbf{Answer.} The suite yields \VThreeESixWitness{}/\VThreeESixMainPairs{} fine-WIN/merged-LOSS comparisons across five families. PC2 contributes no solver WIN/LOSS pair because its merged run times out; the source argument below independently proves merged impossibility.

\paragraph{Why finer is not always better.}
Two components with one state per version share UC labels $x,y$. The only UC loops are $y$ in old~1, $x$ in new~1, $x$ in old~2 and $y$ in new~2; shared labels synchronize. Thus all-old and all-new are quiescent, but either mixed product has a UC loop. With identity transfers and no requirements, either first local transfer prevents quiescence forever, so fine loses. A joint transfer skips the mixed state and wins at rank one. A shared controllable loop makes both endpoints deadlock-free. Atomicity can therefore remove either a necessary or a harmful intermediate state; there is no general refinement order (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:granularity-reverse}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\label{sec:expressiveness}
Zhang and Cheng formalize temporal adaptation semantics~\cite{ZhangCheng2006ALTL}; scenario-based synthesis uses history-based updatability~\cite{Ghezzi2012DynamicScenarioUpdate,PanzicaLaManna2013CorrectnessDynamicUpdates}. Local updating~\cite{PanzicaLaManna2012LocalUpdate}, PROTEOS~\cite[Secs.~4.3--4.6]{Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate}, workflow updating~\cite[Secs.~4.3, 5.2]{Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates}, Kitsune~\cite[Sec.~2]{e6_hayden2014_kitsune} and network synthesis~\cite{McClurgEtAl2015NetworkUpdates} address replacement, migration or transition safety. Live Synthesis carries unfinished obligations~\cite{FinkbeinerEtAl2022LiveSynthesis}; our initialization reasoning uses scopes~\cite{Dwyer1999Patterns} and residual rewriting~\cite{RosuHavelund2005Rewriting}. State attraction bounds startup steps under weak invariance~\cite{NooruldeenSchmidt2015}; compositional/modular control addresses specification structure~\cite{MohajeraniMalikFabian2014Compositional,WonhamRamadge1988Modular}. OTF-DCS requires marked extensions~\cite[Sec.~3]{CiolekEtAl2023OTFDCS}, whereas FG requires every maximal update run to reach handover.

Ma et al. preserve transaction version consistency using dynamic dependencies and a sufficient freeness condition, with concurrent versions and selective blocking~\cite[Secs.~4.1--4.3]{MaEtAl2011VersionConsistency}. Their transaction quiescence differs from FG's UC quiescence; FG's finite handover contract does not establish transaction consistency.

O-DUCS separates update and post-update analysis~\cite[Sec.~3]{HiranoEtAl2024ODUCS}. Our fork's plain OTF route precomputes a new controller and reconnects matching environment/safety states~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact}; identity with the 2024 release is unestablished. FG combines this existing reconnection with supplied endpoints/targets, local transfers, individual lifetimes and initializers.

Thuijsman and Reniers~\cite[Secs.~4--7]{ThuijsmanReniers2024DynamicFeature} support individual/simultaneous reconfiguration, feature-dependent requirements and state retention/reset. Their nonblocking supervisor permits a marked continuation from every reachable state. FG instead requires every maximal update run to finish at a supplied controller, with independent lifetimes and declared initialization.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
Permanently enabled UC arrivals or polling block even local commands. Threads contrasts finite UC phases with perpetual activity. Fairness-dependent waiting is outside the contract; inherited-adaptation effects on feasibility are not isolated. Decoding, history completeness and A1--A4 remain unvalidated; repair is excluded. Constructed cases assume finite startup, reporting, recovery and election; selection may favor separations, and deployed prevalence is unestablished.

Shared authorship, AI and code risk correlated faults; independent parsing covers restricted FSP. Direct-Full is in-house; Legacy's objective differs. Pruning isolation, external solvers and randomized orders remain untested. Ratios omit unresolved costs; extended trials lack medians. Sampling can miss peaks; RSS identifies neither heap occupancy nor bottlenecks. S4 retains interruptions.

Null/LOSS results, faster Update-first, lower Eager/Update-first memory and Travel/PC limits bound gains. PC2's merged timeout remains unresolved. Fixtures/repeats are not independent applications; timing uses one machine. Inherited inputs lack precedence; only Railcab transfers branch. Exponential separation assumes the default order.
