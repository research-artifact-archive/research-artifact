# Root-checked local/shared contract proof

Let A_c(B)=max_{T subset J, sum_(i in T)c_i<=B}sum_(i in T)w_i, allowing zero activation costs. Programs must terminate and obey local caps r_i and shared cap s in every finite-write environment. Identities never recur; completed outputs persist; no guard survives a counted call; kernels are whole and every comparison may be preceded by a fresh own-job write. A new preparation is taken after every rejection.

First consider local caps alone. To force two-mode protection of a target i, reject its cheap calls up to r_i times. A subsequent cheap call at that cap could be rejected in another finite environment and is therefore universally inadmissible. Early fresh completion already protects i. At most r_i actual writes suffice. With cached completion, one extra write before its eventual cached comparison suffices, so at most r_i+1 writes force protection. Targeting a maximizing subset gives the two activation-knapsack lower bounds for all causal programs, including retained preparations and scheduling among ready jobs. At r_i=0, two-mode activation costs zero; three-mode activation costs one.

For the matching local-threshold policy, every protected job has r_i rejected calls; in three modes its fresh final preparation additionally needs a mismatching write. These intervals are disjoint, so the total subset activation cost cannot exceed B. Every feasible subset is attained by the target construction in any topological execution. Thus the values are A_r(B) and A_(r+1)(B), simultaneously for all B.

Adding the shared cap s cannot lower either local lower bound, nor the whole-kernel shared-only lower bounds Omega*1[B>=s] and Omega_(B-s)+. Consider the canonical policy switching when either allowance is exhausted. A run with fewer than s cheap rejections satisfies the local activation bound for all its protected jobs. A run with exactly s rejections pays at most Omega in two modes; in three modes all protected jobs require an additional cached-mismatch write, even if they completed before the global threshold, hence at most B-s jobs can be protected. These are the two cases of the claimed maximum. This policy's Q is n plus its cheap failures. Any min(B,s) failures can be distributed across local allowances since s<=sum r_i, giving n+min(B,s), not necessarily on a path maximizing L.

For known B under only local rejected-call caps, once B failures have occurred all later fresh cheap calls must match. Thus in two modes only jobs whose local caps were reached before the Bth failure need protected completion. Their total local activation cost is at most B-1 (and there are none at B=0). Conversely, a target subset costing at most B-1 can be forced protectively: at every exhausted local cap at least one write could still be issued, ruling out another cheap call under the supplied B. The value is A_r(B-1). In three modes the same local-target argument with extra cached-mismatch writes gives A_(r+1)(B); known B does not improve it. A mechanically mandatory fallback threshold, rather than a cap on rejected calls, is a different known-budget contract.

Uniform r>=1 gives local curves Omega_floor(B/r) and Omega_floor(B/(r+1)), with universal Q<=n+nr. At equal total allowance R, shared-only restrictions are weaker than reserving local allowances, so the optimal shared curves are weakly smaller. This is a contract comparison, not evidence that applications require a batch cap or that protected work predicts elapsed time. A one-job r=1,B=1 example checks freshness: fresh cached completion protects zero work after its one cheap failure, while reuse of the rejected candidate would protect w and violate the formula.
