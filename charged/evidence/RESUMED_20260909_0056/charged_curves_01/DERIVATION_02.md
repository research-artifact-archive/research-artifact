# Proposed three-mode curve derivation (author proof draft)

The full primitive-to-three-mode theorem remains unproved. Here only the explicitly defined three-mode scalar game is considered.

Let m=min(v,g+r), a=c=w+m, delta=g+r-m>=0, P=p+delta>=0, s=w+p>0. The three residual options are P+F(child,b), max(F(child,b),c+F(S,b-1)), and max(delta+F(child,b),delta+s+F(child,b-1)). At zero budget F(S,0)=0 because the cheapest validation route attains the per-job baseline. The third option at zero budget is delta+F(child,0).

Omitting jobs from a simulated policy costs no more, since every omitted job can take its normal completion and all residual success charges are nonnegative. Therefore subset monotonicity still holds. The published cheapest-fast proof uses only subset monotonicity and availability of fast actions; both remain, so a minimum-c fast job suffices.

If f is concave with nonnegative decreasing marginals, max(f_b,s+f_(b-1)) inserts a single marginal s into the decreasing marginal list: before the last marginal at least s, retain f; afterwards use the shifted f plus s. Thus the cached bound is concave/nondecreasing. The minimum h of protected and cached bounds has those properties; g<=F<=h by subset monotonicity and feasible completion actions. The inherited capped-recurrence lemma gives F=min(h,floor_c(g)). Induction over residual sets supplies concavity and the compact construction. Protected completion gives eventual saturation sum P.

Candidate supporting-basis extension: suppose f(b)=min_{d in D}(d*b+q_d) on nonnegative integers. For positive s, define q_s=max_b(f(b)-s*b). The insertion I_s f is represented by lines (d,q_d+max(0,s-d)) and the added line (s,q_s). Before the insertion contact, active slopes are at least s; after the contact+1, active slopes are below s and their intercepts shift by s-d. The new slope-s line handles the two contact positions, including a nonintegral crossing of prior lines. This needs a complete discrete proof and tests before claiming a bound. Slope-floor similarly retains lines with d>c and adds (c,max_b(f(b)-c*b)). Min/vertical shifts preserve the finite slope set. Thus a prospective basis uses only0 and {w_i+m_i,w_i+p_i}, rather than original costs alone. A conservative positive-run bound is twice the number of distinct positive basis slopes; tightness is not claimed.

No primary-source priority check for the insertion extension has yet been completed. Inserting one marginal is inherited elementary concave-sequence/max-plus convolution structure; any new contribution must concern the charged primitive normal form, composition closure, representation/complexity and checkable synthesis for this particular contract, not claim the insertion operation itself as new.


## Discrete supporting-basis insertion proof refinement (before basis implementation test)
Split the old lines by d>=s and d<s. Write H_b=min_{d>=s}(d*b+q_d), L_b=min_{d<s}(d*b+q_d), using infinity for an empty class, and let h_b=H_b-s*b, l_b=L_b-s*b. Then h is nondecreasing, l is nonincreasing, and f_b-s*b=min(h_b,l_b) is unimodal. Let q_s be its integer maximum. For b>=1, the maximum of its two adjacent values equals min(q_s,h_b,l_(b-1)): outside the maximum crossing interval the corresponding monotone branch attains; across the crossing the integer maximum is attained at one of those two adjacent indices. Expanding this equality gives exactly min(s*b+q_s, min_{d>=s}(d*b+q_d), min_{d<s}(d*b+q_d+s-d)). At b=0 the added contact line or an unchanged d>=s line attains zero. This proves the proposed insertion basis for a bounded nonnegative f with f_0=0. Contact q_s is obtained at an encoded profile knot, without traversing multiplicities.

Slopes remain in0 union{w_i+m_i,w_i+p_i}. Intercepts generated by shifts/insertion are bounded above by a sum of input premiums, deltas and kernel works; contacts are bounded by the child saturation cap. Their bit lengths are polynomial in binary input size. The published elementary integer-envelope argument therefore gives at most twice the number of distinct positive basis slopes (at most4n) positive marginal runs per state. This is a conservative bound; tightness is not established.
