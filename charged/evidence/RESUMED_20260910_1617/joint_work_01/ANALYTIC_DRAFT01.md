# Joint work at a strict completion-call cap: analytic draft

Exploratory derivation written before finite corroboration. Not yet a manuscript claim. Preserve errors and successors. Same whole-kernel interface as fixed163 Section2, deterministic causal programs, positive works, permanent completions, opaque preparations/retention, free inspections, and normal progress. Here r=0, so every counted call must complete one job. Write Omega for total work and Top_k for the sum of the k largest works (clipped to n). All costs are kernel work, not measured latency.

## A. Unaware, universally protected-work-optimal policies

Let V be the programs with Q<=n for every finite-write environment and L(P,E)<=Top_min(b,n) for every environment with at most b writes, simultaneously for all b. Then conjectured exact minimum worst W at B is Omega+Top_min(B,n), attained simultaneously by the fresh-prepare/all-cached policy on every DAG.

Lower-bound draft. Fix k=min(B,n) heaviest targets. An adversary writes a fresh target identity immediately before each target comparison, with an unconditional cap of k writes; it never writes before fresh completion. Admissibility forbids a failed cheap call. Before the cap, every target comparison is therefore a cached mismatch. All targeted jobs must eventually complete. If a target completes fresh, fewer than k target comparisons remain, so at most k-1 writes suffice to protect all k targets. This violates the program's required L<=Top_(k-1), since all works are positive. Therefore all k targets complete through cached mismatch, each requiring both a whole preparation and whole protected execution. Every other job still requires one whole kernel, giving W>=Omega+Top_k. Retention/free inspection cannot provide a fresh matching record after the adversary's write. Extra preparations only increase W. At k=0 the mandatory Omega lower bound suffices.

## B. Known B: independent jobs, preserving the optimal L cap

For a supplied integer B, restrict to programs completing with Q<=n and L<=Top_min(B,n) against every environment of at most B writes. For independent jobs, conjectured minimum worst W is Omega+Top_(B+1)-w_max for 0<=B<n, and Omega for B>=n.

Attaining policy for 0<B<n: process jobs in nondecreasing work order, counting d observed cached mismatches. If the number of remaining jobs is at most B-d, complete all of them fresh. If B-d=0, freshly prepare and cheaply publish every remaining job; disjoint mismatches already require B writes, so no further write is possible. Otherwise prepare and complete the next smallest job with cached completion. Every protected job is either one of d mismatches or one of at most B-d final fresh jobs, so L<=Top_B. The largest job is never cached: with one job left the policy is in the fresh or the known-clean case. At most B distinct nonlargest jobs have duplicate kernels, so W<=Omega+Top_(B+1)-w_max. B=0 uses cheap completion; B>=n uses all-fresh.

Lower-bound draft for B<n: target the B+1 heaviest jobs and write at their comparisons, stopping unconditionally after B writes. If all targets completed protected, L would exceed Top_B. Thus at least one target must complete without protection. Under this adversary a target comparison can match only after all B writes have been consumed. Q=n forbids cheap failures; the first B target comparisons must therefore be cached mismatches on distinct completed targets. These B jobs duplicate at least the B lightest works among the B+1 targets, namely Top_(B+1)-w_max. A target fresh completion before exhaustion would leave too few target comparisons to exhaust B and still allow an unprotected target; it cannot evade the argument. B=0 and B>=n follow from mandatory Omega and the obvious policies.

This compares different admissible classes at different B: the L cap itself grows with B, so this constrained informed W value need not be monotone. In particular it can drop to Omega at B=n. The universal program's W curve remains monotone. The information price is w_max-w_(B+1) for B<n, and Omega for B>=n, where works are sorted nonincreasingly. Do not confuse this joint resource comparison with the fixed163 L-only equality.

## C. Known B=1: complete W/L frontier on a DAG

Fix W cap Omega+M, M>=0. Set processing p_i=w_i for w_i>M and p_i=0 otherwise. Set tail tau_i=0 for w_i>M and tau_i=w_i otherwise. For a topological job order pi, define C_i as cumulative p through i. The conjectured exact minimum worst L is min_pi max_i(C_i+tau_i), over all topological orders. Sweep M in {0,w_1,...,w_n} and discard dominated pairs to obtain the whole frontier.

Upper bound: before any mismatch, run jobs with w_i>M fresh and jobs with w_i<=M prepared/cached in order pi. After the first mismatch, complete all remaining jobs with fresh preparation and cheap publication: that mismatch has consumed the sole possible external write. On the no-write trace, protected work is the total of the heavy jobs. On a trace whose first mismatch is a light job i, protected work is the prior heavy work plus w_i; no later work is protected. Thus worst L=max_i(C_i+tau_i), including the all-heavy total. Worst W<=Omega+M because only one cached kernel can be duplicated.

Lower-bound draft for arbitrary programs: follow the no-write trace, which must terminate. A cheap call would admit a one-write failure violating Q=n, so its completing calls are fresh or cached. Any cached job i admits an immediate-before-comparison write requiring total W>=Omega+w_i. Thus w_i>M cannot be cached. The no-write trace gives L>=sum of its fresh works; a one-write continuation at each cached job gives L>=prior fresh work+w_i. These bounds cover actual records and observations and require no assumed observation-free normal form. Converting optional fresh choices with w_i<=M into cached choices weakly decreases the bound: removing their work from all later fresh prefixes cannot increase a later term, and their own new tail term is at most the original total fresh work. The resulting profile has exactly p/tau above. Therefore every arbitrary program with the W cap has L at least the best such schedule. Verify this exchange carefully against original fresh/cached classifications and positive weights.

The scheduling objective is the classical precedence-constrained maximum-lateness/tail objective. A backward algorithm repeatedly places a sink with minimum tau last. Exchange proof: move the selected sink j to the end of any optimal remaining order; intervening completions only decrease, and j's new T+tau_j is no larger than the old last job k's T+tau_k, because k was also an available sink and tau_j<=tau_k. Repeat. This is an application of Lawler's scheduling principle, not a new scheduling algorithm; verify and cite its primary paper before adopting.

Consequences to check: for a unique maximum-work sink, B=1 and the exact L cap w_max permit W=Omega+w_second; otherwise the cap needs W=Omega+w_max (ties included; w_second=0 for a single job). Chain8->16 gives (W,L)=(32,16), versus the universal L-optimal class's (40,16). Reversing the chain removes that saving. This is a resource-information effect, not a claim of runtime or operational importance.

## Verification and nonclaims

Need independent reasoning about the full-program lower bounds, positive/tie/end cases, and a separate finite tree/frontier oracle for small inputs. Do not call finite normalized-mode enumeration complete for the unrestricted programming language. Do not claim r>0, arbitrary native overheads, generalized locks, randomized policies or new scheduling theory. The historical r>0 W-optimality counterexamples remain untouched. No new run, publication, or theorem adoption is implied by this draft.
