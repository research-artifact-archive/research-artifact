\section{Endpoint Interfaces and Certificate Reuse}\label{ta:endpoint-interface}
Let $T$ project loadable targets onto component and new-monitor states, omitting controller state. The update-game construction uses endpoint controllers only through entries $Q_0$ and $T$.
\begin{theorem}[Endpoint-interface reuse]\label{thm:endpoint-interface-extended}
Keep components, transfers, monitors, initializers, event control and precedence fixed. Eligible contracts with equal entries and target projections have identical update games. Every WIN policy/rank and LOSS certificate transfers between them; selecting a compatible target of the second contract preserves that endpoint's continuations. A winning policy also transfers to fewer entries and more projected targets, stopping at newly admitted goals.
\end{theorem}
The following proof establishes each clause. This interface result does not claim an implemented cache or a measured reuse speedup.
Let $K$ and $K'$ satisfy Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-interface-extended}. Their local data define the same finite ambient state space of version-tagged component tuples, monitor memories and pending-command sets. Let $I,I'$ be their entry sets, and let $T,T'$ be their target projections. A target projection retains all component and new-monitor coordinates and removes only the endpoint-controller coordinate. All targets retain their eligibility premises, including reachability and endpoint safety, deadlock freedom and preservation of uncontrollable events.

\paragraph{Identical interfaces.}
Assume $I=I'$ and $T=T'$. On every ambient state, enabled ordinary events and their complete outcome buckets are equal because the component models and controllability agree. Transfer guards and outcomes agree because the transfer relations, pending commands and precedence agree. Boundary guards and memory updates agree because monitors and initializers agree. UC quiescence and safety therefore agree as well. Induction on path length from $I$ gives equal reachable state sets and label/outcome transitions. No rule inspects either endpoint-controller state after entry. Old states collapsed by $\Init$ may have different controller coordinates, but such coordinates impose no source restriction once old control is released.

A reached state is a goal precisely when it is safe, UC-quiescent, has no pending command, and its component/new-monitor projection belongs to $T$. Hence goals agree. The identity map preserves the policy successor relation, safety, nonblocking, all maximal paths and every strict rank inequality. It also preserves the losing-certificate obligations: goals are absent from $L$, a losing entry remains in $L$, and at each safe member the same UC escape witness or all-control counter-outcomes remain in $L$. Thus WIN and LOSS certificates transfer, including their universal quantification over all enabled outcomes.

For every reached goal $q$, membership of its projection in $T'$ supplies at least one full target $z'\in Z'_{\mathrm{load}}$. Choose one and define $\kappa'(q)=z'$. The selected endpoint controller is eligible and the handover copies precisely its component, monitor and controller coordinates. Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-continuation} yields an isomorphism to that endpoint rooted at $z'$. This preserves that endpoint's complete continuation language; it does not claim equivalence between the two endpoint controllers' continuation languages. Nondeterministic local outcomes are unchanged and remain adversarial. Several targets with one projection cause no difficulty: selecting a compatible controller state is part of handover, not selection among an event's nondeterministic outcomes.

\paragraph{A Cell replacement with different continuation behavior.}
Delete only $c_0\xrightarrow{j}c_0$ from the new controller in Table~\ref{tab:cell-finite-main}. Its alphabet still contains $j$ through $c_1\xrightarrow{j}c_0$. The reachable endpoint tuples remain exactly $z_A=(h,e,c_0,c)$, $y_B=(e,h,c_1,d)$ and $z_B=(e,h,c_0,c)$: the cycle $z_A\xrightarrow{m_B}y_B\xrightarrow{j}z_B\xrightarrow{m_A}z_A$ remains, and the deleted edge removes only the inspection self-loop at $z_B$. This closed loop remains safe and deadlock-free; all events are controllable. Keeping $Z_{\mathrm{load}}=\{z_A,z_B\}$ preserves its target projection, and the old endpoint is unchanged. Thus Figure~\ref{fig:cell-story}'s whole update certificate and its handover choices transfer. The endpoint languages differ: from $z_B$, the original admits $j^\omega$, whereas the replacement's first event must be $m_A$. Reuse therefore does not require continuation-language equivalence between endpoints. This finite construction adds no solver measurement.

\paragraph{Smaller entry interface and larger target interface.}
Assume instead $I'\subseteq I$ and $T\subseteq T'$, and let $(\sigma,\rank,\kappa)$ win for $K$. Starting from $I'$, use $\sigma$ until the first goal of $K'$ and stop there. Every resulting nonterminal prefix is also a prefix of the original policy from $I$, because all local transitions agree. Safety and nonblocking are inherited. An infinite resulting path would be an infinite original-policy path, contradicting its finite completion. Every original goal has its projection in $T'$ and is also a goal when reached from $I'$, so a maximal resulting path cannot pass an old goal without stopping. A new goal may be reached earlier, which only truncates a path.

Restrict $\rank$ to this truncated closure and assign zero at its goals. Edges between remaining non-goals keep their original strict inequalities. An edge entering a goal has a positive-rank non-goal source and zero-rank target, so also decreases. Choose a compatible target in $Z'_{\mathrm{load}}$ at each such goal. This supplies a complete WIN certificate for $K'$. The converse is not claimed: fewer entries or additional targets can make a formerly losing contract win. Nor does the theorem preserve the particular policy returned by a new solver run, which may use a different endpoint-dependent exploration order.

\subsection{Exactly When a Fixed Returned Policy Can Be Reused}
\label{ta:certificate-interface}
The following result is relative to an already returned WIN certificate; it does not require equal or nested target interfaces.
\begin{theorem}[Policy reuse]\label{thm:endpoint-interface}\label{prop:certificate-interface}
For a returned WIN certificate $(\sigma,\rank,\kappa)$, write $D=\Reach_\sigma(Q_0)$ and $G_D=D\cap\Goal$, and extend $\sigma$ by the empty set on $G_D$. Fix components, transfers, monitors, initializers, event control and precedence. For another eligible endpoint pair with entries $Q'_0\subseteq D$ and goals $G'$, let $D'$ be the closure under the extended policy, stopping at $G'$. That policy wins exactly when $D'\cap G_D\subseteq G'$.
\end{theorem}
Here is the complete construction and proof.

Let $K$ have certificate $(\sigma,\rank,\kappa)$, finite support $D=\Reach_\sigma(Q_0)$ and terminal set $G_D=D\cap\Goal$. Define $\bar\sigma$ on $D$ by retaining $\sigma$ off $G_D$ and choosing no controllable at $G_D$. The latter states have no UC event by the original goal condition, so they have no successor under $\bar\sigma$. Every state of $D$ is safe. Each original nonterminal has at least one successor, retains every UC and every selected-label outcome within $D$, and has strictly larger rank than each of those successors.

Let $K'$ be eligible with the same components, transfers, monitors, initializers, controllability and precedence, but possibly different endpoint controllers and loadable targets. Assume $Q'_0\subseteq D$. After admission the successor rules inspect only the fixed local data, so they agree on $D$ in both contracts. Evaluate the new goal test $G'$ on these states using the new projected targets. Starting from $Q'_0$, form the least closure $D'$ under $\bar\sigma$, expanding only states outside $G'$. Closure of the old certificate gives $D'\subseteq D$. In particular, constructing $D'$ requires only the stored policy successor graph and the new entry/goal information; it does not explore unselected update actions. Obtaining and validating that endpoint information remains a separate task.

Suppose $D'\cap G_D\subseteq G'$. At a reached non-goal, the state cannot be in $G_D$, so it retains the original nonempty successor set, with all uncontrollables and selected-event outcomes. Every such successor is in $D'$. Safety is inherited from $D$. Set $\rank'(q)=0$ on $D'\cap G'$ and $\rank'(q)=\rank(q)$ elsewhere. An original nonterminal has positive rank: otherwise its nonempty successor set would require a negative natural-number rank. Thus every edge entering a new goal strictly decreases, and every remaining edge retains its original strict inequality. No infinite non-goal run exists, and no finite maximal run can end at a non-goal. The policy therefore wins from every new entry. For each reached new goal, choose any compatible full target in $Z'_{\mathrm{load}}$ to define $\kappa'$. Its existence follows from the new goal test, and endpoint eligibility plus Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-continuation} preserves that endpoint's own continuation language.

Conversely suppose $q\in D'\cap G_D$ but $q\notin G'$. By the definition of $D'$, a finite run from a new entry reaches $q$ without having stopped at a new goal. As an original terminal, $q$ is UC-quiescent, and $\bar\sigma(q)=\emptyset$. Hence this is a finite maximal run ending at a non-goal deadlock in $K'$, so the fixed policy is not winning. This proves both directions. It says nothing about another policy or about extending the old policy beyond its former terminals; either may solve $K'$.

\paragraph{A simple sufficient target condition.}
If the new target projection contains the projections of every member of $G_D$, every original terminal remains a new goal. The criterion then holds for any eligible $Q'_0\subseteq D$. Unlike whole-interface equality or target-set enlargement, it permits removing old loadable targets never used by the certificate. It is only sufficient: new goals can also stop every path before a removed old terminal.

\paragraph{Application to the saved PC2 certificate.}
The PC2-Rolling certificate used in Technical Appendices~\ref{ta:pc2_history_boundary} and~\ref{ta:pc2_all_act} has 126 entries, 381 states and 522 edges, with maximum rank 37. Its only all-commands-completed state is its single terminal, whose handover selects \texttt{new-00000009}. Keeping the old endpoint and narrowing the loadable set to this one reachable target leaves that same certificate valid, even if the original loadable set contained other targets. This is a direct specialization of the sufficient condition, using the already saved certificate; it is not a new synthesis or timing result. The eligibility of a different supplied new controller, and the presence of a target with the required component and all-new-monitor coordinates, must still be checked. No equivalence between different endpoint controllers' continuation languages is assumed.
