\title[Staged Updates between Verified Controllers]{Synthesizing Staged Updates between Verified Controllers}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Verified old and new controllers leave the transition unresolved: component states and active requirements must change safely. Fine-Grained Dynamic Update Controller Synthesis (FG-DUCS) takes local transfers, requirement lifetimes and initial obligations as a contract and synthesizes a staged update policy. A winning policy safely completes from every reachable old state under every outcome in finitely many events, preserving the supplied new controller's selected continuation. Commands and handover require states with no enabled uncontrollable event. Contract/game correspondence and established lazy reachability yield a policy with progress rank or a losing-region certificate. A local condition guarantees that separate transfers preserve a merged solution. Other constructed cases require one or the other granularity; merging changes no decision in 54 comparisons derived from existing models. On \ApplicationContracts{} adapted games, our Modal Transition System Analyser (MTSA) extension decides \LazyDecisions{} within 1,200~s using Lazy, versus \FixedEagerDecisions{} with Eager, \FixedUpdateFirstDecisions{} with Update-first and \DirectFullDecisions{} with Direct-Full. Update-first is sometimes faster. Continuous service and elapsed-time bounds are not guaranteed; the intended use is checking finite maintenance plans before deployment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
An update must coordinate changing component states and requirements between verified controllers~\cite{Cheng2009SASRoadmap,Andersson2013SEAMSProcesses,Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate,Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates}. It should also retain the replacement's allowed continuations, which satisfying new safety goals alone can discard (Section~\ref{sec:formalization}).

A workcell may need to move a workpiece before replacing its station. Audit coverage may need overlap; incompatible access rules forbid it. The policy must retain duties across every old state and environment outcome. A fixed sequence can fail under branching; safety alone permits endless updates.

\emph{FG-DUCS compiles local choices into a policy for safe finite handover.} The contract fixes permitted transfers, requirement lifetimes and endpoint obligations; synthesis chooses how to complete the update (Table~\ref{tab:contract-workflow}). Modelers still supply correct models and transfer implementations; synthesis checks these choices together.

Dynamic Update Controller Synthesis (DUCS)~\cite{Nahabedian2016SEAMS,Nahabedian2020} and generalized-reactivity(1), or GR(1), updating~\cite{Amram2022GR1DynamicUpdate} already generate update control. FG-DUCS fixes both controllers and generates the intervening game from local transfers, requirement boundaries and monitor starting states (Section~\ref{sec:ducs-cell-construction}). We contribute this construction and its guarantees, without claiming greater expressiveness or a new reachability algorithm.

During maintenance, update control replaces old control. Interval requirements cover gaps between old-stop and new-start. Work may pause, and commands and handover need global uncontrollable (UC) quiescence: no environment event outside the controller's control is enabled. Perpetual UC activity prevents completion; neither continuous service nor elapsed-time bounds follow. Within this scope, synthesis checks a maintenance plan before execution.

\Needspace{3\baselineskip}
Our contributions are:
\par\noindent\textbf{C1: Contract and game.} We prove local-history correspondence and endpoint preservation, and give an invariant-initialization criterion (Sections~\ref{sec:formalization} and~\ref{sec:monitor-meaning}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C2: Granularity conditions.} Obstructions and a local sufficient condition explain when separate choices are necessary or preserve merged feasibility (Sections~\ref{sec:example} and~\ref{sec:granularity-analysis}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C3: A policy with checkable evidence.} Sound and complete lazy synthesis returns a completing policy and rank or a losing-region certificate (Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C4: Implementation evidence.} Reference-game checks and \ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts test the MTSA implementation; constructed comparisons separately test granularity (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}).

Our public replication package~\cite{FGDUCSAnonymousArtifact}, prepared for this paper, includes the cited Technical Appendix A--P and Supplement S1--S5 as Parts I and II of \texttt{supplementary\_material.pdf}. Data Availability gives versioned links.

\section{Why Updates Need Separate Component and Requirement Choices}
\label{sec:example}\label{sec:mechanism-boundary}
\label{sec:cell-example}
Verified endpoints leave the intermediate configurations and changing obligations unresolved. Existing update synthesis generates control from a supplied transition model~\cite{Nahabedian2020,Amram2022GR1DynamicUpdate}. Two examples explain why that model needs separate component replacements and requirement boundaries. FG-DUCS constructs their combined game from local inputs; Section~\ref{sec:ducs-cell-construction} compares this construction with existing interfaces.

\subsection{Cell: Moving Work before Replacing a Station}
Our constructed \emph{Cell} example has two stations $A,B$ and exactly one workpiece~\cite{Lewerentz1995ProductionCell,Nahabedian2020}. A station can be replaced only while empty. The old controller moves the workpiece between stations and forbids inspection. The new controller requires inspection at $B$ before departure. Both endpoints are verified, yet simultaneous replacement is impossible: one station always holds the workpiece.

\input{figures/cell_story_visual.tex}

\paragraph{Coordinating moves and changing duties.}
Figure~\ref{fig:cell-story} shows the policy from both old states, called \emph{entries}. Each path replaces the empty station, moves the workpiece, and later replaces the other station. The input requires new-start before old-stop, preventing an unprotected gap; synthesis chooses the remaining order. If $B$ holds at new-start, inspection becomes pending; it must wait until old-stop removes the ban. Starting its monitor---the automaton tracking the new rule---clear would silently discard this duty.

The first path therefore inspects before handover; the second activates the new rule with $B$ empty and needs no inspection. Both reach an allowed new closed-loop handover state with inspection clear: $z_A$ or $z_B$, according to the holder. A nonnegative \emph{rank} decreases on every arrow, excluding safe but endless inspection. Handover restores the selected endpoint's full permitted behavior.

\paragraph{What synthesis chooses.}
These inputs supply a partial constraint, not a complete update sequence. All Cell events are controllable: $m_A,m_B$ move to $A,B$, $j$ inspects, and $\rho_A,\rho_B$ replace the respective empty station. Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:cell} gives the complete automata, initializers and path proof. Existing DUCS can encode this staging in its update environment; FG-DUCS generates the staged game from local transfers. The next example shows why requirements also need separate boundaries.

\subsection{Audit and Role Lifetimes}
We call the second constructed example \emph{Policy(2)}: the number denotes its two old/new requirement pairs, audit and access role. At least one audit rule must remain active throughout, while the old and new roles must never overlap. These obligations require opposing boundary orders:
\[
\begin{array}{r@{\quad}c@{\;}c@{\;}c@{\;}c@{\;}c}
\text{Audit:}&\text{old only}&\xrightarrow{\text{new-start}}&\text{both active}&\xrightarrow{\text{old-stop}}&\text{new only}\\[2pt]
\text{Role:}&\text{old only}&\xrightarrow{\text{old-stop}}&\text{neither active}&\xrightarrow{\text{new-start}}&\text{new only}
\end{array}
\]
The role gap is declared by the interval requirement, rather than forced by access rules alone. The new audit monitor starts unlogged and requires a log before service. Each endpoint alternates its own log and service, permitting only its own access role. With an identity transfer, the saved policy obeys these opposing orders and reaches a compatible new endpoint from both old states in at most six events.

If the starts are merged and the stops are merged, the first start overlaps roles and the first stop removes audit coverage. Ordinary events and transfer do not change which rules are active, so neither old entry has a completing merged policy. Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:policy_interpretation} gives the complete finite input, policy and losing closure. This compares merged and separate FG-DUCS requirement boundaries.

\subsection{Execution and Handover}

The execution connection has four distinct premises (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:execution_proof}):
\par\noindent\textbf{A1:} endpoint observations preserve component/monitor states and same-labeled endpoint edges.
\par\noindent\textbf{A2:} update control disables only unselected controllables, preserves every UC event/outcome, reports the successor and eventually observes a next event.
\par\noindent\textbf{A3:} commands start quiescently and finish atomically, finitely and failure-free with an observed modeled successor.
\par\noindent\textbf{A4:} entry observes/copies the actual old state; quiescent handover installs the selected compatible state, using finite failure-free linearizable boundaries~\cite{HerlihyWing1990}.

Under A1--A4, every execution maps to the linked model and reaches handover within the entry rank (Theorem~\ref{thm:realization}). History completeness places every actual activation history in its declared scope; with RS it justifies the residual interpretation. A2 assumes eventual event progress, without fairness among enabled choices. Internal divergence and unobservable outcomes are excluded; rank bounds events, not time. 

\section{How Granularity Changes Feasibility}
\label{sec:granularity-analysis}
Merging removes intermediate states and can prevent or enable completion.

\paragraph{What is merged.}
Partition transfers, old stops and new starts separately into blocks. A transfer block $B\subseteq I$ simultaneously permits all targets in $\prod_{i\in B}g_i(s_i)$; a boundary block removes or initializes its monitors at the same component tuple. Other active monitors compose member-label effects, checked to commute pairwise on every monitor/residual state. Mapped precedence must remain irreflexive and acyclic. Ordinary behavior, entries, endpoints, relations, initializers and targets stay fixed; quiescence and all member guards apply. These are comparison contracts, not DUCS encodings.

\input{technical_fragments/granularity_obstruction.tex}

\paragraph{A capacity boundary.}
Readiness gates motivate the constructed Rolling contract~\cite{e6_kubernetes_deployments,e6_mongodb_enterprise_replica_set}. It starts with $n$ ready replicas, no spares, and an interval floor of $m$ ready replicas. A transfer boots its replicas; each becomes ready after one UC event, with no waiting loop. Service self-loops are controllable. The all-new, ready target restores capacity; service may pause during the update.

\begin{proposition}[Rolling threshold]\label{prop:rolling-threshold}
For $n\ge2$, $1\le m<n$ and $1\le k\le n$, fixed consecutive batches of size $k$ (with a smaller final remainder) admit safe finite completion iff $k\le n-m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Some mandatory batch has size $k$. Its transfer leaves at most $n-k$ ready replicas, so $k>n-m$ violates the interval. Otherwise, transfer one batch only when all replicas are ready, disable service, and retain every UC readiness outcome before the next transfer. Readiness never falls below $n-k\ge m$. With $u$ old and $b$ booting replicas, $2u+b$ strictly decreases on every selected transfer and every UC event, reaching zero exactly at the target. Thus all maximal runs finish without fairness.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{When separate transfers preserve feasibility.}
Finer commands need not preserve a merged winner. Two one-state components can synchronize UC labels so that both endpoints are quiescent, but either mixed version has a UC loop. Separate identity transfers then lose; a joint transfer skips the mixed state and wins at rank one (complete contract in Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:granularity-reverse}). 

\begin{proposition}[Serial transfer preservation]\label{prop:serial-transfer}
Consider transfer-only merging with unchanged entries, endpoints, targets, initializers and ordinary behavior, Cartesian transfer outcomes, commuting active-monitor effects and valid mapped precedence. If every transfer preserves its component's UC synchronization alphabet and enables no previously disabled local UC label, a merged winner implies a fine winner preserving the same selected endpoint continuations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The local condition keeps partial batches UC-quiescent. Any partial monitor error would persist to a completed Cartesian outcome, contradicting the merged winner. Finite serial expansion retains completion and endpoint choices; Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:serial-transfer} gives the full argument.
\end{proof}
The constructed Threads family, with workers sharing a bounded queue, satisfies this condition: old/new worker graphs coincide and transfers preserve idle states and the queue (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:constructed_contracts}). Rolling enables readiness and violates it, but its capacity threshold separately gives safe batch sizes. 
\section{Synthesizing and Checking an Update Policy}
The procedure specializes strong-planning and on-the-fly reachability principles~\cite{Cimatti1998StrongPlanning,TripakisAltisen1999,CiolekDCS}, retaining every queried event outcome and producing evidence for every old entry.
\label{sec:algorithm}

A \emph{bucket} is $\Post(q,a)$; empty means disabled. Disabling controls at a UC-enabled state removes runs without creating deadlock, preserving winning (\emph{UC priority}, Lemma~\ref{lem:uc-priority}). With quiescent goals and no fairness or controller priority, permitting commands while UC is enabled therefore adds no winning entries. Changing fairness or priority changes the game. The proof establishes memoryless sufficiency.

\subsection{Lazy Successor Generation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:otf} constructs a reachability attractor~\cite{CassezEtAl2005TimedGames}. $V$ contains discovered states, $X$ those with all UC candidates queried, and $W$ proved winners. \emph{Promote} adds an unproved safe state in $X$ to $W$ when its nonempty UC successor set lies in $W$ or, at quiescence, one complete nonempty control bucket lies in $W$. It retains that proving set and assigns rank one above its maximum successor rank. Predecessors are then reconsidered. Lazy queries controls incrementally, keeping unfinished cursors in $\mathsf{Retry}$. Failure requires exhausted candidates and no further promotion. Extraction retains lower-rank controls and matching endpoint targets.

\SetAlgoNlRelativeSize{-1}
\SetAlgoRefRelativeSize{-1}
\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Lazy}
\label{alg:otf}
\footnotesize
\KwIn{Typed update contract (Section~\ref{sec:formalization})}
\KwOut{$(\sigma,\rank,\kappa)$ or $(\textnormal{no solution},L)$}
$V\leftarrow Q_0$; $X,\mathsf{Retry}\leftarrow\emptyset$; $W\leftarrow V\cap\Goal$\;
$\mathsf{Open}\leftarrow\{q\in Q_0\mid\Safe(q)\land q\notin\Goal\}$; set $\rank(q)\leftarrow0$ for $q\in W$\;
\While{true}{
  Add queued goals to $W$ once with rank zero; exhaust \emph{Promote}, retaining witnesses/ranks, removing winners from Retry and propagating predecessors\;
  \lIf{$Q_0\subseteq W$}{\Return $(\sigma,\rank|_{\Reach_\sigma(Q_0)},\kappa)$ constructed from the goal witnesses and retained lower-rank choices}
  \eIf{$\mathsf{Open}\neq\emptyset$}{
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Open}$, add it to $X$, and query all uncontrollable candidates\;
    Close the control cursor if UC-enabled; otherwise initialize it and query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion\;
    Insert $q$ into $\mathsf{Retry}$ iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }{
    \lIf{$\mathsf{Retry}=\emptyset$}{\Return $(\textnormal{no solution},L\leftarrow V\setminus W)$}
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Retry}$; query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion; reinsert iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }
  Add every queried successor to $V$; queue newly discovered goals for rank zero and safe non-goals for $\mathsf{Open}$ (unsafe states stay outside $W$ and $\mathsf{Open}$)\;
}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Design and Implementation}

RQ1 tests granularity; RQ2 checks correctness; RQ3 compares exploration and cost on \ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts.

Each inherited input has three named requirement configurations: Base has no interval requirement; R1 protects selected actions during a requirement gap; R2 imposes selected ordering constraints. R1 blocks each designated action while its flag is true: any old-stop sets it, its designated new-start clears it. R2 requires selected boundaries before their transfers. In the GSM mobile-communication model, R1 blocks communication and decoding; R2 puts both decode-rule boundaries before transfer without ordering them. Event choices vary by model. PC2-Rolling, a production-cell calibration example, separately tests availability. The archived O-DUCS implementation (Legacy) also synthesizes subsequent control, a different objective.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
Our MTSA extension~\cite{paper:MTSA2008} checks certificates~\cite{McConnellEtAl2011Certifying}; its composition checker, Link, checks the connected old, update and new controllers, including entries and targets. An independent oracle parses a restricted subset of Finite State Processes (FSP)~\cite{MageeKramer2006}. Finite-JSON runs save losing membership; FSP benchmarks report only its statistics.

\paragraph{Use of generative AI}
Generative AI assisted contract and example design, proof drafting, Java solver/frontend implementation, Python checkers, experiment drivers, execution, result analysis and manuscript editing. Validation used certificate and exhaustive checks for algorithms, source and automaton comparisons for initialization, saved-output checks for tables and primary sources for citations. Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:implementation_details} details tool roles and validation; Threats discusses correlated faults.

\paragraph{Constructing the benchmark inputs.}
Nine inputs adapt eight DUCS sources. PC1 and PC2 are production-cell variants sharing one parent; they retain state-gated arrivals and make tool results UC. With work/output withheld, pending results and reset/arrival chains reach quiescence. Adaptations shorten Railcab transfers but retain both outcomes, omit two initial environment events in PowerPlant and alter safety/ordering. Equivalence is unestablished (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:additional_populations}).

MTSA synthesizes fixed endpoints from FSP and admits every reachable new-endpoint state. Before search, it builds monitors and global-tuple initializer tables; Update-interval (UPD) requirements use a constant post-entry state. Physical observers track nonlocal labels and survive commands/transfers. The finite-JSON Cell$_n$ family, which extends Cell to $n$ stations, supplies holder-dependent maps directly (S3). S4 reports all preparation and tuple counts.

\paragraph{Exploration variants.}

\subsubsection{A Source-Derived Calibration Case}\label{sec:pc2-case}
PC2-Rolling adds calibration while requiring another arm operational. Requests are controllable, completions UC, and transfers enter calibration while preserving initializer observers. Erasing calibration recovers the original operational process, without whole-problem equivalence. Endpoints retain baseline requirements plus availability; the interval additionally treats transfers as calibration entry. The 25 boundaries and two transfers have no supplied order (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:pc2-generation}).

The saved policy calibrates the arms successively, waiting for each completion. It covers all \PCTwoCaseEntries{} entries; every outcome decreases rank from at most \PCTwoCaseMaxRank{}, with \PCTwoCaseMinOperationalArms{} arm outside calibration. Link checks composition. All thirteen source invariants satisfy the activation-scoped interpretation on reached tuples through their start-only suffixes. This covers neither all initializer tuples nor all 138 occurrences; GSM below exercises ordinary actions under an active new requirement.

For history-wide obligations, only 39/126 entries admit a safe full prefix; 87 fail (21 before entry, 66 during update). All admit a prior violating cycle restoring zero observers. Safe-history nonemptiness for 38,288 initializer tuples and runtime are unchecked (formulas, paths and partitions: Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:pc2_history_boundary}).

\paragraph{Why the fixed merge loses.}
Every old entry admits both raw transfers. Before joint transfer, old plants lack calibration actions and all other steps preserve readiness. The joint transfer therefore puts both arms in calibration, clearing both interval readiness bits and reaching error. This proves impossibility, while the merged solver and fine Direct-Full both time out at \VThreeESixPCTwoCap{}~s/\VThreeESixPCTwoHeap{}~GiB. Those timeouts remain unresolved solver results.

Restricted-transfer comparisons also retain both-LOSS and incomplete cases (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:additional_populations}; S1--S4).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\label{sec:expressiveness}
\paragraph{Generating update environments.}
Update-environment generation from supplied global state-preservation transitions precedes FG-DUCS~\cite[Sec.~V]{Yamauchi2020Partitioning}. DUCS adds \texttt{reconfigure} edges through possibly partial $R$, without a UC-quiescence guard~\cite[Sec.~4.2, Defs.~3.7, 5.1]{Nahabedian2020}. FG-DUCS requires global UC quiescence at commands and handover.

\paragraph{On-demand synthesis.}
Directed controller synthesis (DCS) already combines lazy products and controls, UC priority and backward propagation~\cite[Sec.~IV]{CiolekDCS}. Its 2016 formulation requires safety and eventual goal occurrence on every trace; the later nonblocking formulation (OTF-DCS, 2023) requires a marked extension of each finite controlled trace~\cite[Def.~4]{CiolekEtAl2023OTFDCS}. FG-DUCS specializes these patterns to its contract, with full outcome buckets and all-entry certificates. Other approaches structure specifications compositionally or modularly~\cite{MohajeraniMalikFabian2014Compositional,WonhamRamadge1988Modular}.

\paragraph{Supervisor switching.}
Related guarantees include safe, deadlock-free supervisor switching~\cite[Sec.~3.3]{MeiraGoesLafortune2020Switching}, workcell switching faster than UC events~\cite[Sec.~III]{JiaoGanXiao2013Reconfiguration}, startup bounds under weak invariance~\cite{NooruldeenSchmidt2015} and mode-specific invariants under UC switches~\cite{ReveliotisFei2017SwitchedDES}.

\paragraph{Requirements across adaptation.}
Obligations across change occur in temporal adaptation, scenario updatability and Live Synthesis~\cite{ZhangCheng2006ALTL,Ghezzi2012DynamicScenarioUpdate,PanzicaLaManna2013CorrectnessDynamicUpdates,FinkbeinerEtAl2022LiveSynthesis}; FG-DUCS uses scoped requirements and residual reasoning~\cite{Dwyer1999Patterns,RosuHavelund2005Rewriting}. Thuijsman and Reniers~\cite[Secs.~4--7]{ThuijsmanReniers2024DynamicFeature} support individual/simultaneous reconfiguration, feature-dependent requirements and state retention/reset. Their guarantee requires a marked continuation from every reachable state; FG-DUCS requires all maximal update runs to finish at a supplied controller.

\paragraph{Safe adaptation paths.}
Safe adaptation graphs support minimum-cost paths~\cite[Secs.~3--4]{Zhang2005SafeAdaptation}. Reconfiguration sequences preserve lifecycle and architectural constraints, reaching the target without failures or a consistent architecture after recovery~\cite[Secs.~II--VI]{BoyerGruberPous2013}. FG-DUCS instead checks finite handover to a fixed controller under declared requirement lifetimes; it does not optimize cost or repair failures.

\paragraph{Operational update mechanisms.}
Local, live, workflow and network updating address migration and transition safety~\cite{PanzicaLaManna2012LocalUpdate,Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate,Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates,e6_hayden2014_kitsune,McClurgEtAl2015NetworkUpdates}. Dynamic dependencies and sufficient freeness support transaction version consistency~\cite{MaEtAl2011VersionConsistency}; FG-DUCS's finite handover does not imply that guarantee.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
Perpetual UC arrivals or polling block local commands; Threads contrasts finite UC phases with perpetual activity. Fairness-dependent waiting is outside the contract; inherited-adaptation effects on feasibility are not isolated. Decoding, history completeness and A1--A4 remain unvalidated; repair is excluded. Constructed cases assume finite startup, reporting, recovery and election; selection may favor separations, and deployed prevalence is unestablished.

Shared authorship, AI and code risk correlated faults; independent parsing covers restricted FSP. Direct-Full is in-house and Legacy solves a different objective. External solvers, pruning isolation and randomized orders are untested. Ratios exclude unresolved costs; extended trials lack medians. Sampled RSS can miss peaks and identifies neither heap use nor bottlenecks; S4 retains interruptions.

Null/LOSS results, faster Update-first, lower Eager/Update-first memory and Travel/PC failures bound gains; PC2's merged timeout remains unresolved. One-machine fixtures/repeats are not independent applications. Inherited inputs lack precedence and only Railcab transfers branch. Exponential separation assumes the default order.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
FG-DUCS compiles local transfers, requirement lifetimes and initializers into safe finite updates preserving the selected endpoint's continuation. Merging can help or prevent completion; a local UC condition guarantees safe serialization of transfer-only merges. With the same UC pruning, Lazy decides \FixedLazyDecisions{}/\ApplicationContracts{} contracts to Eager's \FixedEagerDecisions{}; Update-first is sometimes faster. Guarantees require declared lifetimes, UC quiescence and faithful execution. This supports maintenance planning with explicit duties and checkable policies.
