# A positive-retry DAG where hidden cached comparisons cost work

The independent-job theorem10 attains its least universal W curve without a cached Boolean. This does not extend to every DAG. Use the whole-kernel completion contract and the stronger *output-erasing cached observation* contract of the zero-retry comparison theorem: under a zero-write matching completion and a fresh-own-key-write mismatching completion, cached returns the same fixed job result and leaves the entire foreground-visible state identical. It exposes no current-input reference, saved comparison bit, cost counter, clock, allocation observation or callback side effect. Before/after free inspections and retained local records remain allowed. Returning a constant alone is insufficient if the wrapper leaks another diagnostic.

Let G be2->4->1->1, r1, Omega8, and V_1^visible/V_1^erased the B-independent full-program classes with Q<=5 and L(B)<=Top_(B-1)+ at every finiteB. Then

    inf_{P in V_1^visible} W_P(4)=17;
    inf_{P in V_1^erased}  W_P(4)=18.

The guarantees are universal; B4 selects the coordinate at which the two policy classes differ. This is not a supplied-B policy comparison. The lower bounds include arbitrary paid preparations after readiness, old records, free inspections and history-dependent choices in their respective observation contracts.

## Visible upper bound

Use the fixed chain order, re-preparing before every comparison. Before a cheap failure usecheap; afterward usecached. Add one change: if the first cheap failure was at the weight4 job, and its cached call and the immediately following weight1 cached call both mismatch, complete the final1 job fresh.

Q<=5 holds in all finite-write environments. The unmodified policy has the optimal protected-work guarantee. At the changed node there have been at least three distinct invalidating writes: the cheap failure and two cached mismatches. Already protected work is5; adding the last1 gives6=Top_2<=Top_(B-1)+. Thus the change preserves every budget's L guarantee, including environments with extra inert/unrelated writes. No B value is input to the policy.

AtB4, if the first failure occurs at the weight2 job, duplicate work is at most2+Top_3=9. If it occurs at the weight4 job, duplicate work is at most9: after the failed preparation of4, a mismatch at4 plus a mismatch at the next1 triggersfresh and prevents another duplicate; otherwise cached mismatches total at most5. A first failure at a light job costs at most1+2=3 duplicates. No-failure runs have no duplicate work. Consequently W<=8+9=17.

## Visible full-program lower bound

The maximum-job target construction from proof10, with m1 and r1, forces one useful cheap failure and one useful cached mismatch at the weight4 job under an unconditional two-write cap. The predecessor2 must complete first. Global charging has already paid2+3*4=14 and protected4, with no retry remaining. After those two writes the no-more-write completion must have L<=Top_1=4. The next light job cannot complete fresh or stale-cached. Any cheap call could be failed by one additional fresh write, violating the universal call cap. Thus a useful cached comparison must eventually occur at that light job; write once there. Its prepared and protected units cost2 and the final job still costs1, giving W>=14+2+1=17 under a globally three-write environment, which is also permitted atB4. This is the same guarded-tail charging argument as scope proof11, with the weight2 predecessor included.

## Erased full-program lower bound

Run a restricted environment that writes only at useful weight4 cheap comparisons and stops after one write. No protected work is permitted with at most one actual write. Hence all earlier jobs must complete without protection and the target cannot complete until its first useful cheap call has failed. Already-stale failures do not help; consuming the only retry without such a write would make universal completion with L(0)=0 impossible. The weight4 failure consumes the sole retry and a distinct paid weight4 preparation.

Now stop writing. On this one-write complete play, L must remain0. All three residual jobs must therefore finish by matching cached calls: fresh/stale-cached would add positiveL, and a cheap call remains vulnerable to a second failure in a finite-write extension and violatesQ<=5. Free actions cannot avoid completion indefinitely. Retained preparations may be early after readiness but are all charged from the common start.

In a second environment, retain that first cheap-failure write and insert a fresh own-key identity at each of those three cached comparison gates. Use the gate-indexed schedule and stop unconditionally after four writes. Induction on foreground operations gives identical visible states after each cached completion by the erasure contract. Prior inspections/retained records are the same, and a post-gate record choice uses only the common prior store. Therefore the same subsequent cached calls occur even for history-dependent programs. Each matching preparation in the first play is invalidated in the second, causing a distinct duplicate of weights4,1,1. Together with mandatoryOmega8 and the failed weight4 preparation,

    W>=8+4+(4+1+1)=18.

The simple chain-order cheap-then-cached policy attains18 atB4: failure at2 costs at most2+7=9 duplicates, failure at4 costs at most4+6=10, and a light failure costs at most3. It ignores the cached Boolean and belongs toV_1 at everyB. This proves equality for the stronger full-program lower-bound class.
