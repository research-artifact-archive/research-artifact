# Partial-repair boundary: analytical hypothesis and computational check

2026-09-10 ~05:50 JST. SCIENTIFIC author exploration, root sole writer. This is a new operation model motivated by the stronger Roslyn rebase comparison, not a rerun or reclassification of old results. The equations and two-component example below were derived by hand before this computational study; they are hypotheses to check, not preregistered unseen outcomes. All execution outcomes and corrections are retained. Stop by10:00JST, prepare09:50. No humans, paid API, credits or reset.

One persistent job has m independent prepared components of positive work w_j. A write changes a monotone version and invalidates a specified footprint of components. A call observes the dirty set D while holding protection; it can publish after repairing exactly D under protection (cost sum_D w), or release without publishing and prepare outside again. A fresh protected completion costs sum_j w. No cross-call protection, batched independent jobs, free version probes or cost-free protected repair. Q is the number of validation/completion calls; Q≤q=r+1 for all finite-write executions. Total L is protected component repair work; commit integration is an identical baseline not part of L. This is not a native timing model.

Let Γ be the fixed family of nonempty write footprints, each costing one actual write; repetitions allowed. G(b) is maximum dirty work caused by at most b footprints in one preparation interval (weighted maximum coverage), G(0)=0. For the first implementation all m components are reachable; thus G eventually saturates at W=sum w. In particular singleton footprints yield G(b)=Ω_min(b,m).

Hand-derived hypotheses:
1. Known-B optimum is K_q(B)=G(floor(B/q)). Threshold oracle accepts a dirty set of work≤K and retries larger ones, with fresh protected completion also allowed. Every rejected set needs at least floor(B/q)+1 writes; q such sets exceed B. Lower environment repeats a footprint word realizing G(floor(B/q)) in each of q calls, spending at most B.
2. Any deterministic B-unaware policy attaining the zero optimum for every B≤r must reject r successive single-write positive-damage observations (every prefix is compatible with a low-budget execution). At the next call, k further writes can force G(k) protected repair. The ordinary retry-first-then-repair threshold attains G((B-r)_+), so this is the best envelope subject to those low-B optima.
3. For r≥1, a single universal policy simultaneously realizes all K_q(B) iff G saturates after one write: G(b)=G(1) for all b≥1. If G first increases at k≥2, B=r+k gives floor(B/q)<k, so the low-budget-forced execution costs G(k)>G(floor(B/q)). If one write can cause maximum damage, the two formulas coincide. r=0 has no separation.
4. Singleton, unit-work m=r+1 gives a minimal family: at B=2m-1, known optimum1 versus m under any policy also optimal for B≤r=m-1. For m=2,r=1,B=3, the known policy accepts one dirty component, retries two; the low-B optimum forces rejecting the first one-component mismatch and two further writes force cost2 in the second call. This does not refute the original whole-kernel theorem.
