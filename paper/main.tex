\begin{abstract}
A concurrent transformation can prepare optimistically and retry after validation failure, or compute while excluding conflicting writes. Dependencies also make the next ready transformation a control choice. We study persistent completion, fixed opaque work and declared guarded-work premiums. A potential proves that serial retry policies attain the minimum worst work of a preparation/guard interface with retained preparations and batch validation. For serial atomic callbacks, a matching adversary uses at most $B$ actual external writes, including shared internal monitors. Specializing a known reflected recurrence, we compile all-budget values and policies. A basis with input costs as slopes gives a tight $2n$ run bound and $2^n\operatorname{poly}(\lambda)$ bit-time construction/checking for binary input length $\lambda$. Threshold synthesis is NP-complete even for $B=1$, two layers and unit premiums; unique-order and cost-compatible DAGs permit compilation and generated-certificate checking in $O(|E|+n\log(n+1))$ arithmetic operations. Fixed evaluation checks 4,232 complete-primitive inputs and 19,644 native Java paths on 48 generated DAGs plus controls. Among 192 priced roots, adaptation beats the best fixed topological order on two, with maximum total-work reduction 2.08\%. Specified-budget alternatives expose when constructing all budgets is unnecessary. Practical prevalence and elapsed-time benefit remain unmeasured.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A program transforming dependent objects can retain speculative results, hold guards or batch validation. Under our preparation/guard contract, serial retry macros attain the optimum over this richer interface. A program author supplies the dependency DAG, fixed work, protection premiums and interference allowance. The compiler returns job/mode choices and checkable all-budget values; it does not infer the contract or prices from arbitrary code.

\section{The Preparation and Guard Interface}
\label{sec:primitives}

Jobs $J=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ form a DAG with incoming neighbors $\operatorname{Pred}(i)$. Examples and artifacts use zero-based identifiers. Each requires one foreground transformation. The completed set $D$ is an ideal: a job is physically ready only when $\operatorname{Pred}(i)\subseteq D$. Completion persists for the batch objective, although later external writes may change a committed live value. The objective is one linearized transformation per job, not a simultaneous all-object snapshot or commit.

A ready job invokes a pure opaque whole kernel of fixed work $w_i>0$ on its input identity and captured immutable predecessor outputs, including their processing in $w_i$. Partial evaluation, premature preparation, incremental substitutes and external milestone discharge are excluded. A result is a nonforgeable capability tied to the job, identity and predecessor tuple. Commit stores the exact successful prepared/callback output before publishing completion; later live-object reads cannot reconstruct it. External writes change own-input identities without mutating captured outputs.

Kernel evaluation while protection excludes conflicting updates costs $w_i+p_i$, with $p_i\ge0$; unprotected evaluation costs $w_i$. Incidental protection from a shared guard must use the same pricing rule. Acquisition is guaranteed to finish. Acquisition, release, inspection, validation, commit, copying and discard cost zero. The metric excludes their machine instructions, allocation, waiting and elapsed time. Rational prices can be uniformly scaled to integers.

An interface state is $(x,b)$ with remaining invalidation allowance $b$. Initially $x_i=\U$ and $b=B$. The five local states are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cl}
\toprule
$\U$ & Unprepared, unguarded\\
$\C$ & Cached input/result pair, unguarded\\
$\LL$ & Guarded, needs computation\\
$\LC$ & Guarded with a valid prepared result\\
$\D$ & Required foreground transformation committed\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Nature resolves the program's history-dependent choices. Normal validation is always legal; invalidations consume tokens as below. Validation resolves a cached $\C$ result's usability; $\LC$ stays valid while guarded. Kernels require physical readiness, so cached jobs' predecessors remain completed. Unprepared jobs' guards may be acquired/released before readiness.

\paragraph{Individual operations.}
Preparation changes $\U\to\C$ for $w_i$, or $\LL\to\LC$ for $w_i+p_i$. Free deterministic operations are acquisition $\U\to\LL$, release $\LL\to\U$ or $\LC\to\C$, commit $\LC\to\D$, and discard $\C\to\U$ or $\LC\to\LL$. A conditional commit from $\C$ succeeds to $\D$ for zero cost, or, if $b>0$, fails to $\U$ and spends one token. An additional retained-preparation failure to $\C$ may be offered. Acquisition/validation from $\C$ normally reaches $\LC$, or spends one token and reaches $\LL$, retaining its guard for further decisions.

An atomic fresh callback completes $\U\to\D$ for $w_i+p_i$. An atomic cached callback from $\C$ completes for zero cost on a match, or spends one token and recomputes for $w_i+p_i$ on a mismatch. These operations affect only the selected job. Free cached inspection can report a match and remain $\C$, or spend one token and become $\U$. Conditional acquisition of an uncomputed snapshot can normally reach $\LL$, or spend one token and remain $\U$; both outcomes cost zero.

\paragraph{Fixed batches.}
The program may select any subset of unguarded jobs for one atomic acquisition/validation. Selected $\U$ jobs become $\LL$. If $K$ is the selected cached subset, Nature chooses $F\subseteq K$ with $|F|\le b$: members of $F$ become $\LL$, the others become $\LC$, and $b$ decreases by $|F|$. This grants all groups, even if a concrete interface offers fewer. Batch release and commit have the same accumulated cost as individual operations and add no all-object atomicity guarantee.

Interleaving, multiple preparations and held guards are allowed. Invocations must finish; noncompletion, including free cycles, costs infinity. The complete outcomes define the adversary contract. Restricted/correlated outcomes can reduce an application's optimum: synthesis retains an upper bound, while equality needs a matching environment. Section~\ref{sec:java} supplies one for a callback subclass.

\section{The Reduced Retry Game}
\label{sec:model}

Jobs $J=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ have integer costs $a_i,c_i>0$, $p_i\ge0$: normal work, failed-attempt work and protection premium. The initial budget is an integer $B\ge0$. A decision state $(S,b)$ contains unfinished jobs and remaining allowance, initially $(J,B)$. Only $i\in\mathcal A(S)=\{i\in S:\operatorname{Pred}(i)\cap S=\varnothing\}$ is available. Completion persists, so reachable unfinished sets are upsets. For proofs, extend the game to every subset, treating absent predecessors as completed.

The controller selects an available job and mode. \emph{Protected} pays $a_i+p_i$ and reaches $(S\setminus\{i\},b)$. \emph{Fast} lets Nature choose normal completion for $a_i$, or, if $b>0$, failure for $c_i$ to $(S,b-1)$. Normal completion is always legal. Both players observe outcomes/history. A failure spends global allowance, permits reselection and need not undo external writes.

\paragraph{Termination and residual costs.}
Decreasing rank $|S|+b$ gives termination within $n+B$ attempts. Subtracting the once-paid $A(S)=\sum_{i\in S}a_i$ leaves costs zero/$p_i$/$c_i$ for normal/protected/failed execution. Optimal worst excess satisfies
\begin{align}
 V(\varnothing,b)&=V(S,0)=0,\\
 V(S,b)&=\min_{i\in\mathcal A(S)}\min\left\{p_i+V(S-i,b),\;
 \max\left[V(S-i,b),c_i+V(S,b-1)\right]\right\},\quad b>0.
 \label{eq:bellman}
\end{align}
Here $S-i=S\setminus\{i\}$. With $N\le2^n$ reachable completion ideals, the recurrence has up to $(B+1)N$ residual states and $n$ candidate jobs each. Theorem~\ref{thm:hardness} gives a separate complexity lower bound.

A minimizing deterministic action attains each finite perfect-information worst-path value. This does not address expected costs against an oblivious adversary.


Set $a_i=c_i=w_i$ for the primitive interface; the order/compiler results also allow $a_i\ne c_i$. The rank argument concerns macros; primitive programs may have free cycles.

\begin{lemma}[Subset monotonicity]
\label{lem:subset}
For $T\subseteq S$ and any $b$, $V(T,b)\le V(S,b)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Simulate a policy on $S$, completing omitted jobs normally or protected with nonnegative premium. Retained jobs relay actual actions, outcomes and token decrements. Physical and virtual unfinished sets satisfy $R=W\cap T$, making each virtually available retained job physically available. Each omitted job disappears in one action; after physical completion, finish the virtual remainder normally. Physical cost never exceeds virtual cost. Minimize over policies.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cheapest available fast action]
\label{lem:priority}
Fix a minimum-$c_i$ job $i\in\mathcal A(S)$ and $b>0$. For $F_j=\max\{V(S-j,b),c_j+V(S,b-1)\}$, we have $F_i\le F_j$ for every $j\in\mathcal A(S)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $j\ne i$, $j$ remains available after removing $i$, so choosing fast $j$ in $S-i$ gives
\[
 V(S-i,b)\le\max\{V(S-\{i,j\},b),c_j+V(S-i,b-1)\}\le F_j,
\]
where the final inequality follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:subset}. The other branch satisfies $c_i+V(S,b-1)\le c_j+V(S,b-1)\le F_j$. Their maximum is $F_i$. The case $j=i$ is immediate.
\end{proof}


Fast-action dominance neither chooses the protected job nor fixes completion order; the introduction's example requires adaptation.

\subsection{Single-Failure Complexity}

At $B=1$, inspect a policy's no-failure path: a topological order $\pi$ and its protected jobs $H$. After the only possible failure, every remaining job can finish fast with zero further excess, in the same remaining order. Thus its optimal continuation has value
\begin{equation}
 C(\pi,H)=\max\left\{\sum_{j\in H}p_j,\quad
 \max_{i\notin H}\left(c_i+\sum_{\substack{j\in H\\j\prec_\pi i}}p_j\right)\right\},
 \label{eq:onefailure}
\end{equation}
omitting the second term when all jobs are protected. Minimizing over $\pi,H$ gives $V(J,1)$; hence an initially fixed order attains it. Yet finding that order is hard.

\begin{theorem}[Restricted threshold complexity]
\label{thm:hardness}
At $B=1$, deciding $V(J,1)\le K$ is NP-complete even with at most two DAG layers, maximum indegree two, all $p_i=1$, and at most two positive instance-dependent failure-cost values. The reduction's numbers are polynomially bounded in its job count.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An order and mode bits form a polynomial certificate checked by Equation~\eqref{eq:onefailure}, proving membership. Reduce from CLIQUE~\cite{karp}. Handle trivial instances first; otherwise $k\ge2$, $m\ge\ell=\binom{k}{2}$. Add an isolated vertex, preserving clique existence and giving $n>k$ vertices. Set $K=n+m-\ell>k$. Each vertex becomes a job with $(c,p)=(K+1,1)$. Each edge becomes a job with $(c,p)=(K-k,1)$ dependent on its two endpoints.

For a $k$-clique, protect its $k$ vertices, then run its $\ell$ edges fast. A failure costs $k+(K-k)=K$; subsequently all jobs finish fast. With no failure, protect every remaining job, giving total premium $n+m-\ell=K$.

Conversely, on a policy's no-failure path of value at most $K$, every vertex must be protected: its fast failure alone would cost $K+1$. Each fast edge must follow at most $k$ protected jobs, since its failure costs $K-k$. Its endpoints therefore lie among the same first $k$ protected vertices; interspersed protected edges cannot enlarge this set. If $f$ edges are fast, no-failure premium $n+m-f\le K$ implies $f\ge\ell$. At most $k$ vertices contain at most $\ell$ distinct edges, so they form a clique. The DAG has $n+m$ jobs, $2m$ dependencies and costs at most $n+m+1$. The full-work threshold is $A(J)+K$.
\end{proof}

Hardness thus precedes the need for order adaptation and large budgets. It does not prove that ideal enumeration or exponential space is necessary.

\section{A Cost-Preserving Primitive Abstraction}
\label{sec:abstraction}

With $a_i=c_i=w_i$, the retry game matches the optimum over all causal primitive programs.

\begin{lemma}[One fast pass]
\label{lem:pass}
For $K\subseteq\mathcal A(T)$,
\begin{equation}
V(T,b)\le
\max_{\substack{F\subseteq K\\|F|\le b}}
\left\{\sum_{i\in F}w_i+
V((T\setminus K)\cup F,b-|F|)\right\}.
\label{eq:pass}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Attempt each member of $K$ fast once, deferring failures, then continue optimally. Availability persists. Each $F\subseteq K$, $|F|\le b$, is a possible failure set with prefix cost $\sum_{i\in F}w_i$ and residual $((T\setminus K)\cup F,b-|F|)$. This feasible policy's worst cost bounds the optimum.
\end{proof}

Define the active set and potential of a primitive state by
\begin{align}
T(x)&=\{i:x_i\in\{\U,\C,\LL\}\},\\
\Phi(x,b)&=\sum_{i:x_i\in\{\U,\LL\}}w_i+V(T(x),b).
\label{eq:potential}
\end{align}
Let $D$ be actual completed jobs and $Q=\{i:x_i=\LC\}$. Every $q\in Q$ was ready at preparation, so $\operatorname{Pred}(q)\subseteq D$. Thus $D\cup Q$ is an ideal and $T=J\setminus(D\cup Q)$ remains a valid upset. It is a \emph{virtual} residual set: for $a\to b$, $a=\LC$ and $b=\U$, the game on $T=\{b\}$ permits $b$, but physical readiness still forbids its kernel. Conversely, every physically ready job in $T$ belongs to $\mathcal A(T)$, since its predecessors lie in actual $D$. Every cached job is physically ready, and the cached members of any acquisition batch form an available antichain.

The potential provisionally omits valid guarded preparations $\LC$, but must restore their obligations if released instead of freely committed.

\begin{theorem}[Primitive value equality]
\label{thm:primitive}
For the interface of Section~\ref{sec:primitives}, the minimum worst accumulated work from all unprepared objects is
\[
\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{primitive}}(J,B)=\sum_{i\in J}w_i+V(J,B).
\]
An optimal retry controller attains this value through serial macros: preparation and conditional commit, or acquisition, guarded preparation and commit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fast prepares and conditionally commits, discarding after retained-preparation failure; protected acquires, prepares and commits. Both macros execute physically ready jobs, capture exact successful outputs and publish milestones with the game's costs/outcomes. Their availability at every all-$\U$ unfinished boundary gives the upper bound.

For the lower bound, we show that every primitive action admits a legal Nature outcome with immediate cost plus successor potential at least the current potential. Write $T=T(x)$ and remove unchanged baseline terms.

Kernel and cached-validation actions concern physically ready jobs, hence available members of $T$. Unguarded preparation exchanges its $w_i$ baseline for equal cost. Acquisition/release of unprepared objects leaves the potential unchanged, including before readiness. Guarded preparation removes the baseline and job for $w_i+p_i$, using the protected bound $V(T,b)\le p_i+V(T-i,b)$.

Conditional commit or acquisition/validation of $\C$ gives successor terms $V(T-i,b)$ on success and $w_i+V(T,b-1)$ on invalidation to $\U$ or $\LL$. Their maximum bounds $V(T,b)$ by the fast option. Nature may invalidate even when retained failure is also offered. At $b=0$, success suffices since $V(T,0)=0$.

Committing $\LC$ changes no potential. Releasing it restores $i$ to $T$, increasing the potential by Lemma~\ref{lem:subset}. Discarding $\LC$ also restores its baseline; discarding $\C$ restores only the baseline. Cached inspection and conditional uncomputed-snapshot acquisition offer a potential-preserving match branch.

Batch acquisition changes no term for selected $\U$ jobs. Its cached subset $K\subseteq\mathcal A(T)$ admits the one-pass policy. Failure set $F$ adds baseline $\sum_{i\in F}w_i$ and leaves active set $(T\setminus K)\cup F$. Equation~\eqref{eq:pass} supplies the witness, also for empty $K$ or zero budget.

Atomic fresh completion uses the protected bound. Cached completion gives $V(T-i,b)$ on a match and $w_i+p_i+V(T-i,b-1)$ on mismatch. Their maximum bounds $V(T,b)$: a feasible policy tries $i$ fast, protects it immediately on failure, then continues optimally on $T-i$. At zero budget a match suffices.

Choose each outcome after observing the action. Telescoping bounds completion cost by the initial potential; noncompletion costs infinity. This covers arbitrary job changes and retained guards. Serial macros give equality.
\end{proof}

Equality concerns the all-unprepared boundary. The potential is only a lower bound at intermediate states: one cached job with $w=c=2$, $p=1$, $b=1$ has $\Phi=1$ but remaining optimum 2. Nature can invalidate its first use, forcing work at least 2; conditional commit followed by an unguarded retry attains 2. Thus $\Phi$ is not an exact state quotient.

\paragraph{Transfer conditions.}
Exact CP/DP policies inherit Theorems~\ref{thm:primitive} and~\ref{thm:writes}. A refinement may restrict guard groups: executable serial macros at stated prices give the upper bound, while equality also needs realizable lower-bound outcomes and consistent shared-guard pricing. Nonnegative holding costs preserve the lower bound; equality needs unchanged witness costs. Positive-cost failed acquisition has a counterexample in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\section{From Failure Tokens to Actual Writes}
\label{sec:java}

Repeated stale rejection needs no new write, and parent writes preserve captured outputs. We bound \emph{actual successful external writes}.

Java 17's \texttt{ConcurrentHashMap} stores persistent keys and immutable identity-equal wrappers. Writes install fresh identities without reuse, since conditional replacement accepts identity or \texttt{equals} equality~\cite{cmap17,chmsource17}. Fast execution reads, computes unprotected and conditionally \texttt{replace}s; protection evaluates inside \texttt{compute}. Java 17 specifies atomicity and exactly one callback invocation; inspected source evaluates/assigns under the bin monitor~\cite{chm17,chmsource17}. Kernels terminate without exceptions, return non-null outputs, and keys are never removed.

Cached fallback prepares outside \texttt{compute}, then reuses a matching identity or recomputes under protection. The exact successful conditional/callback result becomes the milestone, never a later \texttt{map.get}. Calls perform no cross-key map work and hold no foreground guard between invocations. These conditions define the callback subclass; exposed-monitor programs need the general interface contract.

\begin{theorem}[A bound on actual external writes]
\label{thm:writes}
Use persistent keys, immutable fresh identities without reuse, and Section~\ref{sec:primitives}'s opaque-kernel/readiness/captured-output contract, with no initial preparation. A single foreground uses fresh reads, preparation, identity-conditional replacement, free inspection/discard and atomic fresh/cached callbacks. Even cached matches perform no other-key, other-foreground-kernel or direct-guard work; no guard survives a call. Every commit validates or uses a guarded result. Primitives finish, the foreground is scheduled and at most $B$ external writes occur. Minimum worst counted work is
\[
 \min_P\sup_{E:\,\#\mathrm{writes}(E)\le B}\mathrm{Cost}(P,E)
 =\sum_i w_i+V(J,B),
\]
where $w_i$ is the counted cost of a whole kernel, including its captured-parent processing. Noncompletion has infinite cost. This equality holds whether the keys use distinct or shared bins.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix any admissible $P$. Classify a key as $\C$ when it retains a current-identity result, and $\U$ otherwise; copying or retaining stale results creates no fresh capability. At callback boundaries the unfinished keys form upset $T=J\setminus D$. Set
\[
 \Psi(x,\beta)=\sum_{i:x_i=\U}w_i+V(T,\beta),
 \qquad\beta=B-\#\mathrm{writes\ already\ made}.
\]
The environment writes only before a selected conditional comparison/callback acquires its monitor. Selected kernels and valid cached commits are physically ready, hence available in $T$. Writing changes only the selected input identity, preserving captured parents. The foreground holds no guard at this boundary, so writes finish even with colliding bins.

For a valid cached conditional commit, the environment chooses no write or one fresh-identity write. Removing common baseline terms, the successor potentials are $V(T-i,\beta)$ and $w_i+V(T,\beta-1)$. Their maximum bounds $V(T,\beta)$. For a valid cached callback, the second term becomes $w_i+p_i+V(T-i,\beta-1)$, and the cached-fallback inequality in Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive} applies. At $\beta=0$, no write suffices. Fresh preparation pays for removing its $w_i$ baseline; fresh protected completion uses $V(T,\beta)\le p_i+V(T-i,\beta)$. Preparing an old snapshot cannot create a current capability. A stale cached fallback is a fresh protected computation requiring no additional write. Repeated validation of that same stale result is a zero-write stutter, not a new budget charge. Free inspection does not alter this semantic classification; discarding the only current result increases the potential.

Callbacks read captured parents directly, with no other foreground work under incidental protection. Thus other-key effects end at the next boundary. Each operation admits a zero/one-write continuation preserving cost plus $\Psi$, chosen from observed history and current action. At most $B$ writes are inserted; telescoping gives the lower bound.

The emitted controller rereads after every failure. A failed replacement requires an external write between read and comparison; these serial intervals are disjoint. Failures therefore inject into distinct writes, giving at most $B$ failures even with other-key writes. The observed remaining-failure allowance may exceed $\beta$ but remains valid. Serial macros attain the bound in either layout.
\end{proof}

Multiple snapshots are permitted, without unrestricted Java or elapsed-time optimality. For independent guards, $\Phi$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:potential} applies: invalidate a valid cached key before acquisition, or the entire failed batch subset from Equation~\eqref{eq:pass} before its first acquisition. Stale rejection remains distinct from new writes.


\paragraph{Native data dependence and cost.}
Authored kernels fold captured predecessor seeds and every immutable-array entry with fixed integer arithmetic. A separate writer installs fresh wrappers. These generally noncommuting transformations make children depend on actual predecessor results.

To instantiate integer retry prices $(c_i,p_i)$, let $k_i=|\operatorname{Pred}(i)|$ and choose array length $\ell_i=8c_i-k_i>0$ on the selected inputs. One kernel invocation counts $k_i+\ell_i=8c_i$ work units, including every parent-seed fold. Set $d=\operatorname{lcm}_i c_i$ and $q_i=dp_i/c_i$. If $W$ is all foreground kernel work and $L_i$ is that work for job $i$ inside its callback, the objective is
\[
 dW+\sum_i q_iL_i=8d\times\text{the corresponding abstract total cost}.
\]
This uniformly scales the declared work metric, excluding timing, allocation, initialization, monitor/API instructions, background work and scheduling. Shared-bin effects do not multiply $L_i$ by incidentally blocked keys.

\section{Compiling the DAG Game for All Budgets}
\label{sec:curves}

We specialize a reflected recurrence to concave child curves, eliminating explicit budget iteration at each completion ideal without fixing a topological order.

\subsection{The Capped Recurrence}

Fix a nonempty residual set $S$ and a cheapest available fast job $i$. Write
\[
 g_b=V(S-i,b),\qquad
 h_b=\min_{j\in\mathcal A(S)}\{p_j+V(S-j,b)\},\qquad c=c_i.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:priority}, the full Bellman equation reduces to
\begin{equation}
 v_0=0,\qquad v_b=\min\{h_b,\max(g_b,c+v_{b-1})\},\quad b>0.
 \label{eq:caprec}
\end{equation}
Here $h$ can choose different protected jobs across budgets. Equation~\eqref{eq:caprec} is the jump projection in Burdzy et al.~\cite{bkr}, Equations (2.12)--(2.13), with driving path $c\lfloor t\rfloor$ and barriers $g_{\lfloor t\rfloor},h_{\lfloor t\rfloor}$. Their Theorem 2.6 gives the general explicit solution; Equation (2.8) gives the uncapped running maximum in Equation~\eqref{eq:floorconv}. The following slope-floor/cap formula is a short concavity corollary, proved directly below. Write $\Delta f_b=f_b-f_{b-1}$; discrete concavity means nonincreasing differences.

\begin{lemma}[Capped concave recurrence]
\label{lem:cap}
Suppose $g_0=0$, $g,h$ are nonnegative, nondecreasing and discrete concave, and $h_b\ge g_b$ for every $b$. For $c>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 D_0=0,\qquad D_b=\sum_{t=1}^b\max(c,\Delta g_t).
 \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
Then the solution of Equation~\eqref{eq:caprec} is $v_b=\min(h_b,D_b)$, and is nondecreasing and discrete concave. The result permits $h_0>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Concavity gives the identities
\begin{equation}
 D_b=\max_{0\le k\le b}\{g_k+c(b-k)\}
     =\max(g_b,c+D_{b-1}).
 \label{eq:floorconv}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the successive differences of $g_k-ck$ are nonincreasing. Its maximum over a prefix includes exactly its initial positive differences; adding $cb$ gives Equation~\eqref{eq:floor}.

Induct on $b$. If $h_{b-1}\ge D_{b-1}$, the induction hypothesis and Equation~\eqref{eq:floorconv} immediately give $v_b=\min(h_b,D_b)$. Otherwise choose $k$ attaining the maximum for $D_{b-1}$. Since $h_{b-1}\ge g_{b-1}$ but $D_{b-1}>h_{b-1}$, $k<b-1$. From $h_k\ge g_k$ we obtain
\[
 h_{b-1}-h_k<c(b-1-k).
\]
The next difference of concave $h$ is at most that earlier block's average, so $\Delta h_b<c$. Thus $h_b<c+h_{b-1}$ and Equation~\eqref{eq:caprec} selects $h_b$. The same strict block inequality holds for $g$, so its later slopes are below $c$ and $D_b=D_{b-1}+c>h_b$. Hence the formula also holds in this branch. The strict branch cannot occur at $b-1=0$.

The slopes of $D$ are nonnegative and nonincreasing. Pointwise minima preserve nondecreasingness and the discrete concavity inequality: $2f_b\ge f_{b-1}+f_{b+1}\ge\min_f f_{b-1}+\min_f f_{b+1}$, then minimize the left side.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DAG retry-curve identity]
\label{thm:dagcurves}
For every residual set $S$, $V(S,\cdot)$ is a nonnegative nondecreasing discrete-concave sequence. With $g,h,c$ as above,
\begin{equation}
 V(S,b)=\min\left\{
 \min_{j\in\mathcal A(S)}[p_j+V(S-j,b)],\;
 \sum_{t=1}^{b}\max(c_i,\Delta V(S-i,t))\right\}.
 \label{eq:dagcurve}
\end{equation}
Its eventual constant value is $P(S)=\sum_{j\in S}p_j$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on $|S|$, starting from the zero empty-set curve. By induction all shifted child curves are concave and nondecreasing, hence so is their minimum $h$. Subset monotonicity and each feasible protected action give $g_b\le V(S,b)\le h_b$, without presupposing the new formula. Lemma~\ref{lem:cap} therefore applies.

Protecting all jobs bounds the value by $P(S)$. For $b\ge\sum_{j\in S}\lceil p_j/c_j\rceil$, Nature can give each job its allocated failures before fast completion. Each then pays its premium through protection or failures, regardless of ready-job order. This proves eventual equality.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Policy extraction and ties.}
At runtime, compute the protected minimum from the available children. If it equals $V(S,b)$, protection of an attaining job is optimal; otherwise choose the cheapest available fast job. At $b=0$, fast completion is free in residual cost. This is the prototype's protection-first tie policy and uses the full encoded values.

An optimal policy can stay on a fast job until success. Lemma~\ref{lem:cap}'s strict cap $h_{b-1}<D_{b-1}$ implies $h_b<c+h_{b-1}$, making fast suboptimal at $b$. Thus optimal fast at $b$ implies $v_{b-1}=D_{b-1}$ and fast remains optimal. Repeating proves the claim. Fast-first ties give a fast prefix and protected suffix starting at $\tau=\min\{b\ge1:h_b<D_b\}$; its protected job may change.



\subsection{Encoded Curves and the Compiler}

Curve triples $(s,v,r)$ encode $v+r(b-s)$ from integer budget $s$ to the next breakpoint, merging equal adjacent slopes. A completed curve ends in an infinite constant tail.

The compiler enumerates root-reachable upsets and processes every child before its parent. For each nonempty $S$, find available jobs and their cheapest fast job $i$. Shift each child $V(S-j,\cdot)$ by $p_j$ and take their minimum $h$. Replace the suffix of $g=V(S-i,\cdot)$ with slopes at most $c_i$ by one slope-$c_i$ segment, giving $D$. Store $\min(h,D)$, available-job identities and $i$.

Refine profiles at union breakpoints and floor/ceiling neighbors of affine crossings, then minimize and merge equal slopes. Both neighbors capture the one-step slope at a noninteger crossing; tails are included.

For $N$ ideals and $K$ segments, output has $K$ entries and at most $nN$ action identities. Runtime consults child curves with an unfinished-set identifier and budget; dense expansion can still have $(B+1)N$ states.

\paragraph{Checking values and policies.}
The original checker refines at every pairwise affine crossing. A later checker instead sweeps candidate/child breakpoints and candidate breakpoints shifted by one. On each integer interval, subtract the candidate value from every action. Protected actions become affine lines; fast actions become maxima of two lines.

\begin{proposition}[Direct Bellman and policy checking]
\label{prop:sweep}
Require every action difference to be nonnegative, and require the zero sets of all protected actions plus the stored fast action to cover every integer in the interval. Together with complete state/action coverage, valid curve shapes, zero-budget boundaries and constant-tail checks, these conditions certify all values and the protection-first stored policy.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A line's nonnegativity needs only endpoints. A two-line maximum additionally needs the floor/ceiling neighbors of its own crossing. Its zero set is where both lines are nonpositive and at least one is zero; each constituent contributes an integer interval or singleton. Merge these zero sets, accepting adjacent integer intervals even when their continuous interiors differ. Nonnegativity proves no action improves the candidate; coverage proves an allowed policy action attains it. If any protected action attains, the runtime chooses one; otherwise the stored fast action must attain. Bellman induction on $|S|+b$ gives the unique values and optimal policy. The last interval has only constant constituents, so one point covers its infinite tail.
\end{proof}

For one state let $a$ be its ready-job count, $M$ its constituent profile entries (counting its own twice), and $I$ the merged intervals. Heap merging and zero-set sorting cost $O(M\log(a+2)+Ia\log(a+1))$ arithmetic operations, plus shape validation. Materialized boundaries use $O(M+a)$ auxiliary space. The checker imports no constructor and directly checks every fast action, including stored-policy attainment; it trusts arithmetic and the game, not a mechanically verified abstraction.

\begin{theorem}[A supporting basis and tight run bound]
\label{thm:fpt}
For $\mathcal C_S=\{0\}\cup\{c_j:j\in S\}$ and every integer $b\ge0$,
\[
 V(S,b)=\min_{s\in\mathcal C_S}\{\alpha_{S,s}+sb\},
\]
omitting unused slopes, with integer $0\le\alpha_{S,s}\le P(S)$. If $d$ positive costs are distinct in $S$, its sampled profile has at most $2d$ positive runs. The $2n$ bound is tight even on chains. All emitted profiles together have at most $(n+1)2^n$ entries, including constant tails; construction/checking take $2^n\operatorname{poly}(\lambda)$ bit time for binary input length $\lambda$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose a child $g_b=\min_\ell(a_\ell+s_\ell b)$ has such a basis. Let $A=\max_{q\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}}(g_q-cq)$ and $t$ be its first maximizer. It exists because $g$ eventually becomes constant. Concavity gives $\Delta g_k>c$ through $t$ and $\Delta g_k\le c$ afterwards. Hence $D_b$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:floor} equals $g_b$ through $t$ and $cb+A$ thereafter, and
\[
 D_b=\min\{cb+A,\ \min_{\ell:s_\ell>c}(a_\ell+s_\ell b)\}.
\]
For $b<t$, every line attaining $g_b$ has slope above $c$, or it would imply $g_{b+1}\le g_b+c$; also $cb+A\ge g_b$. For $b\ge t$, each retained line satisfies $a_\ell+s_\ell b\ge g_t+s_\ell(b-t)\ge cb+A$. This proves the identity, including $t=0$ and an empty retained set.

The empty curve is the zero line. Protected actions shift child intercepts by their premiums; flooring retains child slopes and adds $c_i$ with $A\in[0,P(S-i)]$. Equation~\eqref{eq:dagcurve} takes their union/minimum. Keeping the smallest intercept per slope closes induction, including zero premiums and repeated costs.

The continuous supporting envelope has at most $d+1$ pieces and $d$ breakpoints. Integer sampling adds at most one bridging unit interval per noninteger breakpoint, giving $2d$ positive runs. Summing $2|S|+1$ over all subsets gives $(n+1)2^n$. Partial protected envelopes obey the same linear bound, so existing profile operations/checks cost polynomial time per ideal. With $P=\sum p_i$, $C=\max(1,\max c_i)$, saturation occurs by $\sum_i\lceil p_i/c_i\rceil\le P$. Starts, values and intercepts are at most $P$, slopes at most $C$; crossing arithmetic uses $O(\log(P+2)+\log(C+2))$ bits, plus $n$-bit masks.

For tightness, take chain jobs $k=1,\ldots,n$ with $c_k=3^k$, $p_k=5\cdot3^{k-1}$. Reverse insertion preserves larger suffix slopes and appends $3^k,2\cdot3^{k-1}$ once each: $2n$ strictly decreasing positive runs.
\end{proof}

The maximum defining $A$ is over integers: for $g_b=\min(3b,5)$, $c=2$, it is 1; maximizing the supporting envelope over reals gives $5/3$ and the wrong $D_2=17/3$ instead of 5. The sampled linear interpolant remains valid. A cover by $w$ chains of lengths $n_\ell$ gives $N\le\prod_\ell(n_\ell+1)$ ideals: polynomial for fixed $w$ (XP, not FPT in width). Two chains of lengths $a,b$ give at most $(n+1)(a+1)(b+1)$ entries by summing over prefix pairs. Arbitrary certificates additionally pay their supplied sizes. A small root profile does not make finding its coefficients easy or prove that ideal enumeration is necessary.

\section{Fixed-Order Compilation and Verification}
\label{sec:independent}

Without edges, protected insertion preserves readiness, removing the ideal dimension.

\begin{theorem}[Independent order normal form]
\label{thm:order}
Fix a total order by nondecreasing $c_i$. For independent jobs, an optimal policy completes in this order, even compared with policies that switch jobs after failures.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $G(S,b)$ be the optimum subject to the sorted completion order. Inserting a protected job $j$ at its sorted position into an optimal policy for $S-j$ changes no other job and consumes no token. Thus $G(S,b)\le p_j+G(S-j,b)$.

Induct on $|S|$ and then $b$; the empty and zero-budget cases are zero. For the first sorted job $i$,
\[
 G(S,b)=\min\{p_i+G(S-i,b),\max[G(S-i,b),c_i+G(S,b-1)]\}.
\]
Induction replaces all children by $V$. Hence $G(S,b)$ is no greater than fast $i$'s value, which Lemma~\ref{lem:priority} bounds by every fast action. Protected insertion and the smaller-set induction bound $G(S,b)$ by every protected action too. Bellman's minimum is therefore at least $G(S,b)$; feasibility gives the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}

Normal costs are paid once. For $(p,c)=(4,1),(3,2)$, $B=2$, increasing-$c$ order gives residual 3 versus 4 for increasing $p/c$.

\subsection{Suffix Deficits and Slope Packing}
\label{sec:compiler}

For a suffix curve $f$ with $f_0=0$, the current job induces
\begin{equation}
 (R_{p,c}f)_0=0,\qquad
 (R_{p,c}f)_b=\min\{p+f_b,\max[f_b,c+(R_{p,c}f)_{b-1}]\}.
 \label{eq:operator}
\end{equation}
Write $r_b=f_b-f_{b-1}$. For concave $f$, Theorem~\ref{thm:dagcurves} specializes to
\begin{equation}
 (R_{p,c}f)_b=f_b+\min\left\{p,\sum_{k=1}^b\pos{c-r_k}\right\}.
 \label{eq:deficit}
\end{equation}
Subtracting $f_b$ from the slope-floor/cap formula gives Equation~\eqref{eq:deficit}, for any prescribed serial order.

\begin{theorem}[Persistent compilation for a prescribed order]
\label{thm:persistent}
Fix a topological order $\pi$ and integer prices $c_i>0,p_i\ge0$. Its optimal serial retry-macro excess profiles for all integer budgets and earliest protection thresholds admit a persistent certificate with $O(n\log(n+1))$ nodes. Construction and checking take $O(n\log(n+1))$ arithmetic operations after input validation; the checking bound concerns generated artifacts. For a unique topological order these values equal $V$ and transfer through Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive} under its premises. A prescribed order alone establishes no retained-preparation restriction theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Starting with the zero suffix, write $s_b=f_b-f_{b-1}$. For $p>0$, let $a$ count initial positions with $s_b\ge c$, and set $\Delta(b)=c(b-a)-(f_b-f_a)$ for $b\ge a$. Subsequent slopes are below $c$, including the zero tail, so the first crossing satisfies $t>a$ and $\Delta(t-1)<p\le\Delta(t)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:deficit} retains slopes through $a$, uses $c$ on $a+1,\ldots,t-1$, places $z=s_t+p-\Delta(t-1)$ at $t$, and retains later slopes. Here $s_t<z\le c$ and support is $\max(\operatorname{length}(f),t)$. Premium zero preserves the curve, sets $t=0$ and reuses the identical root. Protection-first ties choose protection for $b\ge t$ and fast otherwise; failure keeps the job below its threshold.

Store canonical positive runs in immutable AVL trees with subtree lengths and areas. Deficit searches and constantly many height-sensitive splits/joins replace intervals, share untouched subtrees and coalesce boundaries. Each prepend adds at most two runs, so height and fresh-node allocation are $O(\log(n+1))$. Keep every suffix root and serialize each reachable node once.

The verifier checks the order, prices, decreasing runs, balance, structural aggregates and backward references, with a zero terminal root. For $p=0$ it checks equality and threshold zero. Otherwise it recomputes crossing conditions, support and four ranges: unchanged prefix, plateau, singleton and unchanged suffix. Exact comparison skips identical node IDs, compares run fragments, and otherwise expands a subtree, choosing the longer one when both fronts are subtrees. It uses no probabilistic equality test.

For generated adjacent roots let $h$ be maximum height and $K$ count fresh nodes. Range decomposition exposes $O(h)$ subtree/run chunks, including at most two partially clipped runs. Contract aligned common subtrees inside each unchanged range. Such a subtree is never expanded: its counterpart is identical or inside a longer front ancestor, which expands first. Fresh/boundary structure has $O(K+h)$ nodes; canonical run equality bounds exposed old-side runs, and binary branching bounds common atoms. Each comparison costs $O(K+h)=O(\log(n+1))$. Structural checking and suffix induction complete the proof.
\end{proof}

Validation adds $O(|E|+n\log(n+1))$ work. Bounds assume amortized/expected constant dictionary access and charge integer operand lengths. With $P=\sum p_i$, $C=\max(1,\max c_i)$, integers need $O(\log(P+1)+\log(C+1)+\log(n+1))$ bits: lengths/thresholds are at most $P$, slopes at most $C$. Budget storage separately needs $O(\log(B+2))$ bits. Value queries use $O(\log(n+1))$ and cursor decisions $O(1)$ arithmetic operations.

Arbitrary certificates pay supplied size and actual chunk visits: alternating disjoint copies of one $k$-run profile across $k$ zero-premium roots causes $\Theta(k^2)$ work despite $O(k\log k)$ size. Generation counters do not certify provenance.

\paragraph{Sorted slope packing.}
Insert sorted jobs in reverse order. All positive slopes except possibly the final singleton are at least the next $c$. Premium zero changes nothing. Otherwise raise a final singleton $r<c$ by $\min(p,c-r)$ and deduct that amount. If premium remains, write it as $qc+s$, $0\le s<c$, and append $q$ slopes $c$ and, if $s>0$, one slope $s$. Merge equal boundaries. The resulting support is the positive-premium threshold; each insertion uses constantly many run operations.

The order theorem, deficit identity and packing invariant give the exact root curve and thresholds in $O(n\log n)$ arithmetic and $O(n)$ entries: sorting dominates constantly many run operations per insertion. A cursor advances on success and a budget counter decreases on failure.

\subsection{Cost-Compatible DAGs and Shared Certificates}
\label{sec:compatible}

Suppose $c_u\le c_v$ for every edge $u\to v$. Minimum-$c$ Kahn traversal gives a nondecreasing-cost topological order: any smaller unfinished job has a ready ancestor of no greater cost. Equal-cost ties must respect dependencies. Removing the edges gives an independent lower bound; the sorted optimum attains it legally in the DAG for every $B$. Dispatching this class to packing costs $O(|E|+n\log n)$ arithmetic operations, with $O(n)$ policy entries in addition to the input graph. Otherwise, unique-order DAGs use persistent compilation; remaining DAGs retain the ideal compiler for unrestricted optimality.

The packed output also admits a small all-budget certificate. Index sorted positions from zero, let $P_k=\sum_{j\ge k}p_j$, and let $F$ be the root curve. Each insertion adds premium mass only at the positive marginal sequence's right end, possibly raising its last singleton. Removing that mass restores the previous curve, so every suffix is
\begin{equation}
 f_k(b)=\min\{F(b),P_k\}.\label{eq:suffixcap}
\end{equation}
Instead of trusting packing, a verifier can treat these caps as candidates and check their Bellman equations directly.

For a root with $r$ positive runs, direct cap checking costs $O(n\log(r+1)+n+|E|+r)$ arithmetic. Check the order/prices, descending runs, $F(0)=0$, final value $P_0$ and zero terminal suffix. For each $p_k>0$, set $a=P_{k+1}$, $z=P_k$, $u=\min\{b:F(b)\ge a\}$, $v=\min\{b:F(b)\ge z\}$, taking $u=0$ for $a=0$, and $\delta_b=F(b)-F(b-1)$. Verify
\begin{align*}
 \delta_{u-1}&\ge c &&(u\ge2),\\
 \min(F(u),z)&=\min(z,\max(a,c+F(u-1))) &&(u\ge1),\\
 \delta_{\max(1,u+1)}&=\delta_{v-1}=c &&(\max(1,u+1)\le v-1),\\
 z&\le c+F(v-1).
\end{align*}
These are Bellman before $u$, at $u$, between $u,v$ and at saturation $v$. Concavity extends the marginal endpoint checks across each interval; later protection gives the constant cap. Zero budget is checked separately. Require threshold $v$, where protection first attains; a zero-premium job has identical adjacent caps and threshold zero. Suffix induction proves all values/policies. Prefix lengths/areas answer the constant checks per job by binary search, yielding the bound. The checker imports no generator and trusts arithmetic, the game and the order theorem.

\subsection{Causal Control and Inherited Bounds}
\label{sec:bounds}

For a protected set $H$ fixed before execution, the exact worst residual value is
\begin{equation}
 U_H(B)=\sum_{i\in H}p_i+B\max_{i\notin H}c_i,\qquad
 U(B)=\min_{t\in\{0,c_1,\ldots,c_n\}}\left\{\sum_{c_i>t}p_i+Bt\right\}.
 \label{eq:fixed}
\end{equation}
The empty maximum is zero. Nature spends all tokens on a largest-cost fast job; for threshold $t$, protect exactly $c_i>t$. Sorting gives $U$ in $O(n\log n)$ arithmetic, also with precedence using a topological order.

The clairvoyant allocation lower bound is
\begin{equation}
 L(B)=\max_{k_i\in\N,\;\sum_i k_i\le B}\sum_i\min(p_i,k_ic_i).
 \label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
Nature fails each job's first $k_i$ fast attempts, forcing its premium or allocated failures, so $L$ bounds every DAG policy. Classical concave allocation~\cite{federgruen} sorts each job's $\lfloor p_i/c_i\rfloor$ full $c_i$-chunks and one remainder. Binary multiplicities give $O(n\log n)$ arithmetic/$O(n)$ runs; this and related robust budget calculations~\cite{bertsimas} are inherited.

The bounds can differ: $(p,c)=(2,5),(4,6)$ gives $L(1)=4<V(1)=5<U(1)=6$. Divisible premiums make independent packing sort only full chunks, giving $V=L$; precedence need not preserve this. Every adversarial path of a fixed deterministic controller is reproducible by its per-job failure counts. The gap from $L$ is causal versus advance-informed control.

All 44,838 shared-checker units have expected outcomes, including 20,758 adversarial certificates with valid metadata (74 accepted/20,684 rejected). \paragraph{Specified-budget alternatives.}
At $B=1$, Equation~\eqref{eq:onefailure} gives an exact CP model: protection occupies a $p_i$-length interval; fast execution is a point on the premium clock. Precedence uses interval ends; fast points impose $t_i+c_i\le K$, with total premium at most $K$. During certificate extraction, a point inside an interval moves before it.

At $B=2$, let $q_f$ be protected premium before a first failure at $f$ on a root order, leaving upset $R_f$ including $f$. Attach a $B=1$ certificate of value $C_f$ for each root fast job. The tree costs
\[
 \max\left\{\sum_{i\in H}p_i,\quad\max_{f\notin H}(q_f+c_f+C_f)\right\}.
\]
Every tree is a legal policy; every adaptive policy has one no-failure order and such first-failure continuations. Thus minimizing is exact. Full permutations, order/protection conjunctions and active-remaining flags yield $O(n^3)$ CP size, not polynomial solve time. OR-Tools 9.15.6755~\cite{cpsat} runs one worker with a four-second solve cap; the $B=2$ model also uses $L/U$ and fixed-mode hints.

A screened DP combines $L$ with optimal modes for cheapest-available/ID-first orders. Equal bounds finish immediately; child lower bounds and Lemma~\ref{lem:priority} otherwise prune actions. Separate scanners evaluate emitted fixed-order/branching policies; optimality also trusts the formulation/solver.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Quantitative and fault-tolerant synthesis.}
\v{C}ern\'y et al.~\cite{cerny} synthesize synchronization, including optimistic retries and guards/caches. Their Theorem~6 matches distributions/weights to preserve limit-average values; our potential gives initial total-cost equality and intermediate lower bounds. Their erratum supersedes the original hardness claim. Dumitrescu et al.~\cite{dumitrescu} minimize worst fault cost in weighted reachability, expressing finite instances here. Resilience and cost/resilience synthesis are established~\cite{neider,weinert}.

\v{C}ern\'y et al.~\cite{cernyquality}, Sections 6.1--6.2, also optimize protected-statement counts and use profiled protected-block costs in a contention/lock-acquisition performance model. Our premiums are declared, without runtime calibration. Finite shared failures, persistent completion and encoded causal retry/order policies require comparison beyond the established concern for protected computation.

\paragraph{Concurrent-program reduction.}
Gangamreddypalli et al.~\cite{structured}, Theorem~1, preserve original failures and reach each original successful final state or replace it by failure (P2). Here snapshot read/conditional commit do not commute unconditionally with same-key writes, and $\LC$ release restores a virtual job by inequality. A cost-preserving static/dynamic reduction remains unestablished.

\paragraph{Exact robust dynamic programming.}
Mannor et al.~\cite{mannor}, Section 4/Theorem 5, supply Equation~\eqref{eq:bellman} on unfinished upsets with negated costs and horizon $n+B$: fast completes freely or spends one token and costs $c_i$ on a self-loop; protection completes for $p_i$. Only uncertainty-segment endpoints matter; token-charged nominal outcomes are dominated. State/budget expansion remains explicit.

Postek et al.~\cite{postek} provide exact finite-candidate DP, state expansion, causal budgeted routing and appendix reformulations (Propositions~1--2, Remarks~1--2, Section~5.1). Their code enumerates budgets/reuses saturation; our concavity specialization and primitive abstraction need separate justification.

\paragraph{Parametric robust optimization and allocation.}
Bertsimas and Sim~\cite{bertsimas2003}, Theorem~3/Equations~(15)--(19), solve additive-deviation robust binary optimization through $n+1$ nominal problems. Their $Z(\Gamma)=\min_\ell\{\Gamma d_\ell+F_\ell\}$ has budget-independent $F_\ell$ and only $d+1$ distinct-deviation cases: an inherited input-slope envelope under combinatorial constraints. Repeated failures and causal protection/order require a preserving fixed-binary-set reduction; none is established. A DAG or concavity alone is no distinction.

Classical separable-concave allocation~\cite{federgruen,bertsimas} supplies the inexpensive bound $L$, exact on the divisible independent family. Decision-dependent information and controllable uncertainty are established broader settings~\cite{vayanos,ley}.

\paragraph{Scheduling and hardening.}
At $B=1$, coupled processing/tail modes $(p_i,0)$ or $(0,c_i)$ minimize $\max_i(C_i+\text{tail}_i)$; precedence constrains machine completion, leaving tails overlapping. Fixed modes fall under Lawler's~\cite{lawler} sequencing result. Our hardness concerns mode choices, without priority over every scheduling variant.

Izosimov et al.~\cite{izosimov} activate offline DAG schedules from bounded-failure histories, with nonpreemption, signalling and transparency. An and Yang~\cite{an} synthesize stochastic hardening under probabilistic $(m,k)$ requirements/fixed priorities. We omit these constraints. Chrobak et al.~\cite{chrobak}, Lemma~4/Theorem~5, concentrate $\mathrm{NUM}_k$ faults on a target-dependent critical job, giving prescribed-order latest completion times in $O(n)$ independent of $k$. Their release/deadline model mandates retries; we select premium-priced protection and emit all-budget policies/certificates for counted work, without release/deadline constraints.

Bold and Goerigk~\cite{bold}, Section~3, fix conflict precedences, observe all durations, then choose start times using $\Gamma+1$ DAG layers/a compact MIP. Activities complete despite delay; our failures retain an unfinished state before another causal decision. Transfer needs these semantics matched.

\paragraph{Rent-or-buy ordering.}
Multi-commodity thresholds~\cite{wu} and general rental/submodular purchase costs~\cite{li} establish heterogeneous ordering; Li et al.'s Lemma~3 uses purchase/rental ratios. The direct offline map $f(H)=\sum_{i\in H}p_i$, $g(H)=\sum_{i\in H}c_i$, $T=B$ gives 6 for $(p,c)=(2,5),(4,6)$, $B=1$, versus our 5. Other reductions remain unexcluded.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Contract sensitivity.}
Optimality requires fixed opaque kernels, immutable parents, persistent completion and own-key invalidation. It excludes partial/parallel evaluation, external discharge, replenished budgets, structural changes and unmodeled exceptions.

Restricted outcomes retain the upper guarantee but can break optimality. With $a_1=a_2=1$, $(p_1,c_1)=(10,1)$, $(p_2,c_2)=(10,10)$, $B=1$, let job 2 fail only after job 1 completes. Increasing-$c$ completion then costs 12, versus 3 in reverse order. A free guaranteed operation certifying that every future writer has finished instead reduces the optimum to baseline work. Finite writer counts do not exclude this certificate; a thread count or rate is not a total-write bound.

\paragraph{A refuted broader acquisition model.}
Charged API work and positive-cost failed acquisition let retained guards improve five of 384 earlier inputs, including unrestricted 242 versus macro 243. These counterexamples remain for that contract. Nonnegative holding costs preserve the lower bound; equality needs unchanged serial-witness costs.

\paragraph{Meaning of the evaluation.}
Native runs cover recorded paths; curve checking trusts the game. Finite checks provide no mechanical proof or independent review. Strongest-comparator gains are sparse; CP/DP sometimes avoid timeouts, while the value oracle fares worse. Applicability and stronger theory/comparator coverage remain open.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Under the preparation contract, optimal serial retry policies decide counted-work caps. Actual-write correspondence connects them to execution. General synthesis remains hard, with XP ideal enumeration at fixed width; unique-order and cost-compatible DAGs admit efficient compilation and generated-certificate checking.
