# Exact competitive synthesis with a common publication baseline

Author proof, 2026-09-10. This extends only the specified partial-repair interface in PLAN.md. It is not a theorem about arbitrary source transformations, elapsed time, or Roslyn merge costs.

Let U be m positively weighted components and Γ a fixed nonempty family of nonempty subsets of U. A write changes the versions of its footprint; the observable dirty set in one preparation-to-call interval is the union of the footprints in that interval. Repeated writes are possible, and after rejection all components are prepared again outside protection. There are at most q calls. Accepting dirty set D repairs those components and publishes the whole job; its protected loss is μ+w(D). A fresh whole protected call costs μ+W, where W=w(U). Every mode pays the same μ≥0 exactly once at successful publication. Neither failed calls nor outside preparation add protected loss. We consider deterministic controllers observing dirty sets and their own history, without B or write counts.

Write c(D) for the minimum number of Γ footprints whose union is exactly D, and let S be the family of all reachable unions, including ∅ with c(∅)=0. The previous proof gives G(b)=max{w(D):c(D)≤b} and the known-budget optimum Kμ,q(B)=μ+G(floor(B/q)); adding a constant to every terminating path shifts the minimax optimum by that constant. In particular, adding μ does not change the criterion for one controller to be exact for all B. It changes the competitive objective because its zero denominator disappears.

For a deterministic controller P define R(P)=sup_B sup_{environment with ≤B writes} L(P)/Kμ,q(B). Use 0/0=1 and positive/0=∞. This convention does not change a finite supremum in this problem; the B=0 environment already forces ratio at least one for μ>0, and a repeated positive footprint forces at least one when μ=0.

**Lemma 1 (least consistent budget).** An observed terminating history h=(D1,...,Dt) has least consistent budget e(h)=Σ c(Di). Every real environment yielding h spends at least e(h) writes. Conversely, concatenate a shortest footprint word for each Di in its own preparation interval; this yields h with exactly e(h) writes. A deterministic controller makes the same actions because its observations agree. The numerator depends only on the accepted last dirty set, while Kμ,q(B) is nondecreasing. Thus the greatest ratio of all environments producing h is attained at B=e(h). Extra hidden repeated writes or writes with empty footprint cannot make this ratio worse. Taking the supremum over all histories and B is therefore equivalent to taking the maximum over histories weighted by their least consistent budget. This is a worst-case analysis; e is a controller-computable lower bound, not an observation of actual B.

**Lemma 2 (finite sufficient state).** Every reachable D has c(D)≤m: a shortest union word can contain no footprint that adds no new component. Hence e(h)≤qm. After a rejection, preparation erases old dirtiness. The allowed future dirty sets and their minimum write costs are again precisely S and c, independently of the past. Conditional on remaining calls left and e(h), every future ratio depends only on future observations. Thus (left,e) suffices, with the observed D added for the current response.

**Lemma 3 (fresh dominance and zero observations).** Preparing outside then accepting any dirty set uses one call and loss at most μ+W, so fresh execution cannot improve the competitive objective: for each such history the loss is bounded by the fresh loss at the same or a larger consistent budget. Accepting D=∅ is also weakly dominant. It incurs μ at the current e. If the controller instead retries, the environment can give ∅ on every remaining call, forcing the same loss and denominator; no retry can improve on immediate acceptance for this observation.

**Theorem (compiler).** For each original q, set

a(e,D)=(μ+w(D))/(μ+G(floor((e+c(D))/q))).

For one call left, V(1,e)=max_{D∈S} a(e,D). For left>1,

V(left,e)=max_{D∈S} min{a(e,D), V(left−1,e+c(D))},

where D=∅ may be forced to accept. The value V(q,0) is the minimum competitive ratio over all legal deterministic B-unaware controllers. An optimal controller is obtained by selecting a minimizing action after observing D.

Proof. By Lemmas 1–3 all relevant histories form a finite alternating tree: the environment selects an observed D and the controller selects accept or retry. On the last call it must accept. Acceptance terminates with a(e,D); retry moves to the state in the recurrence. Backward induction gives both a realizing policy and a matching worst history for every state, so the displayed max/min order is necessary and sufficient. The fixed q in the denominator must not be replaced by left: the comparator's original call cap does not shrink after the tested controller retries. Lemma 2 permits merging states with the same (left,e) without revealing any extra information. □

Given S,c,G, the table has O(q²m) states and O(q²m|S|) transitions, with exact rational arithmetic when weights and μ are rational. Computing the union family can be exponential in m and general maximum coverage is not a polynomial-time primitive. This is not a polynomial algorithm in a compact arbitrary footprint input.

**Example and scope.** For three unit components, singleton footprints, q=3 and μ=2, retry-first has ratio (μ+3)/(μ+1)=5/3. Accept zero or one dirty component on either of the first two calls; retry larger dirty sets; repair on the last call. A first-call one-component acceptance has ratio3/2. After one rejection the budget lower bound is at least two, so a second-call one-component acceptance has budget at least three and ratio at most1. After two rejections the lower bound is at least four: a last dirty set of size1/2/3 has ratio at most1,1,5/4 respectively. Zero-dirty acceptance has ratio at most1. This policy therefore attains3/2. Conversely give one dirty component on each of the first two calls. Accepting at either call has ratio3/2, since the least budgets are1 and2. If both calls are rejected, give all three dirty components on the last call; B=5 and ratio5/3. Fresh execution is dominated by Lemma3. Thus3/2 is optimal without relying on the finite checker. For μ=0, finite ratio again forces zero loss for B<q; the prior retry-first optimality and q bound apply.

The 20,920-row finite check finds only six strict improvements in its declared grid; 20,914 ties are preserved. All six are singleton three-component equal-weight cases with q=3 or4 and a sufficiently large positive baseline among the tested values. This is a constructed policy-selection consequence. It does not imply that μ is large, known, or even constant in a real application, and it does not settle nearest-work novelty or FSE-level practical importance.
