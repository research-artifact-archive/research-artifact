\title[Controller-Preserving Update Synthesis]{Controller-Preserving Dynamic Update Synthesis from Local Contracts}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Verified controllers leave the update between them unspecified. Fine-Grained Dynamic Update Controller Synthesis (FG-DUCS) generates the intervening behavior from local transfers and requirement lifetimes for fixed endpoint controllers. We prove that the generated game represents all contract histories and preserves the selected new controller's continuation. Lazy synthesis returns a safe finite-completion certificate covering every old entry and every outcome, or proves impossibility. A mandatory-command obstruction identifies choices needed for feasibility; rolling batches of $k$ among $n$ replicas keeping $m$ ready are feasible iff $k\le n-m$. Five constructed families yield \VThreeESixWitness{}/\VThreeESixMainPairs{} fine-WIN/merged-LOSS comparisons. Lazy decides \LazyDecisions{}/\ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts within budget versus \DirectFullDecisions{} with full construction; independent updates separate $n+1$ from $2^n$ explored states under the default order. Guarantees concern declared scopes and finite event executions with quiescent commands/handover; deployment requires a conforming adapter.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Verified endpoints leave their intervening update unspecified~\cite{Cheng2009SASRoadmap,Andersson2013SEAMSProcesses,Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate,Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates}. An update may be requested while a workpiece remains in a cell: replacing empty stations must preserve that workpiece throughout the change. Replacement can therefore require ordinary movement and overlapping old/new obligations. Every old entry needs safe handover despite all outcomes; safety alone permits infinite execution.

Fine-Grained Dynamic Update Controller Synthesis (FG-DUCS) generates an update game from local replacements and requirement lifetimes (Figure~\ref{fig:contract-pipeline}). Synthesis returns a completing policy, rank and compatible new-controller state, or a losing region. This separates specifying obligations from enumerating mixed configurations; all queried outcomes remain represented.
\input{figures/contract_pipeline_c30.tex}

Dynamic Update Controller Synthesis (DUCS) synthesizes update and subsequent control from global transfers and transition requirements~\cite{Nahabedian2016SEAMS,Nahabedian2020}. Meeting a new specification can still remove supplied-controller behavior. FG-DUCS preserves every continuation of the selected new-controller state, which need not be its original initial state. A constructive Cell encoding relates the interfaces (Section~\ref{sec:ducs-cell-construction}); established finite-game reasoning solves the generated contract.

\par\noindent\textbf{C1: Controller-preserving construction.} We prove that composing local transfers and requirement lifetimes preserves contract histories and the selected endpoint's continuation behavior (Section~\ref{sec:formalization}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C2: Certified synthesis.} We give a terminating, sound and complete lazy procedure covering all entries and outcomes, with checkable WIN/LOSS certificates and a conditional connection to execution (Sections~\ref{sec:algorithm} and \ref{sec:interpretation-execution}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C3: Structural boundaries.} A mandatory-command obstruction explains merged losses; Rolling gives an exact threshold, and independent updates separate default-order $n+1$ from $2^n$ states (Sections~\ref{thm:lazy-separation} and \ref{sec:rq2}).
\par\noindent\textbf{C4: Traceable evidence.} Source-to-monitor and handover checks accompany \ApplicationContracts{} adapted contracts and \VThreeESixMainPairs{} controlled comparisons, including \VThreeESixWitness{} granularity separations (Sections~\ref{sec:monitor-meaning}--\ref{sec:rq3}).

The evaluation follows three obstacles: retaining feasibility with local rather than merged commands (RQ1), safe finite completion from every entry and outcome (RQ2), and avoiding full mixed-state construction (RQ3).

Commands and handover require uncontrollable (UC) quiescence. Guarantees cover finite-state safety lifetimes and finite completion; old liveness, service rates and unvalidated deployment behavior are excluded.

\section{Synthesizing and Checking an Update Policy}
Lazy explores products on demand using finite reachability reasoning~\cite{Cimatti1998StrongPlanning,TripakisAltisen1999,CiolekEtAl2023OTFDCS}.
\label{sec:algorithm}

A \emph{bucket} is $\Post(q,a)$; an empty bucket disables $a$. At a UC-enabled state, disabling controls retains a successor and only removes runs; a winner already handles every UC outcome. This preserves winning (Lemma~\ref{lem:uc-priority}).

\subsection{Lazy Successor Generation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:otf} constructs a reachability attractor~\cite{CassezEtAl2005TimedGames}. It tracks discovered states $V$, states $X\subseteq V$ with all UC candidates queried, and proved winners $W$. States in $X$ may retain unqueried controls; $W$ may omit winners.

Delaying other control events retains all outcomes of the proving choice (Figure~\ref{fig:lazy-choices}).
\input{figures/lazy_choices.tex}

Expansion queries every UC candidate. If none is enabled, it queries controls through the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion; $\mathsf{Retry}$ resumes unfinished cursors. Each query retains all outcomes. Candidates include local events and precedence-enabled commands in any fixed finite order.

\emph{Promote.} A safe expanded state wins when its nonempty proving set---all UC successors, or one control bucket at quiescence---lies in $W$; its rank is one above their maximum. Reverse incidences trigger predecessor reconsideration. Failure requires exhausted candidates and a promotion fixed point. Once all entries win, extraction selects one lower-rank control bucket at each reached quiescent non-goal, none elsewhere; it need not follow promotion or minimize length.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Lazy}
\label{alg:otf}
\footnotesize
\KwIn{Typed update contract (Section~\ref{sec:formalization})}
\KwOut{$(\sigma,\rank,\kappa)$ or $(\textnormal{no solution},L)$}
$V\leftarrow Q_0$; $X,\mathsf{Retry}\leftarrow\emptyset$; $W\leftarrow V\cap\Goal$\;
$\mathsf{Open}\leftarrow\{q\in Q_0\mid\Safe(q)\land q\notin\Goal\}$; set $\rank(q)\leftarrow0$ for $q\in W$\;
\While{true}{
  Add queued goals to $W$ once with rank zero; exhaust \emph{Promote}, retaining witnesses/ranks, removing winners from Retry and propagating predecessors\;
  \lIf{$Q_0\subseteq W$}{\Return $(\sigma,\rank|_{\Reach_\sigma(Q_0)},\kappa)$ constructed from the goal witnesses and retained lower-rank choices}
  \eIf{$\mathsf{Open}\neq\emptyset$}{
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Open}$, add it to $X$, and query all uncontrollable candidates\;
    Close the control cursor if UC-enabled; otherwise initialize it and query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion\;
    Insert $q$ into $\mathsf{Retry}$ iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }{
    \lIf{$\mathsf{Retry}=\emptyset$}{\Return $(\textnormal{no solution},L\leftarrow V\setminus W)$}
    Remove $q$ from $\mathsf{Retry}$; query to the next nonempty bucket or exhaustion; reinsert iff $q\notin W$ and its cursor has remaining candidates\;
  }
  Queue newly discovered goals for rank-zero insertion; put newly discovered safe non-goals in $\mathsf{Open}$\;
}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Why the guarantee holds.}
Every discovered safe non-goal is either waiting for expansion or has all UC candidates queried; every expanded unproved quiescent state with candidates left remains in $\mathsf{Retry}$. Promotion inserts only safe states with a complete nonempty proving set in $W$, assigning a larger rank. These invariants give safe, nonblocking finite completion on success. At failure, exhausted queues and promotion leave an entry in $L=V\setminus W$: every safe state there has a UC outcome in $L$, or every enabled control has an outcome in $L$. The environment can therefore defeat every policy, including history-dependent ones. Finitely many states/candidates ensure termination. Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:source_synthesis} gives the full proof and monitor-interval induction.

\subsection{Why Local Choices Need Lazy Exploration}
\label{sec:exploration-unit}
With indexed predicates and constant-time bookkeeping, output-sensitive exploration is linear in discovered states, queried candidates and returned outcomes. This excludes endpoint preprocessing and Java's sorting/entry scans. Lazy explores a subgraph of Direct-Full but may reach its whole graph (Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:exploration_order}; S2).

\paragraph{An exponential separation family.}
\label{thm:lazy-separation}
For $n\geq1$, take $n$ components with one old state, one new state and singleton transfers. Each version and endpoint has a controllable $tick$ self-loop. No monitors, precedence or UC events occur; the unique new endpoint is the target. Every transferred subset is reachable: Direct-Full discovers $2^n$ states. Lazy orders transfers before $tick$ and Open before Retry, discovering one $n$-step chain and its goal. Ranks $0,\ldots,n$ propagate before alternatives are retried: exactly $n+1$ states. Self-loops cannot prove completion. Both use the same goal; the comparison concerns explicit exploration, not symbolic representations.

\subsection{RQ1: Can Local Choices Change Feasibility?}
\label{sec:claim-granularity}
\label{sec:rq2}
Cell and Policy isolate component and requirement-lifetime obstructions (Section~\ref{sec:example}). We next examine availability mechanisms and controlled comparisons.

\paragraph{What is merged.}
Partition transfers, old stops and new starts separately into blocks. A transfer block $B\subseteq I$ simultaneously permits all targets in $\prod_{i\in B}g_i(s_i)$; a boundary block removes or initializes its monitors at the same component tuple. Other active monitors compose member-label effects, checked to commute pairwise on every monitor/residual state. Mapped precedence must remain irreflexive and acyclic. Ordinary behavior, entries, endpoints, relations, initializers and targets stay fixed; quiescence and all member guards apply. These are comparison contracts, not DUCS encodings.

The following obstruction explains the Cell, Policy and PC2 merges directly; the rolling threshold then gives an exact quantitative boundary.
\input{technical_fragments/granularity_obstruction.tex}

\paragraph{Mechanisms and scope.}
Readiness/rejoining gates motivate staged replacement~\cite{e6_kubernetes_deployments,e6_mongodb_enterprise_replica_set}. Rolling begins with $n$ ready replicas and no spares; its interval requirement keeps at least $m$ ready. Each transfer boots its replicas; each has one UC readiness event and no waiting loop. Service self-loops are controllable. The target is all-new and ready.

\begin{proposition}[Rolling threshold]\label{prop:rolling-threshold}
For $n\ge2$, $1\le m<n$ and $1\le k\le n$, fixed consecutive batches of size $k$ (with a smaller final remainder) admit safe finite completion iff $k\le n-m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Some mandatory batch has size $k$. Its transfer leaves at most $n-k$ ready replicas, so $k>n-m$ violates the interval. Otherwise, transfer one batch only when all replicas are ready, disable service, and retain every UC readiness outcome before the next transfer. Readiness never falls below $n-k\ge m$. With $u$ old and $b$ booting replicas, $2u+b$ strictly decreases on every selected transfer and every UC event, reaching zero exactly at the target. Thus all maximal runs finish without fairness.
\end{proof}

The 15 Rolling separations instantiate Proposition~\ref{prop:rolling-threshold}; the \VThreeESixRollingThresholdCells{} measured threshold cells are separate. Canary bounds reported broken replicas by $n-m$, with UC healthy/broken reports and recovery: all \VThreeESixCanaryPairs{}/\VThreeESixCanaryPairs{} pairs separate. Pending reports do not count as reported failures; this bound is not a continuous service-availability guarantee. Restricting outcomes to healthy wins both ways; removing recovery loses both ways. DB-Rolling preserves availability by replacing secondaries first; its $m=n=3$ no-slack control loses both ways. Threads has bounded worker queues: backpressure permits quiescence (both WIN), saturated UC arrivals prevent it (both LOSS). Rolling+Audit retains an old audit until its replacement starts; singleton boundary merging preserves behavior, transfer merging loses. Policy is defined in Section~\ref{sec:example}; Technical Appendix~\ref{ta:constructed_contracts} specifies the other finite inputs.





\textbf{Answer.} The main suite yields \VThreeESixWitness{}/\VThreeESixMainPairs{} fine-WIN/merged-LOSS comparisons, in Rolling, Canary, Policy, DB-Rolling and Rolling+Audit. These families share capacity constraints and additionally exercise recovery, boundary compatibility and role/quiescence. Threads supplies both-WIN and both-LOSS controls (Table~\ref{tab:rq2-family-counts}). Each obstruction identifies choices lost by the specified merge.
\input{figures/rq2_family_counts.tex}

\paragraph{Source obligations through handover.}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\label{sec:expressiveness}
DUCS already preserves legality and deadlock freedom and completes updates from every old-controller state~\cite[Defs.~4.1--4.3, Sec.~4.2]{Nahabedian2020}, synthesizing update and subsequent control together. FG reuses the supplied new controller and preserves its continuation traces at the selected state (Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-continuation}); Section~\ref{sec:ducs-cell-construction} compares the interfaces.





PROTEOS filters states/interfaces before atomic replacement~\cite[Secs.~4.3--4.6]{Giuffrida2013SafeLiveUpdate}; workflow updating blocks tasks for safe updates~\cite[Secs.~4.3, 5.2]{Sokolowski2022DynamicWorkflowUpdates}; Kitsune migrates whole C programs~\cite[Sec.~2]{e6_hayden2014_kitsune}. FG-DUCS synthesizes ordinary steps, local transfers and requirement boundaries together; rank bounds events, not real time.



O-DUCS prioritizes updates and separates update/post-update exploration~\cite{HiranoEtAl2024ODUCS}. GR(1) updating preserves old then new safety and new justice under new-environment assumptions~\cite{Amram2022GR1DynamicUpdate}; Live Synthesis retains unfinished obligations~\cite{FinkbeinerEtAl2022LiveSynthesis}. RS builds on requirement scopes~\cite{Dwyer1999Patterns} and residual rewriting~\cite{RosuHavelund2005Rewriting}.

State attraction bounds transitions to startup under weak invariance~\cite{NooruldeenSchmidt2015}. Dynamic-feature~\cite{ThuijsmanReniers2024DynamicFeature}, compositional~\cite{MohajeraniMalikFabian2014Compositional} and modular~\cite{WonhamRamadge1988Modular} control address specification structure. OTF-DCS requires marked extensions of controlled finite traces~\cite[Sec.~3]{CiolekEtAl2023OTFDCS}; FG requires finite quiescent handover on every maximal run without fairness. Performance comparison needs aligned objectives.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
\paragraph{Construct and interpretation.}
Perpetual UC activity can prevent completion. Decoding, history completeness and A1--A4 remain unvalidated; resynthesis/repair are excluded. Witnesses assume finite startup, reporting, recovery and election; prevalence is unestablished. Only 39/126 PC2 entries have a safe full prefix for the selected new requirement.

\paragraph{Validation and measurement.}
Shared authorship, AI and code risk correlated faults; independent parsing covers restricted FSP. Direct-Full is in-house, Legacy's objective differs, and pruning isolation is incomplete. External/compositional/symbolic solvers and randomized orders are untested. Ratios exclude unresolved costs; extended trials lack medians. Sampling may miss peaks; RSS establishes neither heap occupancy nor bottlenecks. S4 retains interruptions/follow-ups.

\paragraph{Generalization.}
Null/LOSS, faster Update-first, lower Eager/Update-first memory and Travel/PC limits bound gains; PC2's merged timeout remains unresolved. Fixtures/repeats are not independent applications; timing uses one machine. Inherited inputs lack precedence; only Railcab transfers branch. Exponential separation assumes the default order.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
FG-DUCS synthesizes safe, finite updates and preserves the selected new controller. Fine-grained choices yield \VThreeESixWitness{}/\VThreeESixMainPairs{} controlled separations; inherited inputs yield \VThreeEOneChanged{}/\VThreeEOneTrials{} differences. Lazy decides \LazyDecisions{}/\ApplicationContracts{} contracts within budget and separates $n+1$ from $2^n$ states under the default order. Deployment requires justified scopes and a conforming adapter.
