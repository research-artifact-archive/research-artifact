\begin{abstract}
An optimistic computation can retry, compute under protection, or validate a prepared result. We ask how a scheduler can retain optimal protected work at every unknown interference budget while choosing other job priorities. For finite whole-kernel jobs with persistent outputs, an exact residual safety filter needs only completed/current information, with constant-time mode queries. Every caller following its immediate preparation protocol has worst total work within $(3r+2)/(2r+2)$ of the best general program satisfying the same universal call and protection contract, on every dependency DAG; $r$ is the spare-call allowance. The factor is sharp over all permitted callers, equals $5/4$ at $r=1$, and equals one at $r=0$. An exact independent-job curve supplies the benchmark for sharpness. We also characterize when fixed mismatch and validation charges require future fee information. Conditional Java correspondence and native Linux/Roslyn studies delimit the work, observation and call assumptions. The result bounds the body-work cost of scheduling freedom; the native studies establish neither general cost transfer nor latency gains.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A library that completes optimistic computations must decide when to retry, when to compute under protection, and when to validate a prepared result before protected recomputation. These established mechanisms~\cite{welc2008,maldonado2011} leave a contract question: what total work does a fixed failed-call allowance and optimal protection at every unknown write budget require?

These are different resources. One unit-work job illustrates the choice. Retrying once and then computing under protection uses at most two bodies but protects a body after one interfering write. Preparing again before a guarded cached completion instead protects no body after one write, yet can execute three bodies after two writes. Both have two logical completion calls. A retry counter alone therefore does not determine the work contract.

Linux's rename-lock pathname loop~\cite{linuxDpath,linuxDcache} allows two body attempts; our cached variant permits a third to reduce protected rebuilding. It relaxes that source bound. Roslyn's immutable-solution loop has lazy work and guarded merges that prevent fixed-work transfer, and its dense-arrival measurements favor two modes. A logical call is therefore neither a duration nor necessarily a native attempt.

We study finite dependent jobs with persistent saved outputs and positive whole-kernel work. The writer's total interference budget is hidden. A caller supplies retry slack $r$, allowing at most $n+r$ completion calls for $n$ jobs. After $r$ failures, completing cached operations attain the optimal protected-work curve: the sum of the largest $(B-r)_+$ job weights under at most $B$ writes. We ask what follows when \emph{one policy must keep that optimal curve at every budget}, and how a scheduler can preserve it while selecting other completing modes.

The results answer three connected design questions.
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item \emph{What total work does the contract force?} Independent jobs admit an exact least worst-work curve, attained simultaneously at all budgets by one sorted policy. The lower bound covers every program in the declared interface, including retained records and paid early preparation.
\item \emph{How much can other scheduling priorities cost?} On every DAG, an exact filter exposes all completing modes preserving the residual protection contract. Every caller obeying its immediate preparation protocol has worst body work within $\alpha_r=(3r+2)/(2r+2)$ of the full optimal-protection program class. The factor is sharp over all callers, and is $5/4$ for one spare call. The filter needs no future weight or edge: queries take $O(1)$ operations and updates $O(\log n)$.
\item \emph{When does that interface stop being exact?} Arbitrary mismatch premiums admit an exact region using completed mismatch costs and future premiums. The DAG still affects only readiness. We also solve three fixed actual outcome prices; costly validation changes the required future information. A reachable identical-prefix pair proves that hiding arbitrary future premiums loses exactness.
\end{enumerate}

For independent works 8 and 16 with no spare calls, one universally optimal-protection policy requires worst $(W,L)=(40,16)$ at $B=1$. If $B=1$ is supplied, cache the 8-unit job first: on match finish 16 fresh; on mismatch the write budget is exhausted, so prepare/cache 16. This attains $(32,16)$. The difference measures the price of the simultaneous protection contract, not the price of merely omitting a budget argument.

Top-budget sums, adaptive testing and safety-game filters are inherited. Our contribution is an exact enforcement interface together with a sharp bound on the body-work cost of allowing other scheduling priorities. For independent works $(8,4,2,1)$ and $r=1$, descending cheap order followed by caching costs 38 at a saturated budget; ascending order costs 31, with the same protection guarantee. The factor bounds such choices without optimizing each DAG. Conditional Java wrappers make the contract executable; they do not introduce a new synchronization primitive. An actual client requiring this entire combination of guarantees remains unestablished. Section~\ref{sec:related} makes the closest-work comparisons explicit. Earlier snapshots of this same research project~\cite{artifact,chargedartifact} studied retained guards, supplied-budget frontiers and charged complexity. Those preserved developments are not independent prior work or additional validation of this manuscript.

\section{Completion Interfaces and Resource Contracts}
\label{sec:interface}

Write $\N=\{0,1,\ldots\}$. Jobs $J=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ form a finite DAG, $n\ge1$. Job $i$ is ready when every predecessor in $\operatorname{Pred}(i)$ has completed. Each completion saves an immutable output despite later writes. Completion requires one transformation per job, without a simultaneous snapshot. Policies are deterministic and causal.

Each ready job has a pure, opaque whole kernel on its own input and exact saved predecessor outputs. Every execution performs work $w_i>0$, inside or outside protection. Partial/incremental evaluation, preparation before readiness and external completion are excluded. A prepared result certifies those inputs; subsequent map reads cannot reconstruct saved outputs.

\paragraph{State and transition notation.}
Write $\sigma=(x,\mathcal M,\mathcal R,\lambda)$ for live own-input identities, completed outputs, a finite snapshot/record store and program locals. A record $\rho=(i,s,\mathbf u_i,z)$ certifies a completed kernel execution on captured identity $s$ and saved predecessor tuple $\mathbf u_i=\mathcal M|_{\operatorname{Pred}(i)}$, returning $z$. Copies retain this certificate; fabrication or retagging requires new computation. Define $\operatorname{pub}_i(z)$ to assign $x_i:=z$, save $\mathcal M(i):=z$ and complete $i$. Only publishing completion changes an unfinished live key through a foreground action. Table~\ref{tab:calls} gives effects, observations and charges; $m=[s=x_i]$ is exact identity comparison. A failed cheap call clears selection, while its retained record copies remain available.

\paragraph{A turn and its write gate.}
The program selects an action $a$, fixing its job and mode. The environment then chooses a finite legal write word $\omega$ before that action's effect:
\[
 \sigma\ \xrightarrow{\ \omega\ }\ \widehat\sigma
 \ \xrightarrow{\ a\,/\,o,\,\delta\ }\ \sigma'.
\]
Here $o$ is the returned observation and $\delta=\Delta(Q,W,L)$. Each external assignment changes its own $x_i$ and leaves $\mathcal M$ immutable. At every finite history an unfinished target admits an identity fresh to all its prior identities, records and raw snapshots, including snapshots predating the charged interval. The adversary may choose never to reuse one; finiteness alone does not provide this supply.

After the gate, record selection uses only the already-computed prior store. A new unprotected preparation, even if paid, is a separate action with a later comparison gate. Completing comparison/computation/publication is indivisible to writes; every guard ends on return. Inspections honestly return live entries. Cached completion reveals its output and comparison Boolean; retained values, reference equality and local state remain available. Clocks, allocation identifiers and work counters are not observations. Section~\ref{sec:observation} separately restricts cached observations.

\paragraph{Preparation and charging.}
General programs may prepare early after readiness, using immutable own snapshots and saved parents; raw snapshots may be old. Immediate preparation captures and computes only for the next call. A pure unprotected body has no live interaction: at completed-operation boundaries, contract it with its returned record and charge, and replay intervening writes before the next action. Section~\ref{sec:java} accounts for pending writes without placing a completed result at an unfinished source prefix.

The common start precedes all computed initialization. $W$ charges every kernel, including discarded/early computations; $L$ charges its protected part. $Q$ counts validation/publication/completion calls, including failures, but not preparations or writers. Waiting, common call overhead and nonkernel allocation are outside these coordinates. Write $\Omega_k(T)$ for the sum of the largest $\min(k,|T|)$ works, $\Omega(T)$ for total work, and $\Omega_0=0$; omitted sets mean $J$. These aggregate objects are inherited~\cite{bertsimas,federgruen}.

Free copying, selection and local actions modify only $\mathcal R,\lambda$. Two modes allow cheap/fresh; three add cached. ``Cheap'' refers to kernel work. Direct locking, retained guards, cross-job callbacks and outside completion are excluded. Selected operations terminate. Admissibility requires completion of every job after finitely many actions on each admitted play; infinite free actions fail it. No uniform action bound over all environments is assumed.

\paragraph{Strategies and admissibility.}
$B$ bounds external writes; $r$ permits extra failed calls beyond $n$ completions. An informed strategy is $P(B,h)$; an unaware one is $P(h)$. Let $\mathcal E_b$ contain gate strategies using at most $b$ writes on every play. For mode family $j$, $\mathcal P_j(B,r)$ requires completion with $Q\le n+r$ against every $E\in\mathcal E_B$. In contrast, one $P\in\mathcal U_j(r)$ must satisfy that cap for every $b\ge0$ and $E\in\mathcal E_b$, without receiving $B$. The gate adversary knows the deterministic program and its prior store. A fixed adverse play can be scripted at gates; no wall-clock-oblivious or randomized value follows. Its finite write count also bounds a capped environment preserving that play. Worst protection is $\sup_{E\in\mathcal E_B}L(P,E)$.



\begin{lemma}[Fresh-write and finite-adversary facts]
\label{lem:inspection}
In Section~\ref{sec:interface}'s game, a fresh own-key write at a selected $i$-operation's gate invalidates every retained $i$-record. Any concrete prefix has both a no-write and a fresh-write continuation whenever that extra write respects the environment's budget. An adversary bounded by $b$ writes on every play forces any $P\in\mathcal P_j(b,r)$ to finish every job, regardless of free inspections or record retention.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A fresh identity differs from every retained target identity. Copying/selection cannot change that, and all observations and job/mode choices agree until the gate. The bounded adversary belongs to $\mathcal E_b$; leaving any job unfinished violates admissibility even with infinitely many free actions. Thus every job completes through Table~\ref{tab:calls}. No unbounded adversary is truncated.

\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Work charged by useful comparison writes]
\label{lem:charging}
From the charged common start, consider a complete execution in which the adversary writes only fresh own-key identities at comparisons whose zero-write continuation would match. Let $H$ be the multiset of resulting cheap failures and $D$ the set of resulting cached mismatches. Then
\[
 W\ \ge\ \Omega+\sum_{h\in H}w_{i(h)}+\sum_{i\in D}w_i.
\]
Already-stale failures may consume calls, but need no extra charge in this inequality.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal K$ be the set of completed kernel invocations since the common charged start. Give each original prepared record a proof-only creator $\iota(\rho)\in\mathcal K$; copying preserves that creator. Its job and captured inputs never change. These labels are not observations supplied to the program.

For each useful invalidated comparison $e$, let $\rho_e$ be the record selected by the \emph{zero-write continuation of that same call}. Define $\phi(e)=\iota(\rho_e)$. Although the failure continuation may select another record, $\rho_e$ was already in the pre-gate store, so $\phi(e)$ was executed and charged on the actual run. This defines $\phi$ for both useful cheap failures and useful cached mismatches.

We show that $\phi$ is injective. Different jobs have different kernel invocations. At one job, the fresh write following $\rho_e$ changes the live input to an identity absent from all earlier captures. Subsequent useful comparisons must select records matching later live inputs. Neither the restricted writer nor any unfinished foreground action restores the earlier identity. Thus their creators differ from $\phi(e)$, including all copies of that invocation's record.

For each completed job $i$, choose the invocation $\psi(i)$ supplying its final completion: the protected invocation for fresh/stale cached, or the creator of the matching published record. These $n$ invocations are distinct across jobs. At job $i$, every useful invalidation precedes completion and invalidates the input of its charged creator. Hence $\psi(i)$ is distinct from all $\phi(e)$ at that job. A useful cached mismatch therefore contributes two distinct invocations: $\phi(e)$ for its preparation and $\psi(i)$ for its protected recomputation. Already-stale calls supply no additional $\phi$ charge.

The images of $\phi$ and $\psi$ are disjoint subsets of $\mathcal K$. Each invocation at $i$ costs $w_i$, giving the inequality. This argument charges early work and old-snapshot computations at their actual creation; it does not treat a suffix with prepaid records as a free new start.

\end{proof}



\section{Universal Call and Protected-Work Guarantees}
\label{sec:resources}

\begin{theorem}[Optimal protection on every DAG]
\label{thm:resources}\label{thm:universal}
For integers $B,r\ge0$, the least worst protected work among programs supplied with $B$ and completing with $Q\le n+r$ is $\Omega_{(B-r)_+}$. One policy attains this entire curve without $B$: immediately prepare before cheap calls until $r$ failures or completion, then immediately prepare/cache every remaining job in any ready order.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each failed cheap call needs a distinct write in its current-capture/comparison interval. There are at most $r$ failures and $n$ completing calls. After the switch at most $(B-r)_+$ distinct jobs mismatch, giving the upper bound; at $B\le r$ no protected body is needed.

For the lower, target the $k=\min((B-r)_+,n)>0$ heaviest jobs. Count target cheap failures $f$ and completed targets $t$. At their comparison gates, install a fresh identity while $f\le r$ and $t<k$; stop permanently at $f=r+1$ or $t=k$. Every write causes a failure or cached completion, so at operation boundaries its count is $v\le f+t$. Before another write, $v+1\le f+t+1\le r+k\le B$, even on inadmissible programs. Thus this is an unconditionally bounded writer. The call cap excludes $r+1$ cheap failures; Lemma~\ref{lem:inspection} forces every target to complete. Its comparison cannot match, so all target bodies are protected and $L\ge\Omega_k$. Readiness may delay a target but cannot remove its completion obligation. Free inspections and retained records do not evade its fresh gate identity. At $k=0$ nonnegative work suffices.
\end{proof}

With only cheap/fresh modes, the analogous supplied-budget minimum is $0$ at $B\le r$ and $\Omega$ otherwise; requiring one universally call-bounded policy changes the boundary to $B\ge r$. After $r$ failures, an unaware policy must still tolerate another write. The archive gives those two-mode proofs~\cite{chargedartifact}.

\section{The Total Work Needed for Optimal Protection}
\label{sec:joint}

We minimize worst total work while keeping optimal protection at every budget. Separate $W,L$ suprema may have different witnesses; they do not determine $\sup(W+\lambda L)$.



Let $\mathcal V_r$ contain the policies in $\mathcal U_3(r)$ satisfying $L(P,E)\le\Omega_{(b-r)_+}$ for every $b\ge0$ and $E\in\mathcal E_b$. This requires one \emph{simultaneous optimal-protection curve}; it is stronger than universal completion. Write $W_P(B)=\sup_{E\in\mathcal E_B}W(P,E)$.

\begin{lemma}[Capping a realized play]
\label{lem:capping}
For $P\in\mathcal V_r$, a finite prefix containing $d$ actual writes has protected work at most $\Omega_{(d-r)_+}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Continue the prefix without writes. Admissibility forces completion. Cap that environment unconditionally at $d$: induction preserves each action and realized write, and there are no later writes to remove. The resulting environment belongs to $\mathcal E_d$ and preserves the complete play. Its total protected work satisfies the bound; nonnegative costs give the prefix bound. The program need not observe $d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Least total-work curve under simultaneous optimal protection]
\label{thm:jointuniversal}
For independent jobs with sorted positive works $a_1\le\cdots\le a_n$,
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal V_r}W_P(B)=F_r(B):=
 \begin{cases}
  \Omega+B a_n,&B\le r,\\
  \Omega+\displaystyle\max_{1\le m\le\min(B-r,n)}
       \{r a_{n-m+1}+\Omega_m\},&B>r.
 \end{cases}
\]
One policy attains this entire curve: process jobs in ascending work order, freshly prepare before each cheap call until $r$ cheap failures, and freshly prepare before each cached completion thereafter. It observes neither $B$ nor cached comparison results. Sorting, prefix sums and prefix maxima construct the curve in $O(n\log n)$ arithmetic/comparison operations, representing its initial linear segment analytically.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Upper bound.} Each bad comparison needs a write in its distinct preparation-to-comparison interval. There are at most $r$ cheap failures; cached mismatches occur only thereafter, at distinct jobs. Thus $Q\le n+r$ and $L\le\Omega_{(B-r)_+}$ at every budget. When $B\le r$, at most $B$ preparations are lost, each costing at most $a_n$.

For $B>r$, suppose cached mode starts at rank $j$ and has $m\ge1$ mismatches. All failed preparations were at ranks at most $j$ and the $m$ mismatched jobs lie in the suffix, so $j\le n-m+1$. Duplicate work is at most $r a_j+\Omega_m\le r a_{n-m+1}+\Omega_m$, with $m\le B-r$. Without a mismatch it is at most $r a_n$, below the $m=1$ term. For each permitted $m$, match the first $n-m$ jobs, fail cheap $r$ times at the next job, and mismatch all $m$ cached completions. This attains its term using $r+m$ writes. The analogous maximum-job witness attains $\Omega+B a_n$ at $B\le r$.

\emph{Lower bound for arbitrary programs.} Fix $m\le\min(B-r,n)$ and target the $m$ heaviest jobs. Write a fresh own-key identity only at target comparisons whose zero-write continuation would match. Let $f$ count these useful cheap failures, $c$ useful cached mismatches and $t$ all target completions. Stop forever after $f=r+1$ or $t=m$. At operation boundaries the write count is $d=f+c$, with $c\le t$. Before another write, $d+1\le f+t+1\le r+m$. This is a globally $(r+m)$-bounded adversary even on inadmissible programs.

An admissible program cannot reach $f=r+1$, and every target eventually completes. Before all targets finish, no target comparison can match: a useful comparison is invalidated, and a stale one already fails or protects. Thus all targets complete protectively and $L\ge\Omega_m$. Lemma~\ref{lem:capping}, applied at the realized final count $d$, gives
\[
 \Omega_m\le L\le\Omega_{(d-r)_+},\qquad d=f+c\le r+m.
\]
Positivity forces $d=r+m$, hence $f=r$ and $c=m$. All target completions are useful cached mismatches; this is a consequence of the contract, not an assumed normal form. Lemma~\ref{lem:charging} now charges $r$ distinct failed preparations of work at least $a_{n-m+1}$, all $m$ duplicate target kernels and mandatory $\Omega$. Maximizing over $m$ gives the lower bound.

\emph{Early budgets.} At $d\le r$, zero protection forbids fresh or mismatching cached completion. The $m=1$ witness just constructed has $r$ useful cheap failures at a maximum-work job, then its useful cached mismatch. Truncate after the completed call containing the $B$th useful write, for $1\le B\le r$. Its unchanged prefix has destroyed $B$ distinct paid preparations of that unfinished job. Eventual completion needs another distinct kernel, giving $\Omega+B a_n$ by global charging. At $B=0$ mandatory work suffices. Lower bounds include retained records, free inspections and paid early preparations after readiness.
\end{proof}

The maximizing $m$ need not be largest: $(1,100)$ and $r=1$ give terms 200 and 102. The least $W/L$ curves may have different maximizing executions. Other optimal-$L$ policies can use more work; relaxing protection changes the comparison.

\paragraph{Dependency boundary.}
The lower bound is order-independent. For any DAG, a topological cheap-then-cached policy attains $F_r(B)=\Omega+B w_{\max}$ at $B\le r+1$, retaining optimal $L$ at all budgets. At $r=0$, all-cached gives $F_0(B)=\Omega+\Omega_B$. For positive retries, Theorem~\ref{thm:approx} supplies a uniform work factor; a least entire DAG curve remains unresolved. The archive gives dependent-job observation examples. Proposition~\ref{thm:invisible} isolates the distinct supplied-budget boundary.

\section{A Safety Filter That Needs No Future Job Data}
\label{sec:residual}

A DAG scheduler must decide which protected computations preserve the contract. The following exact interface avoids solving a new game when the ready set changes.

Fix the finite instance $J$ and a completed ideal $D$, with $S=J\setminus D$. Let $k$ count cached mismatches since leaving the cheap phase and $\ell$ be protected work incurred. Each such mismatch completes a distinct job, so physical states satisfy $0\le k\le|D|$. In this section, each remaining job completes once, either fresh or by \emph{current capture and immediate preparation} followed by cached completion. No further cheap calls, early preparations, retained guards or partial kernels are allowed. A scheduler can use its available history to choose a ready job in finite time. It receives no future write bound.

\paragraph{The residual contract.}
A state is viable if there exists one such completing policy $P$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \forall b\ge0\ \forall E\in\mathcal E_b:
 \quad \ell+L_{\rm future}(P,E)\le\Omega_{k+b}(J).
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal E_b$ concerns future writes and has an unconditional cap on every play. The same $P$ must work for every $b$. This is an explicit interface obligation: it does not assert that $k$ is the total number of past writes. A history exposing additional writes can support a weaker global obligation, so necessity below is local to~\eqref{eq:residual}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact residual safety]
\label{thm:residual}
On every DAG, for $0\le k\le|D|$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item some residual policy satisfies~\eqref{eq:residual};
\item all-cached completion in any causal ready order satisfies it;
\item $\ell\le\Omega_k(D)$.
\end{enumerate}
From a viable state every ready cached action is safe. A ready fresh action $i$ has a safe continuation exactly when
\begin{equation}
 \ell+w_i\le\Omega_k(D\cup\{i\}).
 \label{eq:filter}
\end{equation}
Thus this predicate, together with all cached actions, is maximally permissive for the stated residual contract and action class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Specialize Theorem~\ref{thm:fee}, proved in the next section, to $p_i=w_i$ and $h_i=0$. With physical $k\le|D|$ and positive completed weights, its region is $\operatorname{Top}_k(w_D\cup0_S)=\Omega_k(D)$. Every cached limit is the same sum, while each fresh limit is $\Omega_k(D\cup\{i\})-w_i$. Thus all cached successors and exactly the displayed fresh successors are viable. Each completes one job, so selecting cached in any ready order preserves the condition through termination. The general theorem also proves necessity against every causal residual policy, including policies using concrete identities.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Composing the filter with retries.}
Use any immediate-preparation cheap controller until all jobs finish or $r$ cheap failures occur. At the switch initialize $k=\ell=0$ and retain \emph{all} cheaply completed weights in $D$. The filter is initially viable. Every earlier cheap failure and later cached mismatch needs a different write: their current-capture/comparison intervals are disjoint. Hence $r+k\le B$ whenever the switch occurs in a $B$-write execution. The invariant yields
\[
 Q\le n+r,\qquad
 L=\ell\le\Omega_k(D)\le\Omega_{(B-r)_+}(J).
\]
If completion precedes the switch, $L=0$. Theorem~\ref{thm:resources} shows that this protection curve is optimal even against the larger general-program class.

\paragraph{The work cost of scheduling freedom.}
Let $\mathcal A_r$ contain exactly the composed callers just described: one current preparation per cheap attempt until $r$ failures or completion, then only filtered fresh/cached completions, with one current preparation before cached and none before fresh. Ready-job and permitted-mode choices may depend on history, but not $B$; each choice terminates. No redundant kernels or further cheap calls occur. Define $F_J(B)=\inf_{P\in\mathcal V_r}W_P(B)$ over the full simultaneous-contract class of Section~\ref{sec:joint}. A different policy may attain this infimum at different $B$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp uniform work bound for every caller]
\label{thm:approx}
For every finite DAG, integer $r\ge0$, caller $A\in\mathcal A_r$ and budget $B\ge0$,
\[
 W_A(B)\le\alpha_r F_J(B),\qquad
 \alpha_r=\frac{3r+2}{2r+2}=\frac32-\frac1{2(r+1)}.
\]
Every caller is exact at $B\le r+1$; for $r=0$, every caller is exact at all budgets. For each fixed $r\ge1$, no smaller constant works uniformly over all finite instances and all callers in $\mathcal A_r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The preceding composition proves $\mathcal A_r\subseteq\mathcal V_r$. On a complete caller execution, exact kernel accounting gives
\[
 W=\Omega+\sum_{\text{cheap failures}}w_i+
                  \sum_{\text{cached mismatches}}w_i.
\]
Failed-call preparations and mismatching cached completions need writes in distinct current-capture intervals. Put $M=\max_iw_i$. For $B\le r$ this gives $W_A(B)\le\Omega+BM$. For $B>r$, put $m=\min(B-r,n)$ and $T=\Omega_m$. At most $r$ preparations fail and at most $m$ distinct suffix jobs mismatch, so $W_A(B)\le\Omega+rM+T$, regardless of caller order or safe-mode choices.

Theorem~\ref{thm:jointuniversal}'s lower construction works on any DAG: its globally capped writer stays silent while predecessors complete. Admissibility then forces every target to complete, without assuming simultaneous readiness. Thus its full-program lower gives
\[
 F_J(B)\ge\Omega+
 \max_{1\le s\le m}\{r a_{n-s+1}+\Omega_s\}
 \ge\Omega+\max\{(r+1)M,T\}.
\]
The early-budget lower matches the caller upper. The same is true at $B=r+1$, and at every $B$ for $r=0$.

For $r\ge1$, normalize $x=T/M\ge1$ and $z=\Omega/M\ge x$. The ratio is at most $(z+r+x)/(z+\max(r+1,x))$, nonincreasing in $z$. Set $z=x$. For $x\le r+1$ the resulting $(2x+r)/(x+r+1)$ increases; for $x\ge r+1$, $1+r/(2x)$ decreases. Both meet at $\alpha_r$. The execution upper and each full-program lower hold separately, so no interchange of infimum and supremum is needed.

For sharpness, fix $r\ge1$, set $q=r/(r+1)$, and use independent descending weights $w_j=q^{j-1}$. A caller retries the first job until $r$ failures, then caches all jobs. At $B=n+r$, $r$ first-job failures followed by $n$ mismatches give its exact worst work $2\Omega+r$. Every such action is permitted. Since $r w_s+\Omega_s=r+1$ for every $s$, Theorem~\ref{thm:jointuniversal} gives $F_J(B)=\Omega+r+1$, where $\Omega=(r+1)(1-q^n)$. Their ratio tends to $\alpha_r$. Multiplying by $(r+1)^{n-1}$ yields positive integer weights with the same ratio. Every smaller constant therefore fails on a finite input.
\end{proof}

For one spare call, $\alpha_1=5/4$: a scheduler may pursue other ready-job priorities while preserving optimal protection and a 25\% bound on worst body-work overhead. The same filter need not receive future weights or edges. Sharpness concerns the entire caller class; minimum-ready is ascending and optimal on the independent geometric witnesses. The theorem neither solves the exact positive-retry DAG curve nor bounds native waiting, allocation, writer costs or filter maintenance.

\paragraph{What the filter stores.}
Maintain the completed weights in two heaps: the largest $k$ in a min-heap, the rest in a max-heap, together with the top-$k$ sum $T$. For $k=0$, positive fresh work is forbidden. Otherwise let $\tau$ be the minimum of the first heap; the fresh test is
\[
 \ell+w_i\le T+\max\{0,w_i-\tau\}.
\]
It takes $O(1)$ arithmetic/comparison operations. Completion inserts one weight, and a mismatch increases $k$ by one; a constant number of heap transfers gives $O(\log(|D|+1))$ update time and $O(|D|)$ storage. Starting empty needs no preprocessing. Integer bit costs are additional.

No future weight or edge enters this predicate. The scheduler checks readiness and proposes a job/mode; the safety component returns the permitted modes and updates only after completion. Thus the same component works when future jobs are undisclosed, provided each realized instance is finite and obeys the interface. This supplies an invariant for each finite instance; infinitely arriving jobs have no completion guarantee.




\paragraph{Permissiveness and total work.}
The distinction is observable even on a chain. Let $r=1$: cheaply complete weight 100, fail once at weight 1, then mismatch its cached completion. Now $k=\ell=1$; a fresh weight 2 is safe because $1+2\le100$. A rule that waits until \emph{all} remaining work fits the current allowance would reject it when the remaining weights are $(2,80,80,80)$. The smaller interface permits this decision without knowing those later weights.

Safe choices need not minimize $W$. However, any immediate fresh/cached suffix has worst total work no greater than all-cached after the \emph{same cheap controller}. For a concrete execution retain its exact prefix with $b_0$ actual writes and duplicate cheap work $C$. If $m$ suffix jobs mismatch, its work is at most $\Omega+C+\Omega_m(S)$. An all-cached continuation can attain this upper expression by targeting the $m$ heaviest remaining jobs, using $b_0+m\le B$ writes. Script that prefix and cap the resulting writer unconditionally at $b_0+m$. This proves worst-curve domination, not dominance under the same write timing. Therefore ascending cheap order retains Theorem~\ref{thm:jointuniversal}'s least curve for independent jobs under \emph{any} filtered suffix; at $r=0$ the same holds on every DAG. Theorem~\ref{thm:approx} bounds the cost of other cheap-phase priorities; exact positive-retry DAG work optimality remains open.


\section{Exact Safety with Mismatch Premiums}
\label{sec:chargedresidual}

Keep the residual actions and write semantics, but charge fresh completion $p_i>0$, cached match 0, and cached mismatch $q_i=p_i+h_i$ with $h_i\ge0$. These are fixed supplied protection costs. For a multiset $A$, let $\operatorname{Top}_j(A)$ sum its largest $\min(j,|A|)$ entries, with $\operatorname{Top}_0=0$. Put $G_j=\operatorname{Top}_j(q_J)$. The benchmark is the \emph{all-cached charged curve}, not an asserted optimal charged curve. One completing policy, without $b$ as input, must satisfy $\ell+L_{\rm future}\le G_{k+b}$ for every integer $b\ge0$ and every unconditionally $b$-bounded future writer.

\begin{theorem}[Exact charged residual safety]
\label{thm:fee}
For every finite DAG and physical $0\le k\le|D|$, the exact viable region is
\begin{equation}
 \ell\le V(D,k):=\operatorname{Top}_k(q_D\cup h_S).
 \label{eq:chargedregion}
\end{equation}
For each ready $i$, set $X_i=q_D\cup h_{S\setminus\{i\}}$. Its exact permissions are
\begin{align}
 \text{cached }i &: \quad \ell\le\operatorname{Top}_k(X_i),\label{eq:chargedcached}\\
 \text{fresh }i &: \quad \ell+p_i\le\operatorname{Top}_k(X_i\cup\{q_i\}).\label{eq:chargedfilter}
\end{align}
Every ready job has at least one safe mode at a viable state. Future edges and future body costs are absent from these predicates; future premiums can matter.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{One policy for all caps.} Fix a causal deterministic policy and any admissible prefix. Restrict writers to silence or one fresh-identity write at each cached gate, and fix a rule selecting the concrete fresh identities. Even a policy that inspects those identities induces a finite completion tree: every action finishes one job. A leaf with $c$ mismatches and future cost $t$ has a causal script writer. Cap that writer at $c$ writes on \emph{every} play. It reproduces the leaf, since none of its first $c$ writes is suppressed and its selected play has no later write. Thus safety requires $\ell+t\le G_{k+c}$ at each leaf. The cap is never supplied to the policy.

Conversely, a tree satisfying every leaf inequality gives a policy that uses only abstract outcomes. Any actual $b$-write execution follows a leaf with $c\le b$ mismatches, by the disjoint comparison intervals. Extra harmless writes only enlarge $G_{k+b}$. This proves an exact completion-tree characterization for the residual contract. It does not require identical choices at different concrete histories with the same numerical state, nor characterize a weaker global obligation after observations revealing extra writes.

For a tree $P$, its exact permissible prefix cost is $\min_{\text{leaf}}(G_{k+c}-t)$. Define $V$ as the maximum of these limits over the finitely many abstract completing trees. At termination $V(J,k)=G_k$. Fresh $i$ has threshold $V(D+i,k)-p_i$, and cached $i$ has threshold
\[
 \min\{V(D+i,k),\ V(D+i,k+1)-q_i\}.
\]
The two outcome subtrees may differ, but both belong to the same policy and satisfy all suffix caps. Take the maximum over modes and ready jobs. This is backward induction over jobs, not separate optimization at each budget.

For the local algebra, write $C_j=\operatorname{Top}_j(X)$ and $B_j=\operatorname{Top}_j(X\cup\{q\})$, with $q=p+h$. Then
\[
 \min\{B_k,B_{k+1}-q\}=C_k,\qquad
 \max\{B_k-p,C_k\}=\operatorname{Top}_k(X\cup\{h\}).
\]
For $1\le k\le|X|$, let $\tau$ be its $k$th entry. If $q\le\tau$, $B_k=C_k$ and $B_{k+1}\ge C_k+q$; otherwise $B_{k+1}=C_k+q$. This proves the first identity. Since $B_k=C_k+\max(0,q-\tau)$, the second has increment $\max(0,h-\tau)$. At $k=0$ both identities are zero; for $k>|X|$ they reduce to saturated sums. Ties are included.

Inductively, both child multisets for ready $i$ are $X_i\cup\{q_i\}$. The identities give the two displayed mode thresholds and their maximum $\operatorname{Top}_k(q_D\cup h_S)$, independently of $i$. The terminal multiset is $q_J$, completing the induction. Choosing a safe mode for any ready job preserves viability and terminates after $|S|$ calls.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Fixed assignments already attain feasibility.}
For a fixed set $F\subseteq S$ of fresh jobs, with all others cached in any ready order, the exact prefix allowance is $\operatorname{Top}_k(q_D\cup q_F)-\sum_{i\in F}p_i$. To see this, minimize $G_{k+|M|}-\sum_{i\in M}q_i$ over cached mismatch subsets $M$: the minimum is the top-$k$ sum on $D\cup F$, attained by choosing cached entries above its $k$th entry (empty at $k=0$, saturated when necessary). Maximizing over $F$ selects only fresh entries included in that top sum, replacing each selected $q_i$ by $q_i-p_i=h_i$. This gives Equation~\eqref{eq:chargedregion}; forcing inclusion or exclusion of a ready $i$ gives its two mode limits. Thus outcome adaptation does not raise this feasibility threshold. The filter exposes all safe choices; its work guarantee is the separate Theorem~\ref{thm:approx}.

\paragraph{When completed information suffices.}
All-cached has threshold $C=\operatorname{Top}_k(q_D)$: minimize $G_{k+m}-\operatorname{Top}_m(q_S)$ over $m$. The top completed/future subsets give the lower bound; taking future entries above the $k$th completed entry gives equality (use $m=0$ at $k=0$). For $k>0$, Equation~\eqref{eq:chargedregion} equals $C$ exactly when no future premium exceeds that completed entry. Pointwise equality need not persist after a mismatch. A sufficient global contract is $\max_Jh_i\le\min_Jq_i$. Common $h$ satisfies it; then adding $hk$ to both incurred body protection and its top-$k$ allowance cancels, recovering the body-only invariant and fresh test.

\paragraph{The general interface.}
Store $A=q_D\cup h_S$, its top-$k$ sum $T$, and adjacent order statistics $\tau=A_{(k)}$, $\sigma=A_{(k+1)}$, padding missing entries by zero. Exact query thresholds are
\[
 C_i=T-\max(0,h_i-\sigma),\qquad
 F_i=T-\min\{p_i,\max(0,\tau-h_i)\},
\]
with $F_i=-p_i$ when $k=0$. Deleting $h_i$ replaces it by $\sigma$ only when it lies in the top $k$; replacing it by $q_i$ gives the fresh expression. Two indexed heaps provide $O(1)$ queries and $O(\log n)$ completion updates: replace $h_i$ by $q_i$ and increment $k$ only on mismatch. This version receives all future premiums, uses $O(n)$ storage and initializes in $O(n+k\log(n+1))$ operations. The scheduler still establishes readiness; the safety component receives no future edges or future body costs.

\begin{proposition}[Future fees can change a present permission]
\label{prop:futurefees}
Over unrestricted fees, the visible prefix and completed/current costs do not determine the exact safe-mode set, even with the entire DAG and vector $q_J$ supplied.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the chain $0\to1\to2$, with $q=(2,2,4)$ and $p_0=p_1=h_0=h_1=1$. Future job2 is $(p_2,h_2)=(1,3)$ in A and $(3,1)$ in B. Both curves are $(0,4,6,8)$. Initially cache0 and observe a mismatch after one write: $D=\{0\},k=1,\ell=2$. This prefix is reachable under the safe all-cached policy in both worlds. For proposed fresh1, Equation~\eqref{eq:chargedfilter} gives limit $\max(2,h_2,2)-1$, namely2 in A and1 in B. Fresh1 is therefore permitted only in A despite identical stated filter inputs. Theorem~\ref{thm:fee} supplies a single all-budget continuation in A and excludes every such continuation in B.
\end{proof}

The completed-only filter rejects fresh~1 in both worlds; the general filter admits it in A. After A's fresh~1, $q_D=\{2,2\}$, $k=1$, $\ell=3$, and future $h_2=3$. This is viable, but cached~2 is forbidden: one mismatch costs7 above $G_2=6$. Thus permitting every cached action throughout the larger region would be incorrect. More permissions establish neither lower worst total work nor native latency gains.

\paragraph{Charging all three outcomes.}
The same completion-tree argument handles fixed actual prices: cached match $a_i\ge0$, fresh $f_i\ge0$, and cached mismatch $b_i\ge\max(a_i,f_i)$. To avoid confusing $b_i$ with a write cap, denote the latter by $u$. Put $A(U)=\sum_{i\in U}a_i$, $q_i=b_i-a_i$, $p_i=f_i-a_i$, $d_i=(-p_i)_+$ and $t_i=b_i-\max(a_i,f_i)$. The actual all-cached benchmark is $A(J)+\operatorname{Top}_{k+u}(q_J)$.

\begin{theorem}[Three fixed actual prices]
\label{thm:threeprices}
For actual incurred protection $\lambda\ge0$, define $\ell=\lambda-A(D)$, which may be negative. One completing policy satisfies the actual benchmark for every future cap $u$ exactly when
\begin{equation}
 \ell\le \sum_{j\in S}d_j+\operatorname{Top}_k(q_D\cup t_S).
 \label{eq:threeprices}
\end{equation}
For ready $i$, let $R_i=\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{i\}}d_j$ and $X_i=q_D\cup t_{S\setminus\{i\}}$. Add $R_i$ to the cached/fresh limits of Theorem~\ref{thm:fee}, using this $X_i$ and signed $p_i$. Every ready job has a safe mode.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Subtract the once-per-job baseline $A(J)$ from every complete leaf and benchmark. Increments become $0,p_i,q_i$, with current $\ell$, so the preceding tree recurrence applies. For $p_i\ge0$, its maximum identity gives premium $q_i-p_i=t_i$, including zero. For $p_i<0$, fresh dominates and gives $-p_i+\operatorname{Top}_k(X_i\cup\{q_i\})$, with $d_i=-p_i$, $t_i=q_i$. Adding the other future savings $R_i$ proves the region and mode limits by induction. Nonnegative $q_i$ keeps the cached minimum identity valid; ties, zeros and saturation are included.
\end{proof}

For fixed guard, comparison and body prices $g_i,c_i,w_i$, set $(a_i,f_i,b_i)=(g_i+c_i,g_i+w_i,g_i+c_i+w_i)$. Then $q_i=w_i$, $d_i=(c_i-w_i)_+$, $t_i=\min(c_i,w_i)$, with every guard/comparison charge retained. If $c_i>w_i$, fresh is cheaper than match and signed $\ell$ records savings. This formula requires future $d_i,t_i$ and is outside the positive-$p$ heap implementation. Supplied prices exclude waiting, partial work and variable native costs.


\section{Observation and Call Accounting Boundaries}
\label{sec:boundaries}

\subsection{What the Cached Comparison Reveals}
\label{sec:observation}

The filter counts observed mismatches. Removing that information changes the interface. In the following erased contract every kernel returns a fixed per-job object $z_i$, and cached completion's \emph{entire visible transition} retains only that output and completion. No compared input, Boolean, comparison-dependent record selection or callback-local side effect survives. Old snapshots and outside inspections remain available; costs, clocks and kernel internals remain hidden. Constant outputs alone would not erase a callback that saves its compared input.

\begin{proposition}[A strict observation boundary]
\label{thm:invisible}
For independent works $(8,16)$, supplied $B=1$ and $Q\le2$, the visible frontier is $\{(24,24),(32,16)\}$; erasure changes it to $\{(24,24),(40,16)\}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All-fresh gives $(24,24)$ and the introduction's policy gives $(32,16)$. Protection below 24 requires a cached match on the no-write path; one useful write charges at least 8 extra work, proving the visible lower bound. Under erasure, a changed cached return restores exactly the same live/saved outputs, old records and observable locals; later inspections/preparations preserve this equality. If any other completion protects its body, changing the cached match makes worst protection 24, whether that protected completion precedes or follows it. Thus protection below 24 requires both jobs cached on the no-write run. Invalidating 16 costs at least 40; caching both attains it. Cheap cannot be used with no spare call. The archive gives the full coupling, including dependent-job extensions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A Native Attempt Is Not Necessarily One Call}
\label{sec:recount}

If a cached mismatch consumes two units rather than one, set $Q'=Q+\#\text{cached mismatches}$. On completion $Q'=n+z$, where $z$ counts cheap failures plus cached mismatches.

\begin{proposition}[A changed call unit]
\label{prop:recount}
Among $B$-unaware policies satisfying $Q'\le n+r$ at every finite budget, the least protected-work curve is 0 for $B<r$ and $\Omega$ for $B\ge r$. One cheap-then-fresh policy attains the whole curve. With supplied $B>r$, all-fresh also attains the complete frontier $(\Omega,\Omega)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Invalidate otherwise-matching comparisons until $z=r$, then stop. Only protected calls complete before that point. Afterwards another cheap/cached call is stale or one further write could make it bad, violating universal $Q'$ admissibility. Thus remaining jobs finish fresh. Cheap then fresh attains the curve. When $B>r$ is supplied, the same writer capped at $z=r+1\le B$ forces all completions to protect their bodies. All-fresh and mandatory work give the frontier. The archive expands retained-record and other supplied-budget cases~\cite{chargedartifact}.
\end{proof}

Maldonado et al.'s irrevocable transition validates an executed prefix, retaining it on success and restarting irrevocably on failure~\cite[Section~III.D]{maldonado2011}. Specializing the prefix to a whole kernel gives the cached body charges, but grouping the failed transaction and restart into one episode gives $Q$, while counting both attempts gives $Q'$. Their mechanism also allows partial prefixes: computing $u$ of a $w$-unit kernel before protection gives protected work $w-u$ on validity, outside our whole-kernel modes. Our lower bounds do not cover that larger action language.

\section{Conditional Transfer to Java Map Operations}
\label{sec:java}

Java~17's per-key map interface provides a concrete interpretation~\cite{cmap17,chm17,chmsource17}. The result below is conditional on specified API/source properties and concerns completed traces. It is not a proof of arbitrary Java clients or the JDK/JMM.

\paragraph{Premises.}
Retain Section~\ref{sec:interface}'s controller, kernel, saved-output and termination requirements. Map keys are permanent and distinct, with stable, pure, terminating hashing/equality/ordering. Values are non-null, deeply immutable and reference-equal; replacement may call \texttt{equals}. Writers preserve membership, value shape and completion milestones, and admit references fresh to all prior target records/snapshots. Kernels make no recursive or other-key map updates. Native waiting, allocation and traversal are separate from body work.

Use three per-key synchronization premises. A successful retrieval returns a value live at a point within its call. Conditional replacement checks/stores atomically. A normally completing \texttt{compute} invokes its remapping once and excludes same-key publications through its store. The pinned source's list and tree paths remap/store at lines1940--1942 and1975--1978, returning the saved value at2003; replacement saves success before returning at1174. Forwarding and structure-only node copies preserve stored value references. These are the API/source assumptions used, rather than consequences of the finite tests.

Cheap wraps \texttt{replace} or validation-only \texttt{compute} and returns only success/failure. Fresh always computes; cached returns its saved output and comparison Boolean. The controller may retain payloads, reference equality, records, saved parents and selector state. Raw cheap-mismatch values, numeric allocation IDs, clocks and diagnostics are not observations. Erasure additionally needs Section~\ref{sec:observation}'s complete restriction.

\paragraph{The relation at completed-operation boundaries.}
Let $H$ be the concrete heap. An injective map $\eta$ carries value references to abstract identities, preserving aliases and immutable payloads. Let $\Delta$ be a proof-only ordered queue of committed external assignments deferred during normalization. Relate $H$ to $\sigma=(x,\mathcal M,\mathcal R,\lambda)$ by the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item $\operatorname{decode}_{\eta}(H.\mathtt{map})=\operatorname{apply}(\Delta,x)$ and $b_H=b_x+|\Delta|$, counting committed external publications.
\item $\mathcal M(i)=\eta(\mathtt{done}[i])$ is the saved completion output. A later map write never changes it.
\item Each selectable record corresponds to an actually available handle $(i,\eta(s),\mathcal M|_{\operatorname{Pred}(i)},\eta(z))$; a discarded ghost computation supplies no selectable record. Observable locals and reference relations agree.
\item Completed wrapper calls and grouped kernel invocations have the same $Q,W,L$. A full kernel charge and its output are introduced only after the source kernel returns.
\end{enumerate}
The queue contains committed assignments, not callbacks to be executed again. Its presence handles publication followed by a writer before the wrapper returns: the wrapper still saves its own result even though the live entry has changed.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional resource transfer for actual writes]
\label{thm:writes}
Under these premises, the body-work bounds and lower witnesses of Sections~\ref{sec:resources}, \ref{sec:joint} and \ref{sec:residual} hold for actual external writes, including colliding bins. Completed traces preserve declared observations, $Q,W,L$, operation kinds and cached mismatches, hence also $Q'$ of Section~\ref{sec:recount}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Projection for upper bounds.} For a completed unprotected kernel, retain its captured operands and group its actual invocation, returned record and full charge into Prepare. Defer writes between capture and return in $\Delta$. This maintains the relation after the source body returns; it never assigns a completed result to an unfinished prefix. For each next operation, flush the pending assignments through its selected atomic/retrieval point and retain later commits. This constructs one consistent projection; a unique normalization is unnecessary. For an inspection, flush commits through its retrieval point, return the saved reference, and defer later commits.

During fresh/cached remapping, the own-key value is fixed through publication. Saved parents and all other kernel operands are immutable. Other-key commits may therefore precede the corresponding abstract effect without changing its result or readiness: completion milestones and saved operands are immutable. This commutation concerns observed values and body charges; bin contention and waiting are excluded. The protected body and publication give precisely Table~\ref{tab:calls}'s update and charge. Extend $\eta$ on new output references. After publication, further writes enter $\Delta$; on return, save the prepared output for successful cheap or the callback's returned output, never a map reread. Thus the saved-output and live-map clauses both hold. Structure-only changes preserve value references; a concurrent value publication remains a write.

Finally flush any terminal queue as a zero-foreground-cost write suffix. The resulting completed trace preserves observations and costs without adding writes. Our explicit upper policies use boundedly many finite kernel/call segments; selected-operation progress therefore supplies termination, and the abstract inequalities transfer. Completed-trace projection alone would not prove arbitrary low-level termination or equivalence of writer diagnostics.

\emph{Realizable lower witnesses.} Suspend the controller after its job/mode and prior store are fixed but before API entry. No foreground guard is held. Run a terminating own-key writer that installs a fresh legal reference, then resume the call. This works even when bins collide, and invalidates every prior target preparation. Repeating the restricted target adversaries gives the lower proofs' comparison outcomes and distinct kernel charges, including early computation. A concrete script with a physical cap of $d$ writes belongs to $\mathcal E_d$ on every play and preserves its designated $d$-write trace. It suffices to realize these witnesses; we need not implement every abstract environment. Immutable saved parents preserve readiness, and the Boolean supplies the permitted branches.

Operation and mismatch labels are preserved by both constructions, establishing the stated $Q'$ accounting as well. This argument uses API/source synchronization premises; it does not derive them from trace agreement.
\end{proof}

Earlier fixtures execute $8w_i$ kernel iterations; newer wrappers execute $w_i$. Neither scale charges native allocation, waiting, writer work or map internals. Full source control points and the relation are archived. Finite tests do not establish concurrent-resize or JMM correctness.

\subsection{RQ2: A Native Traversal and Its Guard Boundary}
\label{sec:linux}

Linux v6.12 \texttt{dentry\_path\_raw} first walks mutable dentry parents/names under RCU, validates \texttt{rename\_lock}'s sequence, and on failure rebuilds under its reader-exclusive lock~\cite{linuxDpath}. Pathname lock contention motivated the upstream optimistic fallback~\cite{linuxPathChange}. The related \texttt{is\_subdir} comment explicitly connects one lockless try with fallback for progress~\cite{linuxDcache}. In this pathname loop, the caller sets the marker to 1 after failure; the primitive does not automatically count retries. Our KUnit-only variant prepares a second traversal with a \emph{new even} sequence, then acquires the lock and validates that saved sequence: reuse on match, reset/rebuild on mismatch. Every traversed component must be initialized, with no concurrent initialization; a valid reference alone is insufficient. Initial dentry/name publication must precede parallel reading; subsequent relevant changes must participate in the global sequence, without wrap. This covers the initialized \texttt{d\_exchange} fixtures, not every filesystem: \texttt{d\_mark\_tmpfile} changes names without that sequence during initialization~\cite{linuxDcache}. Mount traversal and \texttt{getcwd} are excluded.

Assume a private buffer of capacity $1\le K\le\mathtt{INT\_MAX}$, nonnegative name lengths fitting \texttt{int}, valid dentry/RCU lifetimes and successful stable-name copies. Put $C=K-1$ after NUL. Attempted names $v$, requested copy bytes $c$, fault-fill bytes $f$ and successful slashes $s$ obey $v\le s+1$, $f\le c$, $c+s\le C$, hence $T=1+v+c+f+s\le2C+2$. For validation failures $X,Y$, fresh capture gives $X+XY\le\min(B,2)$ for actual global writer sections, including unrelated renames. For the cached variant, counting $Q$ per lockless validation or guarded completion block gives $Q\le2$, $T_{\rm total}\le(1+\min(B,2))(2C+2)$ and $L_T=0$ for $B\le1$. Guard/RCU instructions, waiting and postambles are separate. This single-job upper refinement does not instantiate the publication/observation contract.

Two-CPU ARM64 QEMU runs Linux with lockdep and atomic-sleep checking. A writer using \texttt{lock\_rename} and \texttt{d\_exchange} uses gates outside RCU or a nonsleeping mid-parent gate. All 1,920 rows (948 paths, 972 expected overflows) match quiescent-upstream strings, offsets, canaries and persistent outputs, with no lock/RCU diagnostics. Sequence brackets confirm designated write counts. Four faulty variants reject: unvalidated overflow, unrestored buffer and omitted final validation corrupt output; reusing the first sequence violates protection at $B=1$. Copy-fault/allocation failure and native-time performance are untested.

\section{Related Work and Research Lineage}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{The contract and existing mechanisms.}
Maldonado et al.~\cite[Section~III.D]{maldonado2011} retain a valid transaction prefix on becoming irrevocable, or restart irrevocably after invalidation. Our cached operation uses that established protection/reuse idea; the new object is its resource guarantee under a whole-kernel completion-call contract. Their partial-prefix migration and native transaction attempts do not obey that contract automatically. Section~\ref{sec:recount} makes the accounting difference explicit.

\paragraph{Fallback and contention.}
Protected reexecution~\cite[Section~3.3]{kung}, bounded lock-elision/RPC retries~\cite{glibcElision,hotspotRTM,ulrichRetries,envoyRetries}, irrevocable prefix preservation~\cite{welc2008}, contention management~\cite{attiya 2006,guerraoui2005,alistarh2018} and switching/tuning~\cite{lim1994,usui2010,diegues2014} are established. HTM aborts need not consume writes~\cite{gccRTM}, and RPC can fail. Their progress/time objectives differ from persistent-job $W/L$ caps.

\paragraph{Synthesis.}
\v{C}ern\'y et al.~\cite{cerny} synthesize retries, guards and caches for weighted limit-average safety (PSPACE by erratum); later work optimizes protection/profile costs~\cite{cernyquality} or preemption-safe locks~\cite{cernysafe}. We inherit global-budget/information-state minimax DP~\cite{mannor,postek,dave}, backward induction and Pareto composition; our lower bounds supply an exact independent-job work curve, and our residual theorem replaces game enumeration for one explicit all-budget protection contract.

\paragraph{Scheduling with testing.}
At $r=0$, fresh is untested execution with $u_i=w_i$; cached tests for $t_i=w_i$, then executes for $p_i\in\{0,w_i\}$. Thus $W$ counts testing plus execution; $L$ counts execution. For any deterministic tree, hidden bad-job labels represent its mismatch set and own-gate writes realize them. Dynamic writing alone does not distinguish this canonical game.

D\"urr et al.~\cite{durrtesting} introduce adversarial testing; Buld and Schulz~\cite{buldtesting} and Albers and Eckl~\cite{alberseckl} allow unequal test times/upper bounds under competitive time objectives. Damerius et al.~\cite{dameriustesting} separate testing and execution with a \emph{policy-side} budget; ours bounds adverse outcomes and permits preparation at $B=0$. Dufoss\'e et al.~\cite{dufosseoracle} study minimax testing, revealing durations even for untested jobs; polynomial adaptive optimality assumes their two-phase conjecture. Albers and Janke~\cite{albersjanke} bound failing jobs for irrevocable parallel-machine assignment.

Our absolute $W/L$ caps include retry slack. For the 8/16 adaptive and cache-both policies, $(\sup T,\sup L)=(24,16)$, where $T$ counts preparation, yet $\sup(T+L)=32$ and 40. The distinction is the absolute joint contract and its simultaneous all-budget quantifier. Adaptive testing and binary policy trees are inherited; dynamically placing a write is not by itself a novelty claim.

\paragraph{Budgeted sums and safety filters.}
Bertsimas and Sim's cardinality-budget protection function~\cite[Section~3]{bertsimas} supplies the top-weight sum. Our sorted-union identity is elementary, not a new robust-optimization method. Bloem et al.~\cite[Sections~3--5]{bloem2015} enforce safety through a winning region; allowing only winning successors is inherited. Feng et al.~\cite[Sections~4.4--4.5]{feng2025} enlarge control envelopes through runtime parameter inference, while Cano et al.~\cite[Appendix~A]{cano2026} construct maximally permissive shields with history. Our game can be encoded in these general control frameworks. The specific result is a closed-form region for every DAG: body-only costs discard all future job data; arbitrary mismatch premiums require future fees but still no future body values or edges. Three actual prices add a separate future-savings term. These formulas specialize general safety-game construction to this interface. Fixed mode assignments already attain this residual feasibility threshold, so no feedback advantage is claimed for safety. The further all-caller approximation bounds secondary body work against the full simultaneous-contract optimum; the safety region alone does not give that coefficient. Observed mismatches give a certain write lower bound, not a minimum-history theorem for general shields.

\paragraph{Scheduling freedom.}
Graham's arbitrary-list bound~\cite[Theorem~1]{graham1969} and ter Mors et al.'s price of autonomy~\cite[Theorem~1, Proposition~4]{termors2010} already quantify performance under scheduling freedom. Graham's $1+(m-1)/m'$ even equals $\alpha_r$ at $m=r+1,m'=2(r+1)$, but those parameters count processors and its objective is makespan. Our comparison charges repeated kernels and gives both caller and full causal comparator the same universal $Q/L$ contract, without extra processors or speed. Numerical coincidence alone supplies no reduction.

\paragraph{Precedence and reuse.}
The supplementary charged frontiers use established precedence~\cite{lawler1973} and scheduling-hardness constructions~\cite{gareyjohnson1976}; they do not prove general DAG work optimality for the present filter. Universal restart~\cite{luby1993} concerns expected Las Vegas time. Build and incremental systems~\cite{mokhov2018,leywild2009,adapton2014} may reuse partial work outside our whole-kernel interface.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limits}

Supplied-budget results require that input; our main filter and approximation do not receive it. Native costs are uncalibrated; instrumentation, caches and runtime compilation limit timings. The archive preserves scaling timeouts, policy counterexamples and reversed timings, alongside two adverse Java cost studies. With declared scalar charge $W+L+8\kappa Q$, three modes improve only 2 of 96 policy/budget cells; 94 tie. A separate 72-cell JMH study (four payloads, two bin layouts, nine wrappers) fits nonnegative work/validation/callback/premium prices in only 2 of 8 settings under a 20\% maximum-error criterion, including cached-path validation. Every fitted premium is zero; none of 72 admitted sensitivity roots improves, and 1,080 remain outside the model. All-budget compilation can cost more than requested-value computation. In an archived fixed-digest study on the Valkey key-value store, writer-maintained materialization beats reader repair in time/transfer in all 16 cells; maximum command times are mixed. Such larger interfaces delimit our claims. Finite/AI-assisted checks are neither mechanical proofs nor independent reproduction; human benefit is unmeasured.

\section{Conclusion}

An exact filter permits every safe completing mode without future body data. On every DAG, each caller obeying its immediate preparation discipline preserves optimal protection with worst body work within the sharp uniform factor $(3r+2)/(2r+2)$ of the full simultaneous-contract optimum. Independent jobs admit an exact curve; mismatch and validation charges identify when future fees matter. These guarantees quantify scheduling freedom within an explicit software resource interface. Conditional Java transfer and adverse native comparisons delimit its application.
