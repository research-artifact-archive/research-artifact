# Outcome: per-call toll extension

The fixed exploratory run completed in 7.3405 seconds. All 16,844 profile/cap/toll inputs matched the independently coded known-budget dirty-set Bellman game, across 143,226 budget values. All 33,688 competitive rows matched the direct unknown-budget game and passed their upper/lower certificate checks. Eight fixed certificate corruptions were detected; there were zero failures. The sharp simultaneous-optimality criterion predicted 12,888 ratio-one rows and 20,800 other rows, all correctly.

The main group uses 4,184 previously observed geometry/cap inputs with four new toll values, and two common baseline prices per input. The extra group uses 36 previously observed rational/long-horizon inputs, three tolls and two baseline prices. These are constructed mathematical inputs with a changed cost model, not held-out systems or measurements of native repair cost. Complete input, curve, outcome, control and receipt files are in `run01/`; no cases or adverse results were excluded. `PROOF.md` states the theorem, its quantifiers and its profile-construction prerequisite.

Positive result: the known-budget curve is a capped order statistic of shifted profile rows, and a minimum-bad-path compiler still computes the exact unknown-budget ratio. More restrictive result: with at least two calls, any strictly positive toll below the maximum dirtiness weight destroys all-budget simultaneous optimality, including cases that had it at zero toll. A sufficiently high toll instead makes first acceptance optimal. This explains why adding an actual rejection price is mathematically different from adding a common completion baseline.

Full curve materialization costs O(qm) memory. No new scale campaign was conducted for this extension, and the earlier singleton compact-space bound is not transferred to it. The proof technique's relationship to known online-search and threshold methods remains a novelty question. These results do not establish native performance improvement, broad practical importance, or submission readiness.
