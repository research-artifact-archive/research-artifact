# Root review of the exposed-guard bounds

This records an author mathematical review of CHARGED_EXPOSED_GUARD_AUTHOR_REPORT_07.md. It is not a blinded review, mechanical proof, or proof of unrestricted three-mode equality. The original report and all intermediate-state counterexamples remain unchanged.

The reviewed game is exactly the five-state primitive graph in charged_guaranteed_02/explore.py: nonnegative separable action prices, positive whole work, a fixed finite DAG, permanently completed jobs, per-job guards, and a match branch always available to Nature. Nontermination has infinite objective. No cross-job memory or locking interference is added by this result.

The LC-to-D simplification is valid by a simulation, not by assuming that an optimal program immediately commits. From LC every eventually completing local path pays at least r. Physically committing early costs r and can only enlarge the ready set. A simulated original history supplies match outcomes for all later virtual validations of the now-completed job; those outcomes are legal and consume no budget. Operations on other jobs are unchanged. Thus the transformed policy has no larger cost on every matched adversarial path and remains terminating. The same argument applies to a ready L when p<=m: any guarded completion costs at least w+p+r, while a release-first path costs at least r+w+m. Nonnegative fees and p<=m make early guarded completion no more expensive. Multiple held or prepared jobs cause no extra coupling under the stated game.

After those simplifications, the potential sum_U c + sum_C m + sum_L(c-g) + F(T,b) handles U acquisitions, preparations, discards, releases and the three complete-job actions by direct cancellation. A negative coefficient c-g is harmless for telescoping over terminating paths. The only deficient edge is an acquire-validation failure at a job with p>m. Adding m to that failure edge makes its maximum with the success edge dominate the cheap-validation recurrence option. Each such correction consumes one budget unit. Therefore H-B*mu<=Vfull<=H, where mu=max({m_i:m_i>0 and p_i>m_i} union {0}). The special case mu=0 is exact for every B.

Clamping p to m on the exceptional jobs gives a cheaper game whose full value is exactly Hflat by the preceding special case. Monotonicity in action prices gives Hflat<=Vfull. A three-mode execution performs a protected kernel at most once per job, because such a kernel completes that job even on a cached-callback mismatch. Executing an optimal clamped-price macro policy at original prices therefore adds at most E=sum_exceptional(p-m). This justifies H-Hflat<=E and the joint lower bound max(Hflat,H-B*mu), without assuming equality for the unclamped full game.

For B=0 the exact arbitrary-state cost is Z=sum_U c+sum_C m+sum_L(r+w+min(p,m))+sum_LC r. Every job has this local lower bound and a topological completion order attains their sum. A held guard on a currently unready job can wait for its parents. At B=1 use the previous potential while budget remains and Z after a failure. Each already-held L gains delta+min(p,m)>=0 at the switch. For an acquire-validation failure, g+Z_i(L)=k+w+min(p,m); after subtracting the old cached credit, its maximum with the match branch is delta+max(F(T-i,1),w+min(p,m)). This dominates the cheap-validation option when p>=m and the cached-completion option when p<=m. All other edges retain their earlier lower bounds. Thus the arbitrary-DAG all-U root is exact for B<=1, including simultaneous C/L holdings.

The conditional eta bound in the report is also valid. The strict success-branch inequality is necessary for that argument; the two-job chain with prices (w,p)=(1,0),(10,10) and zero API fees refutes its unconditional form. None of the bounds above needs eta or a concavity claim.

This review found no missing primitive edge under the stated abstract interface. It does not supply the remaining proof for B>=2 with some m>0 and p>m. It also does not turn the observed 43-versus45 held-L counterexample into a false all-U equality claim. The separate atomic-callback theorem covers all nonnegative fees for its own interface, where no guard survives a call; that proof and this partial exposed-guard result have different scopes.
