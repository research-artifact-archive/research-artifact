# Specified-budget screened DP development

Generalize the already checked B1 bound-screened DP to B2 before any results for this implementation. Use the inherited static allocation lower bound on each (S,b). Compute two feasible fixed-order value/mode tables for ID-first and cheapest-available-first orders in O(|S|b) after their order construction. Close when lower and incumbent agree. Otherwise try all available protected choices and the cheapest available FAST choice, pruning only when valid child lower bounds cannot improve the incumbent. FAST evaluates both the smaller-set same-budget child and the same-set smaller-budget child. Memoization is over (S,b); no cyclic dependency occurs.

Each state keeps its attained incumbent policy: either a complete fixed-order bounded-budget mode table or a choice referencing solved children. Serialize only the reachable policy nodes, keeping total search states separately. An independent checker computes worst path cost from the emitted policy without importing the solver, its bounds or its claimed values. Zero-budget continuation is explicitly all FAST; the failed job remains in the failure child. The table/node checker is a feasible-policy check, not an independent proof that bound pruning was sound.

Freeze implementation/checker/driver hashes and run all4,336 existing B2 development inputs exactly once against their preserved CP and independent scalar values, with 3second units/180second campaign. These are known development regressions, not held-out. No retries/exclusions/replacements. Four malformed policy structures must be rejected. The lower bound, cheapest-fast dominance and fixed-order recurrence are inherited; only their implementation and resulting scope are being assessed.
