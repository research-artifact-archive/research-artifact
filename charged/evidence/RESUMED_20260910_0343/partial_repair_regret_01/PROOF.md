# Additive excess: objective dependence of the common baseline

Keep precisely the prior partial-repair interface and known-budget minimax comparator K_mu(B)=mu+G(floor(B/q)). Define additive excess E(P)=sup_B sup_{writes<=B}(cost(P)-K_mu(B)). For a fixed policy, mu cancels at every terminal observation. Consequently E(P) and its optimal value are mu-independent. This is an algebraic consequence of a common once-paid baseline; it would fail for a cost charged only to one completion mode or at every retry.

The minimum consistent expenditure lemma still applies: after prior rejected observations of total minimum cover count e, current D has worst consistent excess w(D)-G(floor((e+c(D))/q)). Exact c(D), complete re-preparation and repeatable fixed footprints remain required. The budget witness is selected for the policy's stopping prefix, not fixed to the full lower-path length.

For a target Delta>=0, call an observation unacceptable when this excess exceeds Delta. Any policy accepting there violates the target at that prefix's minimum consistent budget. If no q-step sequence is entirely unacceptable, accepting the first acceptable observation meets Delta within q calls. At fixed k the maximum H(k) dominates smaller weights, and at fixed k the excess is nonincreasing in e. The same induction as the compact competitive proof shows that repeatedly taking the smallest unacceptable k has no larger expenditure than any other all-unacceptable history. One path therefore decides feasibility.

Scale rational weights exactly to integers; mu is absent. The optimum is an integer terminal value in[0,W]. Zero is a lower bound from the zero-write execution, and W bounds every acceptance excess. At target-1, q empty observations form an unacceptable path. Binary search between-1 and W finds adjacent infeasible/feasible integers; the last infeasible q-step path has every value at least the optimum, and its minimum equals the optimum, since a larger minimum would also refute the feasible target. The feasible path certifies the matching upper bound. Construction uses O(qm log(W+1)) arithmetic operations and O(m+q) space after a correct profile; exact encoding costs still count. Both the profile and online cover queries retain their previous conditional scope.

Thus exact simultaneous budgetwise optimality, worst ratio, and worst additive excess are different objectives. No minimizer of one is automatically a minimizer of another. In particular, the need to reject positive dirty weight at small budgets with K_0(B)=0 is a finite-ratio requirement, not a prohibition under additive excess. The tests compare these objectives explicitly and do not calibrate either metric to Roslyn.
