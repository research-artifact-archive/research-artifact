# Joint total and protected work: corrected proof dossier

Author research derivation, September10,2026. This supersedes the claims' wording in ANALYTIC_DRAFT01 and NORMAL_FORM_DRAFT02 while preserving both originals. It incorporates explicit observation and initialization conditions found during author checks. Finite/native corroboration is separate from these arguments. Lawler's rule and Pareto backward induction are inherited methods, not newly invented algorithms. Let Top_b(S) sum the largest min(b,|S|) positive job works; Omega(S) is their total.

## Contract and scope

Use the whole-kernel completion interface of manuscript163 Section2. Jobs form a finite DAG and complete permanently with their exact immutable predecessor outputs. Each job's opaque kernel execution has prescribed positive work wi. A job requires its own completing counted call; no other operation can fabricate a result, complete a job, share a whole-kernel charge, or retain a guard. The policy is deterministic and causal. At the gate after its job/mode choice, the environment can install a fresh own-key identity not previously used for that job. Allowing other legal environments also to reuse identities does not remove these fresh-writing adversaries. Free inspections are honest. Preparation is disallowed before readiness, but records and old captured raw snapshots may be retained.

The common starting point is before either program generates computed records; all whole-kernel preparation and replaceable computed initialization is charged to W. This is not an arbitrary continuation-cost theorem with free precomputed records. Inputs and raw captured identities can be given; they do not supply a free computed result. W counts all kernel work and L its protected part, with neither counter nor elapsed time implicitly observable. We compare the separate worst values (sup_E W,sup_E L), not sup_E(W+lambda L).

For attaining adaptive policies B/C/D, a cached completion returns its completed output **and its local Boolean captured-identity comparison result**. Completion publishes that output over the live own-key entry. The flag is part of the visible history. Purity of a kernel alone does not make its recomputation observable; different invocations may return the same canonical object. A source wrapper can export the actual reference comparison from inside compute without another counted call. The nonbranching all-cached policy in A needs no flag.

Here r=0, so Q<=n forces exactly n completing calls. Informed policies are admissible against every unconditionally B-bounded environment. Universal policies satisfy the cap in every finite-write environment. sup is necessary for arbitrary programs, whose finite preparation counts can vary unboundedly with values. Finite extracted trees have maxima. A program can terminate on every play and nevertheless have infinite worst W; termination alone does not imply a finite uniform bound.

## A. Cost of requiring one optimal protected-work curve

Let V consist of universal policies satisfying L(P,E)<=Top_b(J) for every b>=0 and every E with at most b writes. For each B>=0,

    inf_(P in V) sup_(E in E_B) W(P,E) = Omega + Top_B.

The same policy attains this whole curve on every DAG: choose any ready job, prepare only for its immediately upcoming cached completion, and always use cached completion. Each job has one mandatory kernel, plus one duplicate iff that cached call mismatches. The preparation-to-comparison intervals for completed jobs are disjoint. Each mismatch interval requires at least one actual own-key write, so the mismatched jobs have cardinality at most B. Hence W<=Omega+Top_B and L<=Top_B, and Q=n even outside any supplied bound.

For the lower bound set k=min(B,n) and target k heaviest jobs. Write a fresh identity at each target comparison, with unconditional cap k, and nowhere else. An admissible program cannot incur a cheap failure. Every target completes fresh or by cached mismatch; a comparison cannot match before the cap. If j>=1 targets complete fresh, at most k-j target comparisons occur and hence at most k-j writes are used. But all k targets are protected, giving L>=Top_k>Top_(k-j), contradicting membership in V. Thus every target is a cached mismatch and has both a charged preparation and protected recomputation. Other jobs still require one kernel, so W>=Omega+Top_k. For k=0 use W>=Omega. Retention, extra preparations and free inspections do not affect the fresh-write or disjoint per-job charging arguments.

## B. Informed optimal-L corner for independent jobs

For independent jobs and a supplied B, minimize worst W subject to Q=n and L<=Top_B in all <=B-write environments. The value is

    Omega + Top_(B+1) - wmax,  if 0<=B<n;
    Omega,                   if B>=n.

For B<n, use nondecreasing weight order. Let d be the number of observed cached mismatches and m the remaining job count. If d=B, prepare/cached the suffix. Otherwise, if m<=B-d, finish that suffix fresh. Otherwise prepare/cached the next smallest job. For B=0 this simply prepares/caches every job; for B>=n use all-fresh. The scheduler uses only its supplied B, job weights/order, completion state, and comparison flags.

Every protected job is either one of d mismatches or one of at most B-d final fresh jobs. Thus at most B distinct jobs contribute to L, yielding L<=Top_B. The largest job can only complete fresh or after B mismatches have already certified exhaustion. Therefore at most B distinct nonmaximum jobs duplicate, yielding W<=Omega+Top_(B+1)-wmax. After d=B, cached calls replace cheap calls without changing within-budget costs. This also preserves Q=n outside the supplied B; those executions need not satisfy the claimed W/L caps.

For the lower bound, target B+1 heaviest jobs and write at their comparison gates until B writes have been made, with no other writes. If all targets complete protectively then L>=Top_(B+1)>Top_B. At least one must complete unprotected. Such a comparison can match only after all B writes are spent. The B earlier writes each caused a cached completion of a distinct target, since Q=n forbids failed cheap calls. Their duplicate weight is at least the B smallest weights among the B+1 targets, namely Top_(B+1)-wmax. Global mandatory charging adds Omega. This lower bound also holds on DAGs, but the ascending upper policy may violate precedence; the exact formula is restricted to independence. B=0 and B>=n use the mandatory Omega bound.

The gap between A's class and B's optimum is wmax-w_(B+1) for B<n, with weights sorted nonincreasingly; for B>=n it is Omega. The classes impose different conditions: A requires one policy's L to be optimal simultaneously at every b, while B requires optimal L only at the supplied B. This is a price of that simultaneous contract, not an unrestricted pointwise ignorance penalty. B's constrained value can decrease at B=n because its allowed L increases to Omega.

## C. Complete one-write frontier on a DAG

Fix B=1 and a total-work allowance W<=Omega+M, M>=0. Define heavy jobs by wi>M. Give a heavy job processing time pi=wi and tail taui=0; give a light job pi=0,taui=wi. For any topological order pi_order, let Ci be cumulative processing time through job i. Then the exact least worst protected work is

    ell(M) = min_(topological orders) max_i (Ci+taui).

Upper bound: follow that order, using fresh for heavy jobs and immediate prepare/cached for light jobs until the first mismatch. Thereafter prepare/cache all remaining jobs. There is at most one mismatch, so W<=Omega+M. The no-mismatch path protects all heavy work. A mismatch at a light job i protects the earlier heavy prefix plus wi and no later work. These possibilities give exactly max_i(Ci+taui), including the heavy total; gate writes realize the relevant mismatch paths.

Lower bound independent of D: follow the arbitrary program's no-write path. Its completing operations must be fresh or cached because a cheap call has a one-write failing continuation. If cached i occurs, an own-gate fresh write forces a second charged kernel for i, hence worst W>=Omega+wi. Thus every heavy job is fresh. A zero-write cached call already stale can instead be classified as fresh: it protects wi already; counting its wasted preparation only strengthens W. The no-write path forces L at least its total fresh work, and a fresh-gate-write at each matching cached job i forces L at least its prior fresh work plus wi. The resulting lower expression consists of those cached terms and total fresh work. Convert each optional fresh light job into cached in this expression: later fresh prefixes decrease, and the new own term is at most the original total fresh work. The expression cannot increase. The final profile is precisely (p,tau). Therefore the program's worst L is at least the best such topological schedule. Extra preparations and value-dependent later paths cannot weaken these prefix bounds.

The inherited Lawler backward rule places a sink with minimum tail last, repeatedly. To justify the specialization, take an optimal remaining order with total processing T and old last job k, and move the selected sink j to the end. Intervening completion times weakly decrease. The new last value T+tauj is at most T+tauk since k was also an eligible sink and tauj<=tauk. It is therefore no larger than the previous objective. Repeat on the remaining graph. Zero processing times are permitted. A straightforward implementation takes O(n^2+|E|) per threshold; sweeping at most n+1 distinct thresholds gives polynomial construction (an elementary O(n^3+n|E|) bound suffices). More efficient implementations are inherited scheduling engineering.

Only M=0 and distinct works change the heavy/light partition. Increasing M changes a heavy job to light; its own Ci+taui is unchanged and later processing prefixes decrease. Thus ell(M) is nonincreasing. Evaluate the finite threshold set and discard dominated pairs (Omega+M,ell(M)); a threshold can have slack W if no cached job weighs exactly M, which is removed by the preceding threshold or Pareto filtering.

At the optimal L cap wmax, the minimum duplicate allowance is wsecond (zero for n=1) exactly when the maximum is unique and its job is a sink; otherwise it is wmax. If M<wsecond, at least two heavy jobs have total exceeding wmax. If the unique maximum has a successor, that successor's term after the heavy maximum exceeds wmax whenever M<wmax. Tied maxima force the same failure. Conversely, place a unique maximum sink last at M=wsecond, making the objective wmax. M=wmax always allows all-cached with L=wmax. Thus chain8->16 admits(W,L)=(32,16), whereas chain16->8 requires(40,16), and A's simultaneous class requires(40,16) on both.

## D. Full-program reduction and exact finite recurrence

For any supplied B and admissible deterministic program P, there is a finite fresh/cached tree T such that every <=B-write execution of T has an execution of P under some <=B-write environment with at least its W and L. This compares resources, not equal traces or output values under the same writer schedule. Thus the pair of separate maxima of T is componentwise no larger than the pair of suprema of P.

Restrict the adversary to zero writes except an optional one fresh own-key write at a selected completing comparison, at most B times. Fix one legal fresh identity at each concrete finite branch history. No-write continuation between counted calls must terminate: otherwise P is inadmissible against the environment that makes no further writes. If remaining allowance b>0, a selected cheap call has a fresh-write child causing noncompletion, which is incompatible with Q=n. Fresh completion gives a unary node. Cached completion with a matching zero-write outcome gives binary zero-/one-write continuations, both completing the selected job. A cached call already stale in its zero-write continuation is collapsed to unary fresh, retaining that original zero-write continuation. This permits old raw captures without assuming every retained record is valid. Once b=0, replace the remaining continuation by a ready prepare/cached suffix. Completing depth is at most n, so this concrete-history extraction is finite despite arbitrary uncounted local computation and infinitely many possible input values in the full model.

For a complete extracted path with binary mismatches D, the corresponding original execution has W>=Omega+sum_(i in D)wi: every job needs one distinct charged kernel, and each such mismatch needs both the selected record's preparation and its completing protected execution. Charges are disjoint across jobs, even when P prepared later jobs early. Its protected work is at least the sum of the extracted fresh and mismatched weights. A replaced b=0 suffix can only lower L. Do not claim a standalone mandatory-Omega lower bound for the original residual suffix: its records may have been prepaid. This is a global charging argument from the common start.

Realize T by preparing only when its selected cached node is reached, immediately before its comparison. Fresh nodes use current own input and actual exact completed parents. The schedule ignores values and follows the fixed node choices using the explicit comparison flag. They remain enabled because the completed set is fixed by that path. Each actual mismatch needs a distinct write in its disjoint preparation-to-comparison interval, so at most B occur. Unrelated writes, writes before capture or in fresh nodes, and multiple writes per interval can be invisible; no converse injection is required. At B observed mismatches no write remains. Every actual run therefore follows a represented path and has exactly the path charges above. Functional correctness follows from the operation semantics, not from reproducing P's concrete values.

Let F(S,b) be the Pareto-minimal pairs (e,l), where e is worst W minus Omega(S) and l is worst L, for such suffix trees on unfinished set S with remaining conservative mismatch allowance b. Then

    F(empty,b)=F(S,0)={(0,0)}.

For b>0 and ready i, let R=S\{i}. Include fresh candidates (e,wi+l) for (e,l) in F(R,b). Independently choose matching successor (e0,l0) in F(R,b) and mismatching successor (e1,l1) in F(R,b-1), and include

    (max(e0,wi+e1), max(l0,wi+l1)).

Take the nondominated union over all choices. Subtracting Omega(S) is legitimate because T has one mandatory execution per job on every branch; cached mismatch contributes one extra wi. Every resulting tree can realize either branch through own-gate writes. Conversely, its root choices and two successor trees give exactly this recurrence. Future available jobs depend only on S, and future charges on wi/mode/comparison; actual values do not affect these costs because kernels have fixed works and T prepares at its selected node. Earlier work is a branch-specific additive prefix, which the root combination keeps. A dominated successor can be removed by monotonicity of both maxima. Thus the two numbers are sufficient for separate resource caps, even if their maximizing paths differ. They would be insufficient for arbitrary correlated scalar objectives.

There are at most(min(B,n)+1)2^n subset/budget states; b>n can be clipped. Frontier coordinates are finite sums of weights, so enumeration terminates, but states and frontier size can be exponential. The reduction proves exactness against arbitrary programs; finite check01 only corroborates the recurrence on its fixed small inputs and is not that proof. No extension to r>0 follows: noncompleting calls can retain stale state at the same unfinished key. Previous r>0 counterexamples remain unchanged.

## Empirical corroboration, distinct from proof

FINITE_PROTOCOL01 generated26,844 known-budget frontier cases from5,421 DAG/weight inputs,18,390 one-write threshold cases checked against all topological orders and independent path evaluation,2,004 independent-job corner cases,237 small universal-tree cases,363 saturated-budget cases, and five specified fixtures. All retained results agreed. ORDER_PROTOCOL02 compared all26,844 frontiers with every fixed topological order and found no advantage from adaptive ordering; ascending-order comparisons also agreed on all2,004 independent cases. These are finite facts, not a general fixed-order theorem.

Native PROTOCOL01 then fixed2,076 correct-policy executions and52 flag-ignoring controls across six fixtures, two key layouts, two pure kernels and every specified one-write phase/target schedule. All2,076 correct policies satisfied functional/event/call/work checks. Four of52 controls had the predicted L=24>16, while48 did not violate their caps. Returned outputs equaled prepared outputs in70 actual mismatch calls across the full matrix, including correct policies; the explicit Boolean remains sufficient.864 executions ended with a saved milestone different from the later live map entry; predecessor checks used the saved outputs. These are kernel-work units and source-wrapper executions, not native-time savings or a deployed SLA.
