# Canary with explicit finite health reports

For n=2..6 and m=1..n-1, every old replica is healthy. A replica transfer has two environment-selected outcomes: healthy-awaiting-report and broken-awaiting-report. Each has one uncontrollable, outcome-specific report to the corresponding stable healthy or broken state. A controllable restart restores stable broken to stable healthy. Healthy replicas permit controllable service self-loops. This is a constructed model inspired by staged deployment practice, not a reproduction of a particular incident.

The interval tester counts reported broken replicas: a broken report adds that replica; restart removes it; other events leave the count unchanged. Error is absorbing once fewer than m replicas remain reported healthy. This is an observation-based requirement, not a direct physical-state predicate at the instant of transfer. Every outstanding report consumes an awaiting-report state, so report sequences are finite. With E1's uncontrollable priority, restoration or another controllable transfer cannot bypass pending reports.

Expected before execution: separate transfers permit one replica to be updated and, if necessary, restarted before the next update; n-1>=m makes this strategy viable. All-at-once transfer has an all-broken outcome. Its uncontrollable broken reports force the interval tester into error before restoration. All initial and final endpoint states have all replicas healthy. Complete WIN certificates will additionally be checked on their physical states, counting broken-awaiting-report as physically broken. LOSS diagnostics must exhibit the all-broken transfer outcome and a forced report path to error.

The schedule includes separate Lazy, separate with E1 transfer merging under Lazy, explicitly grouped all-at-once Lazy, and separate Direct-Full for each of the 15 parameter pairs. Grouping keeps the n local components and gives their transfers one shared event label, whose relation is the Cartesian product. The adapter declares `generated_contract_mode=transfers` through the fixed E1 implementation's existing internal grouped-contract builder; ordinary transitions, Post and goal rules are unchanged. The two all-at-once representations are compared by exhaustive reachable Post equality, decision and discovered state count. No expected value is a replacement for measurement.

Primary practice anchors: CrowdStrike's 2024-07-24 preliminary post-incident review (updated 2024-07-25), section Rapid Response Content Deployment, proposes staged deployment beginning with canaries and deployment feedback; the 2024-08-06 technical root-cause analysis, page 6, section 6, describes successive deployment rings with telemetry and rollback on problems. These sources motivate staging and observation. They do not establish this model's threshold, perfect reports, guaranteed restart, or independent transfer outcomes.
